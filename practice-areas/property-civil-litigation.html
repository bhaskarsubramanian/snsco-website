What This Practice Covers

Property and civil litigation deals with disputes over ownership, possession, and enjoyment of immovable property that are litigated before ordinary civil courts under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, rather than before a project-specific regulator such as RERA. It is distinct from, though it often sits alongside, our real estate and RERA practice, which is concerned specifically with builder-buyer and project-registration matters. This practice instead addresses questions such as: who actually owns a parcel of land, how jointly held property should be divided among co-owners, whether a party in long, uninterrupted possession has acquired title by adverse possession, and whether a party is entitled to an injunction restraining interference with their possession or use of property.

These disputes frequently arise from inherited or ancestral property, long-pending family arrangements, boundary and encroachment disagreements between neighbouring owners, and disputes over agricultural or undeveloped land where documentation of title has historically been informal. Because such suits are governed by general civil procedure and the Limitation Act, 1963, questions of limitation, valuation of the suit, and the correct relief to claim (declaration, possession, partition, or injunction, alone or in combination) are frequently as important to the outcome as the underlying facts of ownership.

Our Property & Civil Litigation Services

  • Title suits — suits for declaration of title where ownership of a property is disputed by a rival claimant.
  • Partition suits — dividing jointly or ancestrally held property among co-owners, including contested partitions where shares or the property itself are in dispute.
  • Suits for injunction — temporary and permanent injunctions restraining dispossession, encroachment, or interference with lawful possession, under Order XXXIX of the CPC.
  • Suits for possession — recovering possession of property from a trespasser or a party whose right to occupy has ended.
  • Adverse possession claims and defences — pursuing or defending claims to title based on long, open and hostile possession under Article 65 of the Limitation Act.
  • Boundary and encroachment disputes — between neighbouring owners over demarcation, easements and rights of way.
  • Suits for specific performance — enforcing or defending agreements to sell or transfer immovable property.

How a Civil Suit Over Property Typically Proceeds

  1. Title & Documentation Review

    We review title documents, revenue records, prior litigation, and the factual history of possession to assess the strength of a claim or defence before drafting.

  2. Drafting & Filing of the Suit

    We draft the plaint (or written statement, where defending), identifying the correct relief (declaration, partition, possession, or injunction) and the appropriate civil court based on the property's location and the suit's valuation.

  3. Interim Relief

    Where urgent, we apply for a temporary injunction under Order XXXIX to preserve the status quo pending trial, particularly where dispossession or construction is imminent.

  4. Trial & Evidence

    We conduct the trial, including examination of witnesses and documentary evidence establishing title, possession, or the factual basis for partition or injunction.

  5. Execution & Appeal

    Where necessary, we pursue execution of a decree for possession or partition, or appeal an adverse judgment before the appropriate appellate forum.

Illustrative Scenarios

The following are hypothetical, illustrative scenarios prepared for educational purposes. They are not descriptions of actual client matters, and no outcome is guaranteed in any specific case.

Illustrative Scenario

Siblings Disputing Division of Ancestral Property in Noida

Three siblings jointly inherit a family property, but disagree on how it should be physically divided and whether one sibling's earlier occupation of a larger portion should be treated as an advance against their eventual share. A partition suit, supported by clear valuation of each portion and an account of possession to date, provides a structured route to a binding division where informal family discussion has stalled.

Illustrative Scenario

Neighbouring Owners in a Boundary Dispute in Salt Lake, Kolkata

Two adjoining property owners disagree over the precise boundary line after a fence is moved during construction on one plot. A suit for injunction, supported by a fresh survey and the original title documents, can restrain further construction on the disputed strip pending a final determination of the correct boundary.

Illustrative Scenario

Long-Term Possessor Facing a Sudden Ownership Claim in Greater Noida

A family that has occupied and maintained a plot of land for over two decades, paying local taxes and undertaking improvements, faces a sudden suit from a party asserting paper title. Where possession has genuinely been open, continuous and hostile for the statutory period, a defence, or counter-claim, based on adverse possession under Article 65 of the Limitation Act may be available, though each element must be independently proved.

Illustrative Scenario

Purchaser Facing a Title Dispute After Buying From One Co-Owner

A buyer purchases a share in jointly held property from one co-owner, only to find other co-owners dispute the extent of what was validly transferred. A title suit clarifying the buyer's actual entitlement, as successor to the selling co-owner's undivided share, followed by partition, is often the necessary next step to convert a disputed purchase into a clearly defined holding.

Notable Judgments

Supreme Court2026
Proof Required to Establish Adverse Possession

Supreme Court Reiterates That Each Element of Adverse Possession Must Be Independently Pleaded and Proved

The Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasised, as reported in legal commentary through 2026, that a party claiming title by adverse possession must specifically plead and prove the date on which possession became adverse, and establish that such possession was open, continuous, and hostile to the true owner's knowledge throughout the statutory period; a vague or general assertion of long possession is not sufficient to discharge this burden.

Key Takeaway — A party relying on adverse possession, whether as claimant or in defence, should maintain contemporaneous records (tax receipts, utility bills, improvement records) that can later establish each specific element of the claim.
High Court Practice2026
Interim Injunctions in Property Possession Disputes

Courts Continue to Apply the Three-Fold Test Before Granting an Interim Injunction in Property Disputes

As reported across recent High Court commentary, courts continue to apply the settled three-fold test (prima facie case, balance of convenience, and irreparable injury) before granting a temporary injunction restraining interference with possession of immovable property, and remain reluctant to disturb settled possession through an ex-parte order except in clear and urgent cases.

Key Takeaway — An application for interim injunction in a property dispute should be supported by clear, specific evidence on all three limbs of the test; general assertions of ownership or harm are unlikely to persuade a court to grant urgent relief.
Legislative & Policy2026
Digitisation of Land Records

Continued Digitisation of Land Records Is Reshaping How Title Disputes Are Evidenced

State-level digitisation of land records and integration with registration systems continues to expand across Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal, giving litigants faster access to historical mutation and encumbrance records, a development that is gradually changing how title suits are evidenced, though it does not substitute for the independent title verification that a suit still requires.

Key Takeaway — Parties to a title or partition dispute should obtain certified digital land records early, but should not treat digitised records alone as conclusive proof of title without independent verification of the underlying chain of documents.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a title suit and a suit for partition?

A title suit seeks a declaration of ownership where that ownership is disputed. A partition suit assumes co-ownership is not disputed and instead seeks division of jointly held property into separate shares.

Can a co-owner sell jointly held property without the consent of other co-owners?

A co-owner can generally transfer only their own undivided share, not the entire property or a specific portion, without the consent of the other co-owners; a purchaser typically takes subject to the rights of the remaining co-owners pending partition.

How long does adverse possession take to establish in India?

Under Article 65 of the Limitation Act, 1963, a claim generally requires open, continuous and hostile possession, to the exclusion of the true owner, for twelve years, with the burden of proving each element resting strictly on the claimant.

What is the difference between a permanent injunction and a temporary injunction in a property dispute?

A temporary injunction under Order XXXIX CPC is interim relief granted during the pendency of a suit to preserve the status quo. A permanent injunction is final relief granted only after trial.
