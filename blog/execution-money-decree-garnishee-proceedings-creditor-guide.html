Execution of a Money Decree and Garnishee Proceedings: A Creditor's Guide

A guide for decree-holders on how to actually recover a money decree in India: the modes of execution available under Order XXI CPC, and how garnishee proceedings work against third parties holding a judgment-debtor's assets.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 14 August 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice. See our disclaimer

Why Execution Is a Separate Battle

Winning a money decree is a milestone, not the finish line: a decree only establishes the legal right to payment. Actually recovering the money requires execution proceedings under Order XXI of the Code of Civil Procedure, and a judgment-debtor who is unwilling or unable to pay voluntarily can make this a genuinely separate, sometimes prolonged, contest.

Modes of Execution Available

Order XXI offers a decree-holder several modes of execution, which can often be pursued together: attachment and sale of the judgment-debtor's movable property; attachment and sale of immovable property; appointment of a receiver over specific assets; garnishee proceedings against third parties who owe money to the judgment-debtor; and, in appropriate cases involving a debtor who has the means to pay but wilfully refuses, arrest and civil detention in execution.

How Garnishee Proceedings Work

Garnishee proceedings under Order XXI Rule 46 allow a decree-holder to reach money owed to the judgment-debtor by a third party: commonly a bank holding the debtor's account, or a business counterparty who owes the debtor payment under a separate contract. The court issues a garnishee order directing the third party to pay the attached amount to the decree-holder instead of the judgment-debtor, effectively redirecting a debt the judgment-debtor was owed.

The debt attached must be a certain, ascertainable one (a genuinely contingent or speculative obligation cannot be garnisheed) but courts have recognised that even a debt not yet due for payment can be attached where its existence and amount are otherwise certain, with payment to the decree-holder simply deferred until the debt would have matured.

Practical Steps for Decree-Holders

An effective execution strategy typically starts with identifying the judgment-debtor's known assets and any third parties who may owe the debtor money: including banks, business partners, and any pending receivables. Filing simultaneous execution applications across multiple modes, rather than pursuing one route to exhaustion before trying another, often improves the practical chances of actual recovery, particularly against a debtor actively trying to frustrate execution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a decree-holder attach a debtor's bank account directly?

Yes. A bank holding the judgment-debtor's account can itself be proceeded against through garnishee proceedings, being directed to pay the attached balance to the decree-holder.

Is a debt that has not yet fallen due still attachable through garnishee proceedings?

Yes, provided the debt is certain in amount and destined to fall due: a genuinely contingent or uncertain future obligation cannot be attached.

What happens if the judgment-debtor has no traceable assets?

Execution becomes difficult in practice: a decree-holder may need to investigate further to locate assets, including through examination of the judgment-debtor under Order XXI Rule 41.

Can a decree-holder pursue multiple modes of execution at the same time?

Yes. Attachment of movable property, immovable property and garnishee proceedings can generally be pursued simultaneously rather than sequentially, improving the practical prospects of recovery.
