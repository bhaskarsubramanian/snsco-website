Succession Disputes Among Family Members: Common Grounds for Challenging Inheritance

A guide to how disputes over inheritance and succession commonly arise among family members in India, and the typical legal grounds on which such claims are challenged or defended.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 29 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

Overview

A guide to how disputes over inheritance and succession commonly arise among family members in India, and the typical legal grounds on which such claims are challenged or defended.

What You Need to Know

  • Succession disputes among family members typically arise either where a person dies intestate (without a valid will), triggering the applicable personal law of succession, or where a will exists but is challenged by family members who feel excluded or under-provided for
  • For Hindus, intestate succession is governed by the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, which lays down a specific order of Class I and Class II heirs; for persons governed by other personal laws or the Indian Succession Act, 1925, different rules of intestate distribution apply
  • A will can be challenged on grounds including lack of testamentary capacity at the time of execution, undue influence or coercion by a beneficiary, fraud, and failure to comply with the formal requirements of execution and attestation under the Indian Succession Act, 1925
  • Courts require strong, specific evidence to unsettle apparent testamentary intention. A will is not invalidated merely because it favours one family member over others, or appears unequal, so long as it was validly executed by a person of sound mind acting freely
  • Family settlements: a negotiated division of assets among family members, often used to resolve or pre-empt succession disputes. Are generally upheld by courts where entered into voluntarily and with full disclosure, and can be a faster, less adversarial alternative to prolonged probate or succession litigation

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a will be challenged simply because it treats children unequally?

No. Unequal distribution alone is not a ground to invalidate a will; a challenger must show a specific defect such as lack of capacity, undue influence, fraud, or non-compliance with execution formalities.

What happens if a family member dies without leaving a will?

The estate is distributed according to the applicable law of intestate succession. For Hindus, this is the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, which sets out a specific order of Class I and Class II heirs.

Are family settlements a good alternative to succession litigation?

Often yes: a voluntary, fully disclosed family settlement can resolve or avoid prolonged succession litigation and is generally upheld by courts, though it should be properly documented.
