Closing or Selling a Food Business: Surrendering or Transferring Your FSSAI Licence

How to surrender an FSSAI licence when a food business closes, when a licence can be transferred instead, and what happens if neither step is taken.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 5 November 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

As the law stands today, a food business that permanently shuts down is expected to surrender its FSSAI licence or registration rather than simply stop operating and let it run out, while a business that changes ownership through a sale or the death of the licence holder has a narrower set of situations in which the existing licence or registration can actually be moved to the new person, rather than requiring a fresh application from scratch.

Operators tend to assume that an unused licence quietly lapses on its own once the business stops trading, or that a buyer can simply carry on using the seller's licence after a sale closes. Neither assumption holds up, and both can leave someone exposed, the seller to continuing responsibility for a licence still in their name, and the buyer to operating a food business with no valid licence of its own at all.

Surrender is the route out, not a lapse

Under the Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Regulations, 2011, a licence holder who is permanently closing operations, whether a restaurant shutting down, a manufacturing unit stopping production, or any other food business ceasing entirely, applies to surrender the licence or registration through the FSSAI's online portal rather than simply allowing it to expire unrenewed. The application is made to the same licensing authority that granted the licence, Central or State depending on the category, and takes effect only once that authority accepts it; filing the application alone does not end the licence holder's obligations under it.

This distinction matters because a licence that is neither renewed nor surrendered does not disappear from the regulator's records. The holder can still be treated as a person obliged to comply with the Act for as long as the licence remains open on paper, including facing enforcement action if a stale or unused licence is later found associated with food activity that has in fact continued, or is wrongly assumed by an inspecting officer to have continued. Operators closing a business should treat the surrender application as part of the closure itself, not as optional housekeeping to be done later if anyone asks.

Surrender is different from cancellation

Surrender, initiated voluntarily by the licence holder on closing the business, should not be confused with cancellation, which is an enforcement step the licensing authority takes against a licence holder for a violation, typically following a show cause notice and an opportunity to respond. A business owner who is simply shutting down for commercial reasons, with no pending compliance issue, follows the surrender route; a business facing cancellation is in an entirely different position, since the authority is acting against it rather than the holder voluntarily walking away.

Practically, the two outcomes can look similar from the outside, in that the licence stops being valid, but the record behind the closure is very different. A voluntarily surrendered licence that was in good standing when surrender was sought leaves a cleaner history for the people behind the business than a cancelled one does, which matters if any of them go on to apply for a fresh FSSAI licence for a different venture later.

Can a licence be transferred when a business is sold?

Guidance on this point is not entirely uniform even among FSSAI's own public material, and an operator planning a sale should check the current position with the relevant licensing authority rather than assume either answer. What is consistently described is that a licence is tied to the specific licence holder as a legal entity or individual, so a change in the underlying business structure, for instance converting a sole proprietorship into a partnership, an LLP, or a company, is generally treated as requiring a fresh licence in the name of the new entity rather than a simple transfer, since the legal person behind the licence has itself changed.

Where the licence holder dies while the licence is still in force, FSSAI's process does recognise a transfer to the legal heir or representative, applied for online with supporting documents including a death certificate and proof of the applicant's status as legal representative, generally within a defined window after the death. Outside that specific situation, a sale of the business to an unrelated buyer is more often handled by the seller surrendering the existing licence and the buyer applying afresh in its own name, though some guidance describes a modification or transfer-of-ownership request as available in certain cases; given this inconsistency, the safer course for a transaction involving a change of ownership is to confirm directly with the licensing authority, before the deal closes, which of these routes it will actually accept.

Changing premises is its own, separate process

A food business that moves to new premises, as opposed to closing or changing hands, faces a related but distinct problem: FSSAI's practice has been that a licence cannot simply be carried over to a new address under the same number. The operator typically has to surrender the existing licence tied to the old premises and apply for a new one for the new location, a requirement that has caused friction for businesses that wanted continuity of their licence number for commercial or compliance-tracking reasons. FSSAI has since allowed certain limited exceptions, including migration between State and Central licensing without requiring a fresh number in some circumstances, but a straightforward relocation to a new address should still be planned as a new application rather than assumed to be a simple address update on the existing licence.

Businesses operating from multiple premises under a single licence, or adding a new outlet, face their own separate set of rules about when a new licence is needed for the new location and when the existing one can be extended to cover it; this is a different question from surrender or transfer and should not be conflated with either.

What goes wrong if this is ignored

Operating without a valid licence, whether because the business never applied for one, let an existing one expire without renewing, or continued trading after a sale under a licence that technically still belongs to the seller, is treated under the FSS Act in the same bracket as operating without a licence altogether, carrying penalties that can run into lakhs of rupees and, in serious cases, prosecution. The seller of a business who fails to surrender a licence that is no longer in use remains formally associated with it in the regulator's records, a loose end that tends to surface at the least convenient moment, often during due diligence on an unrelated transaction or when the buyer's own licence application is being processed and the authority notices the earlier licence was never closed.

The practical sequence that avoids most of this is straightforward: on closure, apply to surrender before trading actually stops, not after; on a sale, settle with the licensing authority in advance whether a fresh licence or some form of transfer is acceptable, and build that timeline into the transaction rather than leaving it to be sorted out after completion; and on relocation, assume a fresh application will be needed unless the specific migration exception is confirmed to apply.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an FSSAI licence lapse automatically if the business stops operating?

No. The licence remains open on the regulator's records until the holder formally applies to surrender it and the licensing authority accepts the application. Simply stopping trading without surrendering leaves the licence holder associated with an open licence.

Can a buyer keep using the seller's FSSAI licence after purchasing a food business?

Generally no, especially where the legal entity running the business has changed, such as a sole proprietorship becoming a partnership or company. The usual course is for the seller to surrender the existing licence and the buyer to apply for a new one, though the specific position should be confirmed with the licensing authority before the sale closes.

What happens to the FSSAI licence if the licence holder dies?

FSSAI's process allows a transfer to the legal heir or representative on application, supported by a death certificate and proof of legal representative status, generally within a defined window after death. This is a distinct, narrower route than an ordinary business sale.

Is surrendering a licence the same as having it cancelled?

No. Surrender is a voluntary step taken by the licence holder on closing a business. Cancellation is an enforcement action the licensing authority takes against a holder for a violation, usually after a show cause notice, and leaves a different compliance history behind.

Can an existing FSSAI licence simply be updated with a new business address after relocation?

FSSAI's general practice has been to require surrender of the licence tied to the old premises and a fresh application for the new location, rather than a simple address update, with only limited exceptions for certain State-to-Central migrations.
