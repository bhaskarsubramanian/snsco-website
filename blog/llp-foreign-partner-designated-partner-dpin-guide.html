Setting Up an LLP With a Foreign Partner: The Resident Designated Partner Rule and the FDI Conditions

A foreign national can be a designated partner in an Indian LLP, but a resident designated partner is still mandatory. What that means for FDI and DPIN.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 20 November 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

A foreign national or a foreign company can be a partner, and even a designated partner, in an Indian limited liability partnership. What cannot be avoided is Section 7 of the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008, which requires every LLP to have at least two designated partners who are individuals, and at least one of them resident in India. A structure with only foreign designated partners, however senior or however much capital they bring, does not satisfy this requirement and cannot be registered as it stands.

This guide sets out the resident designated partner rule, the separate question of whether foreign investment into the LLP is even permitted for the chosen business activity, and the Designated Partner Identification Number process a foreign individual has to go through either way.

The resident designated partner requirement

Section 7(1) of the LLP Act requires at least two individual designated partners, with at least one resident in India. The Act's own definition of resident in this context turns on the number of days an individual has stayed in India during the relevant financial year. Since the LLP (Amendment) Rules, 2022, the threshold is at least 120 days in the financial year, replacing the earlier test of 182 days in the immediately preceding year; an LLP should still confirm the current text before relying on it for a specific filing.

A foreign national can absolutely be a designated partner, and the Act does not bar a non-citizen from holding that role. What it requires is that at least one of the two designated partners, not necessarily a majority if there are more than two, independently satisfies the residency test. An LLP with two foreign promoters and no India-based individual anywhere in its structure typically has to bring in a resident nominee or a resident individual partner specifically to fill this role, which is a planning point that needs to be settled before incorporation documents are filed rather than discovered afterward.

A body corporate partner still needs an individual nominee

Where one of the partners is itself a body corporate, such as a foreign holding company investing through the LLP structure, that body corporate cannot act as a designated partner in its own name. It can only be represented through a nominee who is a natural person, and that nominee individual is the one who must hold a valid Designated Partner Identification Number and meet whatever residency or other qualification the designated partner role requires.

This is where groups sometimes assume that naming the foreign holding company as a partner is enough and separately overlook that someone still has to be nominated, identified, and registered as the actual designated partner standing behind that corporate partner. The nomination itself needs to be formally recorded and filed with the Registrar, not handled as an internal, undocumented understanding within the group.

FDI eligibility is a separate question from the designated partner rule

Satisfying Section 7 does not by itself mean foreign investment into the LLP is permitted. Under the FDI policy, foreign investment into an LLP is allowed only in sectors where 100% FDI is permitted under the automatic route and which are not subject to FDI-linked performance conditions, such as minimum capitalisation requirements that apply in some sectors. An LLP operating in a sector that allows only partial automatic-route FDI, or that carries performance conditions, generally cannot take foreign investment in LLP form at all, regardless of how the designated partner structure is arranged.

The LLP structure also cannot be used to raise the kind of capital an investor might expect from a company. Preference shares and other capital instruments do not exist in an LLP, and debt investment, whether convertible or non-convertible, into an LLP from a foreign investor is not a route the FDI policy accommodates in the way it accommodates external commercial borrowing into a company. A foreign investor who wants convertible instruments or a structured debt-equity mix as part of the deal usually needs to use a company rather than an LLP for that part of the investment.

The Designated Partner Identification Number for a foreign individual

Every designated partner, resident or foreign, must obtain a Designated Partner Identification Number before being appointed, applied for through the incorporation form or the relevant DPIN application process, and a digital signature certificate is needed to complete the filing. A foreign national applying from outside India typically has to submit notarised and apostilled copies of identity and address proof, since the usual Aadhaar-based verification route available to Indian residents is not available to them.

This documentation step is often the slowest part of bringing a foreign designated partner onto an Indian LLP, because apostille processing abroad can take weeks depending on the country, and a foreign national's passport and overseas address proof both typically need separate notarisation and apostille before the DPIN application can be filed. Building this lead time into the incorporation timeline avoids the common mistake of assuming DPIN approval will move at the same speed for a foreign applicant as it does for a resident one.

Practical sequencing for a foreign-backed LLP

Before committing to the LLP structure, confirm that the intended business activity sits within a sector where 100% FDI is allowed under the automatic route with no performance conditions, since this is the threshold question that determines whether the LLP route is even available, independent of who the designated partners are. Only after clearing that question does it make sense to work out who will serve as the resident designated partner, whether that is an individual partner who happens to be India-based, a trusted nominee, or an Indian professional engaged specifically for the role.

Start the DPIN and apostille process for any foreign designated partner well before the target incorporation date, given the documentation lead time involved, and keep the nomination of an individual behind any corporate partner properly documented and filed rather than left as an informal arrangement. An LLP that gets the sector eligibility and the resident designated partner requirement settled early avoids the more disruptive alternative, which is a registration that stalls at the Registrar's desk, or a foreign investment that arrives before anyone has checked whether the LLP route was ever open to that sector.

What happens when the LLP later adds or replaces a foreign partner

The resident designated partner requirement is not a one-time box checked at incorporation. If the individual who satisfied the residency test later moves abroad, resigns, or is removed as a partner, the LLP has to replace that role with another qualifying resident individual and file the change with the Registrar, and an LLP that lets this lapse, even briefly, is technically out of compliance with Section 7 until it is fixed. Groups that rely on a single Indian employee or professional to hold the resident designated partner role should plan for what happens if that person leaves the company or the country, rather than treating the role as a fixed fact of the structure.

Adding a new foreign partner later in the LLP's life raises the same DPIN and apostille questions as the original incorporation, and the lead time involved does not shrink just because the LLP already exists. A group bringing in a new foreign co-investor as a partner midway through the LLP's life should start the DPIN and documentation process as early as it would for a brand new incorporation, rather than assuming an existing, already-registered LLP can absorb a new foreign partner on a shorter timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an LLP have only foreign designated partners?

No. Section 7 of the LLP Act requires at least two individual designated partners with at least one resident in India. An LLP cannot be registered, or cannot remain compliant, with only foreign designated partners and no India-resident individual filling that role.

Can a foreign company be a designated partner of an Indian LLP?

A body corporate, including a foreign company, can be a partner but not a designated partner in its own name. It must nominate an individual to act as designated partner on its behalf, and that nominee individual needs a valid Designated Partner Identification Number.

Is foreign investment allowed into any LLP, regardless of its business?

No. Foreign investment into an LLP is permitted only where the business activity falls in a sector with 100% FDI allowed under the automatic route and no FDI-linked performance conditions. Outside that, the LLP structure is generally not available for foreign investment in that sector.

What documents does a foreign individual need for a DPIN application?

Typically notarised and apostilled copies of a passport and overseas address proof, since the Aadhaar-based verification available to Indian residents does not apply. This step often takes longer than other parts of incorporation because of apostille processing time abroad.
