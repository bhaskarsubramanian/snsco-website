Water Rights and Irrigation Disputes for Agricultural Land: What Farmers Need to Know

A guide to how irrigation water entitlements for agricultural land are allocated and enforced in India, covering canal-command water rights, groundwater extraction rules, and the forums available for resolving irrigation disputes.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 29 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice. See our disclaimer

Overview

A guide to how irrigation water entitlements for agricultural land are allocated and enforced in India, covering canal-command water rights, groundwater extraction rules, and the forums available for resolving irrigation disputes.

What You Need to Know

  • A farmer's entitlement to canal irrigation water is generally governed not by ownership of the land alone but by the specific irrigation department's notified water-release schedule and rotational allocation (often called a 'warabandi' or similarly named rotation system) for the command area in which the land falls, administered under the applicable state irrigation act
  • Registered water users within a canal command area (particularly those at the 'tail end' of a distribution channel, furthest from the main source) are especially vulnerable to unauthorised upstream diversion, and several state irrigation acts and tribunals have recognised an enforceable entitlement to a proportionate share of notified releases, even where enforcing it in practice requires ongoing vigilance
  • Groundwater extraction for agricultural use is regulated separately, generally through state groundwater regulation legislation (often modelled on a central model bill) that can require registration of agricultural bore wells in notified over-exploited or critical groundwater zones, and can impose spacing norms between wells to prevent excessive localised depletion
  • Water User Associations, where constituted under Participatory Irrigation Management legislation in a given state, are given a formal role in distributing water within a designated command area and can themselves be a first forum for resolving disputes between farmers over their respective shares, before escalating to the irrigation department or a state irrigation tribunal
  • Where multiple states share a common river basin, inter-state water allocation disputes are governed by a different framework entirely (generally resolved through an inter-state water disputes tribunal constituted under central legislation) and are legally distinct from a farmer's intra-state dispute over canal or groundwater allocation within a single state's irrigation system
  • A farmer facing recurring water shortage should keep a contemporaneous record (dates, observed flow or absence of flow, and any complaints raised with the Water User Association or irrigation department) since tribunals and courts examining an irrigation dispute have generally placed significant weight on a documented pattern of deprivation rather than a general, undocumented complaint

Frequently Asked Questions

Does owning agricultural land in a canal command area automatically guarantee water supply?

Not automatically: actual entitlement is generally governed by the irrigation department's notified rotational release schedule for that command area, and enforcing a fair share, particularly at the tail end of a distribution channel, often requires active vigilance and documentation.

Is groundwater extraction for farming unregulated?

Not entirely: many states regulate agricultural bore well extraction, particularly in notified over-exploited or critical zones, through state groundwater regulation legislation requiring registration and, in some cases, spacing norms between wells.

Where should a farmer raise a dispute over unfair canal-water distribution?

Typically first with the Water User Association (where one is constituted for the command area) or the irrigation department, escalating to a state irrigation tribunal if the dispute is not resolved.
