Guardianship vs. Custody of a Child in India: Understanding the Legal Distinction

A plain-language guide to the difference between legal guardianship and physical custody of a minor child under Indian family law, and why the distinction matters in practice.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 29 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice — see our disclaimer

Overview

A plain-language guide to the difference between legal guardianship and physical custody of a minor child under Indian family law, and why the distinction matters in practice.

What You Need to Know

  • Guardianship, governed primarily by the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 and, for Hindus, the Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956, refers to the overall legal responsibility for a minor's person and property, including major decisions about education, health and finances
  • Custody refers specifically to who the child physically lives with day-to-day; a parent can have custody without being the sole legal guardian, and a person can be a guardian without having physical custody
  • Under the Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, the father is traditionally treated as the natural guardian, but courts have increasingly recognised the mother's equal claim, and in practice courts award custody based squarely on the child's welfare rather than a fixed rule
  • In custody disputes, courts distinguish between legal custody (decision-making authority) and physical custody (day-to-day residence), and can order joint legal custody with physical custody vesting in one parent, subject to visitation for the other
  • A guardian appointed by the court has fiduciary obligations regarding the child's property, including restrictions on selling or mortgaging the minor's immovable property without prior court permission under Section 29 of the Guardians and Wards Act

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a custodial parent automatically the child's sole legal guardian?

Not necessarily. Courts can and often do recognise both parents as guardians for certain purposes while awarding physical custody to only one parent, with visitation rights for the other.

Can a guardian sell property belonging to the minor child?

Only with the prior permission of the court, which will examine whether the sale is genuinely necessary and in the minor's interest before granting permission.

Does custody automatically end when a child turns a certain age?

Custody and guardianship arrangements typically continue until the child attains majority at 18, though courts can modify arrangements earlier if circumstances materially change.
