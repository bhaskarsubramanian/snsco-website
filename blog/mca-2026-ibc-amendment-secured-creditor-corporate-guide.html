Incorporation Paperwork and Secured Creditor Status in 2026: Two Compliance Changes Companies Should Not Miss

A practical look at the MCA's Companies (Incorporation) Amendment Rules, 2026 and the IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026's narrowing of who counts as a secured creditor, and what both mean for founders, company secretaries and insolvency stakeholders.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 20 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice. See our disclaimer.

Two Unrelated Rule Changes, Same Compliance Season

Two separate developments in company and insolvency law arrived within the same compliance year and are worth tracking together, even though they address entirely different stages of a company's life: incorporation on one hand, and insolvency resolution on the other. The first is the Ministry of Corporate Affairs' Companies (Incorporation) Amendment Rules, 2026, which simplify several steps in setting up a company. The second is the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026, which narrows who can claim secured creditor status in a corporate insolvency resolution process.

Incorporation Gets Simpler

The Companies (Incorporation) Amendment Rules, 2026, reportedly notified on 8 April 2026, make several changes that reduce paperwork at and shortly after incorporation. The cap on directors who can be allotted a Director Identification Number at the time of incorporation itself is reported to have risen from three to five, which should help companies that want to appoint a larger founding board without a separate post-incorporation DIN application round. Form DIR-12, previously used to notify changes among directors, is reported to have been omitted, with subscribers to the Memorandum of Association reportedly receiving deemed consent to act as directors, removing a standalone consent filing in the ordinary case. Nine existing company e-forms are also reported to have been consolidated into two broader forms, intended to cut down the number of separate filings required for routine post-incorporation changes.

A further reported change concerns registered office verification: the Registrar's physical inspection of a company's registered office, previously mandatory in every case, is reported to shift to a risk-based, discretionary model, meaning inspection will be triggered selectively rather than applied uniformly. Companies should still keep their registered office documentation current and verifiable, since a risk-based model does not mean verification will never happen, only that it is no longer automatic for every company.

Secured Creditor Status Gets Narrower in Insolvency

On the insolvency side, the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026, reported to have taken effect from 26 May 2026, is understood to bar a claimant from asserting secured creditor status in a corporate insolvency resolution process where the claim rests purely on a statutory charge created by operation of law, rather than on a security interest created by contract with the corporate debtor. The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal is reported to have applied this in the Shubhkamna Buildtech matter, rejecting claims by industrial development authorities to secured creditor status based on a statutory charge over a developer's leasehold land, a ruling reportedly significant for homebuyers in the underlying project since it avoided passing an additional liability, estimated at roughly Rs. 116 crore, onto the insolvency estate ahead of their claims.

For businesses and statutory bodies with outstanding dues from a company that later enters insolvency, this change matters because secured creditors are typically paid ahead of unsecured creditors in a resolution or liquidation waterfall. A creditor whose claim to security rests only on a statutory charge, rather than a specifically executed mortgage, charge document or security agreement, should expect that claim to be treated as unsecured going forward.

Practical Takeaways

Founders and company secretaries handling incorporation should update their standard process to reflect the new DIN cap, the omission of Form DIR-12, and the consolidated e-forms, and should still keep registered office records audit-ready despite the shift to discretionary verification. Any statutory authority or creditor relying on a charge created by legislation, rather than a signed security document, should review its position in any pending or anticipated insolvency proceeding in light of the 2026 amendment, since a claim previously treated as secured may now be reclassified as unsecured.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How many directors can now be allotted a DIN at the time of incorporation?

Under the Companies (Incorporation) Amendment Rules, 2026, reportedly notified on 8 April 2026, the cap on the number of proposed directors who can be allotted a Director Identification Number at incorporation has reportedly been raised from three to five.

Is Form DIR-12 still required for director changes?

Form DIR-12 is reported to have been omitted under the 2026 amendment, with the information it previously captured reportedly folded into other filings, and Memorandum of Association subscribers reportedly receiving deemed consent to act as directors without a separate consent filing.

Does a statutory charge still make a creditor a secured creditor in a company's insolvency?

Following the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026, reportedly effective from 26 May 2026, a claimant whose security rests solely on a statutory charge created by operation of law, rather than a contractual security interest, is reported to no longer qualify for secured creditor status in a corporate insolvency resolution process.

Is physical verification of a company's registered office still required?

The 2026 amendment is reported to shift registered office verification from a mandatory physical inspection in every case to a risk-based, discretionary model, meaning inspection is triggered selectively rather than for every company, though companies should still keep their registered office documentation accurate and verifiable.
