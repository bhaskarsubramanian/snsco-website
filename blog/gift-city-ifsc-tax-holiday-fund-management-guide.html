GIFT City IFSC: Tax Holiday for Funds and Family Offices

What a fund manager or family office actually gets by setting up in GIFT City's IFSC: the tax holiday, who qualifies, and what the 2026 changes extended.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 16 November 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

GIFT City, India's International Financial Services Centre near Gandhinagar, is regularly pitched to foreign fund managers and Indian family offices as a way to run an offshore-style fund or treasury book from inside India rather than from Mauritius, Singapore or the Cayman Islands. The pitch rests mainly on one feature: a long tax holiday for units registered with the International Financial Services Centres Authority on income earned in convertible foreign exchange, together with relief from GST on many IFSC-to-IFSC and IFSC-to-offshore services and an easier path for an offshore fund to relocate its management into India without triggering the adverse tax consequences that would normally follow.

None of this makes GIFT City a blanket tax shelter, and the benefit is tied closely to a unit's registration category, its income being genuinely earned in foreign exchange, and conditions that have to be satisfied year after year rather than claimed once. This guide sets out what an IFSC unit actually gets under the current law, who it is realistically for, and what changed when the Finance Act, 2026 extended the headline holiday.

What an IFSC is and who can set up there

The International Financial Services Centres Authority Act, 2019 created a single regulator, the IFSCA, for financial products, financial services and financial institutions operating out of an IFSC, replacing the earlier arrangement under which SEBI, RBI, IRDAI and other domestic regulators each separately supervised activity there. In practice, a fund management entity, a banking unit of a bank, an insurance office, a broker-dealer, or an aircraft or ship leasing entity can register with the IFSCA and operate largely under a lighter, more globally benchmarked rulebook than its mainland Indian counterpart, provided its business is structured to deal predominantly with persons and transactions outside India or with other IFSC entities.

For a foreign fund manager, the common structure is a fund management entity registered under the IFSCA's fund management regulations, managing one or more funds that are themselves set up in the IFSC, with management fees and other income received in convertible foreign exchange through an IFSC banking unit. For an Indian family office or HNI, the more common route is either investing into a GIFT City-domiciled fund as an investor, or setting up a smaller fund management entity to manage the family's own pooled capital and that of a limited set of other investors, subject to the applicable regulatory thresholds.

The income tax holiday and its 2026 extension

Under the Income Tax Act, 1961, Section 80LA gave a unit operating in an IFSC a deduction equal to one hundred per cent of its eligible business income for ten consecutive years out of a fifteen-year block, at the unit's option, provided the income was derived from specified IFSC business and, for most categories, received in convertible foreign exchange. With the Income-tax Act, 2025 coming into force from the tax year beginning 1 April 2026, the equivalent deduction is re-enacted under Section 147 of the new Act, and it is this section, not Section 80LA, that governs the deduction for tax years from 2026-27 onward; a unit already claiming the deduction under Section 80LA for an earlier year continues its claim under the renumbered provision rather than starting the ten or twenty-year clock afresh.

The Finance Act, 2026 extended the headline holiday itself: for an eligible offshore banking unit the holiday runs for twenty consecutive years rather than ten, and for other eligible IFSC units it runs for twenty years within a widened twenty-five year block rather than ten years within fifteen, with the change applying from the tax year beginning 1 April 2026. Reports on the Budget also describe a fifteen per cent rate proposed for business income of an IFSC unit once its holiday period has run out, though the final enacted rate for the post-holiday period should be checked against the Finance Act's text rather than assumed from Budget commentary, since a Finance Bill proposal does not always survive unchanged into the enacted Act.

Why a fund manager cannot assume the deduction applies automatically

The Section 80LA and successor Section 147 deductions are not a blanket exemption for anyone with an IFSC address. They are conditional on the income actually falling within the categories the provision specifies, generally fund management and other specified financial services income connected to the IFSC business, and on that income being received in, or brought into India in, convertible foreign exchange within the time the law allows; management fees invoiced or collected in rupees, or through a banking channel outside the IFSC banking unit structure, put the deduction at risk. The unit also has to file the prescribed audit report, commonly referenced as Form 10CCF, certifying the deduction claimed, and recent reports of processing friction around this form are a reminder that the deduction, while generous on paper, is administered like any other tax deduction and not treated as self-executing.

A further point advisers flag often enough to be worth stating plainly: setting up in GIFT City does not by itself exempt a fund manager's other, non-IFSC income, and it does not retroactively change the tax treatment of a business that was already being run, in substance, from outside the IFSC before the unit was registered. A family office relocating an existing informally run investment desk into a GIFT City entity should expect scrutiny of whether the IFSC entity is where the actual investment decisions are now made, not simply where the entity is licensed.

GST, stamp duty and the broader cost picture

Services supplied by one IFSC unit to another IFSC unit, or by an IFSC unit to a recipient located outside India, are broadly treated as exports or as transactions attracting relief from GST under notifications specific to IFSC units, which matters for a fund management entity whose principal cost lines are advisory and administration services bought from other financial institutions inside the same zone. GIFT City also offers relief from stamp duty on specified instruments executed by units within the IFSC under Gujarat's state notifications, though the scope of this relief is specific to the instrument and the notification in force and should be checked against the current Gujarat stamp duty notification for the transaction in question rather than assumed to cover every document a fund executes.

None of these reliefs replaces ordinary regulatory compliance. An IFSC-registered fund management entity remains subject to the IFSCA's own fund management regulations on matters such as minimum corpus, investor eligibility and reporting, and a fund manager moving an existing offshore fund's management into GIFT City needs the move itself, not just the destination, to be structured correctly, including under Section 9A of the Income Tax Act's safe harbour for offshore funds managed by an eligible fund manager in India, which is designed to stop the fund itself from being treated as having a taxable presence in India merely because its manager now sits in an IFSC.

Who this realistically suits, and who it does not

GIFT City is best suited to a fund manager who already has, or is building, a genuine India-facing or India-origin investor base and management team, and who wants the regulatory and tax architecture of an offshore centre without physically basing the business outside India. It also suits an Indian promoter family running a family office that wants to pool and manage its own offshore-eligible capital through a licensed vehicle rather than informally, and it suits banking units of Indian and foreign banks booking foreign-currency lending and treasury business that would otherwise sit in a Singapore or Dubai branch.

It suits less well a manager whose investor base and operations are substantially outside India already and who has no particular reason to touch India at all; for that manager, an established offshore centre with a longer regulatory track record may remain the simpler choice, and the IFSC's tax architecture only pays off once the compliance cost of registration, the Form 10CCF audit, and ongoing IFSCA reporting is weighed against the specific deduction actually available on the entity's real income mix. A promoter weighing the move should also treat the decision as a multi-year commitment rather than a one-off filing choice, since the holiday is measured across a long block of years and depends on the unit continuing to meet the foreign-exchange and income-category conditions every year it claims the deduction, not merely in the year of registration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every business set up in GIFT City get a tax holiday?

No. The deduction under Section 147 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, successor to Section 80LA, applies only to specified categories of IFSC business income, and only where that income is received in convertible foreign exchange and the unit files the required audit report. A unit earning income outside those categories, or receiving it through the wrong channel, does not get the deduction on that income.

How long does the IFSC tax holiday now run?

Following the Finance Act, 2026, an offshore banking unit gets twenty consecutive years, and other eligible IFSC units get twenty years within a twenty-five-year window, both from the tax year beginning 1 April 2026, up from the earlier ten-year windows. A unit already claiming the deduction under the old Section 80LA continues under the renumbered provision rather than restarting the clock.

Can a foreign fund simply relocate its manager to GIFT City without any other change?

Relocating only the licence while real decision-making stays elsewhere defeats the purpose and invites scrutiny. The benefit depends on the fund management entity in the IFSC actually making the relevant decisions and earning genuine specified income in convertible foreign exchange, not merely holding a registration.

Is GST charged on services between two IFSC units?

Services between IFSC units, and from an IFSC unit to a person outside India, are broadly treated favourably under notifications specific to the IFSC, which commonly means no GST is charged on such supplies. The exact treatment depends on the specific service and notification and should be checked against the current notification for the transaction.

Does setting up in GIFT City protect an offshore fund from being treated as having an Indian permanent establishment?

This is addressed separately through the safe harbour under Section 9A of the Income Tax Act for an eligible offshore fund managed by an eligible fund manager in India, which is designed so that having an India-based manager does not by itself create a taxable presence for the fund, subject to the fund and manager meeting the conditions the provision sets.
