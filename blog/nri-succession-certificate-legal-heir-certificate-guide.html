NRI Inheriting Property in India: Succession Certificate, Legal Heir Certificate and What Each Actually Does

How an NRI heir proves inheritance in India, and the real difference between a succession certificate and a legal heir certificate.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 16 October 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

An NRI who learns of a parent's death and an estate back home usually discovers two unfamiliar documents standing between them and the assets: a succession certificate and a legal heir certificate. Banks ask for one, the land records office asks for the other, and the two get confused constantly because both are, in a loose sense, about proving who the heirs are. They are issued by different authorities, cover different kinds of property, and serve different legal purposes, and getting the wrong one first can cost months.

This guide sets out what each document actually does, when a will changes the picture, and the specific complications that arise when the person applying is living abroad.

Succession certificate: for movable assets, from a civil court

A succession certificate is granted by a civil court under Part X of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, and it exists to let the holder collect debts and securities due to the deceased, bank deposits, fixed deposits, shares, mutual fund units and similar movable, financial assets, and to give valid discharge to whoever pays under it. It does not cover immovable property at all, and a bank or a registrar of companies relies on it precisely because it protects them from being sued twice over the same payment.

The application is filed in the district court within whose jurisdiction the deceased ordinarily resided, or where the relevant property is situated if there is no fixed residence. The court issues a public notice inviting objections, generally running for a fixed period, and the certificate is granted only once that period passes without a sustained objection. Where the estate is uncontested, the practical timeline runs several months once the petition is properly filed and the notice period has run; where another relative objects, it becomes a full contested proceeding and the timeline extends considerably.

Legal heir certificate: for revenue and mutation purposes

A legal heir certificate is issued by the local revenue authority, typically the Tahsildar or the equivalent Mandal Revenue Officer depending on the state, and it is the document ordinarily used to support mutation of immovable property in land records and municipal registers, and for a range of other administrative purposes such as compassionate appointment claims or pension transfer. It records who the department understands the heirs to be, based on local inquiry, but it is not itself a judicial determination with the same evidentiary weight as a succession certificate or a probate decree.

Because it rests on administrative inquiry rather than a court process with formal notice to all interested parties, a legal heir certificate is more vulnerable to challenge later if an omitted heir surfaces or a dispute arises. For a family with one undisputed heir and a straightforward residential property, it is usually sufficient and considerably faster to obtain than a succession certificate. For a larger estate, multiple properties, or any hint of family disagreement, relying on it alone is a real risk.

Where a will changes the analysis

If the deceased left a will, the relevant document for a legatee is probate or letters of administration with the will annexed, not a succession certificate or legal heir certificate, though in practice a bank may still accept a succession certificate obtained with the will referenced as supporting the application, and some families end up with more than one document on hand simply because different institutions demanded different proof. Following the 2025 repeal of Section 213 of the Indian Succession Act, probate is no longer a mandatory precondition to enforcing rights under a will in court, but it remains the strongest document for dealing with banks, registries and third parties who continue to insist on it as a matter of their own internal policy.

Where some assets are specifically bequeathed by will and others pass by intestate succession because the will does not cover the whole estate, a family can end up needing probate for the bequeathed assets and a succession certificate or legal heir certificate for the remainder, which is a common source of delay when the will's drafting did not anticipate this split clearly.

What changes when the applicant is an NRI

An NRI heir can appoint a power of attorney holder in India to file and pursue the application, which is the standard route given that attending every hearing in person from abroad is impractical. The power of attorney executed outside India needs to be properly authenticated for use in India, through apostille where the country of execution is a party to the Hague Apostille Convention, or through consularisation at the Indian embassy or consulate where it is not, and a document that skips this step is routinely rejected at the registration stage in India regardless of how clearly it is drafted.

NRIs should also keep their PAN and Indian tax filings current if the inherited assets generate income, since banks increasingly ask for PAN details before releasing large sums even where the succession certificate itself is in order, and an NRI who has never filed an Indian return can find this an unexpected extra step layered onto what should otherwise be a straightforward disbursement.

Repatriating the proceeds

Once inherited assets are sold or liquidated, an NRI typically wants to move the proceeds abroad, and this is governed by FEMA rather than by the succession certificate or legal heir certificate process itself. The usual route allows repatriation up to a specified annual limit per financial year from the balance held in an NRO account, supported by proof of inheritance, the relevant tax clearance certificate, and the authorised dealer bank's own compliance checks, and the current limit and the exact certification required should be confirmed with the remitting bank and the applicable RBI directions at the time of the actual remittance, since this is an area where the specific figures are updated periodically.

Agricultural land is treated differently for this purpose regardless of how it was acquired. Inherited agricultural land, plantation property or a farmhouse cannot be transferred by an NRI or OCI to another NRI or OCI; it can only be transferred to a person resident in India, which affects how a family structures the sale even after all the succession paperwork is otherwise complete.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need both a succession certificate and a legal heir certificate?

Often yes, if the estate includes both financial assets and immovable property. The succession certificate covers debts, deposits and securities before a bank or company; the legal heir certificate, or a sale deed route following mutation, is what land and municipal records typically require. Many families end up applying for both rather than assuming one covers everything.

Can I apply for a succession certificate without travelling to India?

Yes, through a power of attorney holder in India, provided the power of attorney was validly executed and properly apostilled or consularised for use in India. The PoA holder can then file the petition, respond to the notice period, and collect the certificate on the NRI's behalf.

Is a legal heir certificate enough to sell inherited property?

It is generally used to support mutation of the property into the heirs' names, which is usually a precondition before the heirs can convey clear title by sale. Whether it is sufficient on its own, or whether a succession certificate, probate, or a court declaration is also needed, depends on the state's registration practice and whether any co-heir or third party disputes the inheritance.

Can I repatriate the full sale proceeds of an inherited house abroad immediately?

Repatriation of sale proceeds from an NRO account is subject to an annual limit under FEMA and requires proof of inheritance and tax clearance; it is not automatic or unlimited. Confirm the current limit and the specific documentation your authorised dealer bank will require before assuming the full amount can move out in one transfer.
