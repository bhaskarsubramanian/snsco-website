Land Pooling Schemes for Agricultural Land: How They Work and What Landowners Should Check

A guide to land pooling schemes used for urban and infrastructure development on agricultural land, explaining how pooling differs from acquisition, the typical return-of-developed-plot structure, and key points landowners should verify before consenting.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 29 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice. See our disclaimer

Overview

A guide to land pooling schemes used for urban and infrastructure development on agricultural land, explaining how pooling differs from acquisition, the typical return-of-developed-plot structure, and key points landowners should verify before consenting.

What You Need to Know

  • A land pooling scheme is a mechanism, increasingly used by development authorities as an alternative to compulsory land acquisition, under which landowners voluntarily pool their agricultural land into a scheme area in exchange for a smaller developed plot (with infrastructure such as roads, drainage and utilities already provided) once the scheme area is developed
  • Unlike compulsory acquisition, land pooling is generally structured as a voluntary participation mechanism: landowners typically execute a specific pooling agreement or consent document, and the scheme's terms (including the ratio of developed plot area returned against pooled agricultural land area) are usually notified in advance by the development authority
  • The 'return ratio' (the proportion of developed land handed back to the original landowner relative to the raw agricultural land pooled) is the single most consequential term of any pooling scheme, and varies materially between schemes and development authorities, so landowners should verify this ratio, and how it is calculated for their specific parcel, before consenting
  • Because a pooled parcel typically loses its original identity once merged into the larger scheme area for planning and infrastructure purposes, landowners should also verify the location-allocation mechanism used to decide which specific developed plot a given landowner ultimately receives, since not every scheme guarantees a plot at or near the original location
  • Landowners should check whether the scheme requires interim possession of the pooled land to be handed over to the development authority before the developed plot is actually allotted, and if so, for how long, since this can create a substantial gap during which the landowner holds neither the original agricultural land nor the promised developed plot
  • Because land pooling schemes are typically notified under state town-planning or development-authority legislation rather than a uniform central statute, the specific consent requirements, return ratio, and remedies available if the authority fails to deliver the developed plot on schedule must be checked against the specific scheme notification applicable to the land in question

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a land pooling scheme the same as compulsory land acquisition?

No. Pooling is generally structured as a voluntary participation mechanism under which the landowner consents to pool land in exchange for a developed plot, rather than compulsory acquisition against compensation.

What is the most important term to check before consenting to a pooling scheme?

The return ratio (the proportion of developed plot area to be returned relative to the agricultural land pooled) since this determines the actual value the landowner ultimately receives.

Does a landowner get a developed plot at the same location as the original agricultural land?

Not necessarily: many schemes use a location-allocation mechanism for the scheme area as a whole, so a landowner should check whether the specific scheme guarantees an at-or-near-original-location allotment.
