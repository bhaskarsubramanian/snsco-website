GST Anti-Profiteering After the Sunset: What Businesses With Pending Cases Need to Know

A guide for a business currently facing, or previously subject to, a GST anti-profiteering investigation, explaining that the Central Government has stopped accepting new anti-profiteering complaints from 1 April 2025, what happens to complaints and investigations already underway, and which forum now decides them.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 28 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice — see our disclaimer

Overview

A guide for a business currently facing, or previously subject to, a GST anti-profiteering investigation, explaining that the Central Government has stopped accepting new anti-profiteering complaints from 1 April 2025, what happens to complaints and investigations already underway, and which forum now decides them.

What You Need to Know

  • The Central Government notified 1 April 2025 as the sunset date for the anti-profiteering provisions under GST — no new anti-profiteering complaints or investigations can be initiated after that date
  • Section 171 of the CGST Act itself remains on the statute book unchanged, continuing to require that any reduction in GST rate or benefit of additional input tax credit be passed on to the recipient by way of a commensurate reduction in price — the sunset affects new enforcement, not the underlying substantive obligation
  • The anti-profiteering enforcement function has passed through three institutional homes over time: the erstwhile National Anti-Profiteering Authority (NAA), then the Competition Commission of India (which took over jurisdiction from 1 October 2024), and now the Principal Bench of the GST Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT)
  • Complaints and investigations that were already underway — meaning a DGAP investigation report or a hearing notice had already issued — before the 1 April 2025 sunset continue to be adjudicated to conclusion by the GSTAT Principal Bench, regardless of the sunset date
  • A business currently defending a pending anti-profiteering matter should confirm the current procedural stage of its case and the GSTAT Principal Bench's applicable timelines, since the Bench has continued issuing final orders on legacy cases through 2026
  • Businesses should not assume the sunset means Section 171's substantive price-pass-through obligation has disappeared — pricing decisions around a GST rate reduction should still account for the statutory pass-through requirement, even though the enforcement mechanism for new complaints has closed

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a business still face a new anti-profiteering complaint today?

No — the Central Government stopped accepting new anti-profiteering complaints and investigations from 1 April 2025, though Section 171's underlying price-pass-through obligation remains on the statute book.

Who decides anti-profiteering complaints that were already pending before the sunset?

Complaints and investigations already underway before 1 April 2025 continue to be adjudicated to conclusion by the Principal Bench of the GST Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), which took over after the erstwhile NAA and a stint with the CCI.

Does the sunset mean businesses no longer need to pass on a GST rate cut?

No — Section 171 itself is unchanged, so a business should still account for the statutory pass-through requirement when pricing a GST rate reduction, even though the enforcement mechanism for new complaints has closed.
