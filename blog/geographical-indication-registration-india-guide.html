Registering a Geographical Indication (GI) in India: Process, Eligibility and Who Can Apply

A practical guide for a producer association or authority seeking to protect a regional product under the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 — eligibility, filing process, term and renewal.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 24 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice — see our disclaimer

Who Can Apply — and Who Cannot

Unlike a trademark, a Geographical Indication cannot generally be registered by an individual trader acting alone. Only an association of persons, producers, or an organisation or authority representing the interests of producers of the goods can apply, by filing the appropriate form — GI-1A through GI-1D, depending on the nature of the applicant — with the Geographical Indications Registry in Chennai. This restriction reflects the collective character of a GI: it protects a product tied to a region and its community of producers, not a single trader's individual brand.

What the Application Must Show

The application must be filed in triplicate, together with a statement of case explaining how the goods' quality, reputation or other characteristics are essentially attributable to their geographical origin. This is supported by maps of the defined geographical area, details of the production process, and a list of the producers the application covers. The strength of this evidentiary link between the product and its place of origin is central to whether the application succeeds.

The government fee is charged per class — reported at the time of writing as Rs. 5,000 for individuals and associations and Rs. 10,000 for government or other applicants, though the current GI Rules, 2002 fee schedule should be checked at the time of filing since fee amounts are periodically revised.

The Registration Process and Timeline

Once filed, the application goes through examination, followed by a two-month window during which interested parties can raise objections after the application is advertised in the GI Journal. If the application is unopposed, or once any opposition has been resolved in the applicant's favour, the mark is entered in the GI Register. The full process typically takes anywhere from several months to a couple of years, depending on the volume of examiner queries and whether any opposition is filed.

Term, Renewal and What Registration Actually Gives You

A registered GI is valid for ten years from the date of registration and can be renewed indefinitely for further ten-year periods on payment of the applicable renewal fee — unlike a design registration, a GI does not have a fixed outer limit on protection so long as renewals are kept current. Registration gives the right to exclude others from using the GI on goods that do not originate in the defined region, and allows producers within that region to separately register as 'authorised users' entitled to use the GI logo or tag on their own goods. Unauthorised use of a registered GI by someone outside the region, or by someone within it who has not registered as an authorised user, can attract infringement action.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an individual trader apply to register a Geographical Indication?

Generally no. Only an association of persons, producers, or an organisation or authority representing producers' interests can apply for GI registration — not an individual trader acting alone.

Where is a GI application filed in India?

With the Geographical Indications Registry in Chennai, using the appropriate Form GI-1A through GI-1D depending on the applicant's category, filed in triplicate with a statement of case and supporting evidence.

How long does GI registration last, and can it be renewed?

A registered GI is valid for ten years from registration and can be renewed indefinitely for further ten-year periods on payment of the renewal fee, unlike a design registration which has a fixed outer limit.

What does GI registration actually let the producers do?

It excludes others from using the GI on goods not originating in the defined region, and lets producers within the region separately register as authorised users entitled to use the GI logo or tag on their products.
