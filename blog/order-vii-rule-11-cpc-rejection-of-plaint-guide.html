When Can a Court Reject a Plaint Under Order VII Rule 11 CPC?

A defendant's fastest route to end a hopeless suit is often Order VII Rule 11. Here is the narrow, plaint-only test courts apply, and when it will not work.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 7 October 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice, see our disclaimer

The Six Grounds and the Plaint-Only Test

Order VII Rule 11 of the Code of Civil Procedure lists specific grounds for rejecting a plaint at the threshold: no disclosed cause of action, undervaluation of the relief claimed, insufficient stamping, the suit being barred by law, failure to file the plaint in duplicate, and non-compliance with Order VII Rule 9 on the filing of documents.

Courts decide such an application by looking only at the averments made in the plaint itself, and the documents filed along with it. The defendant's written statement, defence or any external evidence cannot be relied on to reject a plaint, and no evidence or merits of the underlying controversy can be examined at this stage: the court must assume the plaint's factual averments are true, for the limited purpose of deciding whether a cause of action, or a legal bar, is disclosed on the face of the plaint.

When Limitation or a Legal Bar Is 'Obvious From the Plaint'

Where limitation or another legal bar is obvious and glaring from the plaint's own averments, courts have held that they should reject the plaint at the threshold rather than let a hopeless suit proceed all the way to trial.

If a plaint claims several reliefs and at least one of them is properly pleaded and maintainable, the entire plaint cannot be rejected merely because the other reliefs claimed may not themselves be maintainable.

Practical Strategy for Plaintiffs and Defendants

For a defendant, a well-prepared Order VII Rule 11 application can end a suit at an early and inexpensive stage, without the cost and delay of a full trial.

For a plaintiff, the best defence to such an application is drafting: ensuring the plaint itself pleads enough facts to disclose a cause of action, and to show that the claim is not ex facie time-barred on its own averments.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a court look at the defendant's written statement when deciding an Order VII Rule 11 application?

No. The application is decided solely on the averments in the plaint itself and the documents filed with it; the defence and external evidence are not considered at this stage.

If a plaint pleads multiple reliefs, can it be partly rejected?

No. Where at least one of the reliefs claimed in the plaint is properly pleaded and maintainable, the plaint as a whole survives and cannot be rejected merely because other reliefs may fail.

Can limitation alone be a ground to reject a plaint at the threshold?

Yes, where the bar of limitation is obvious and glaring from the plaint's own averments, courts have rejected the plaint under Order VII Rule 11 without proceeding to trial.
