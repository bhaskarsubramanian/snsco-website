Registering a Property Sale in Kolkata and West Bengal: Process and Stamp Duty

How a property sale is registered in Kolkata and West Bengal, including e-registration, valuation, who presents the deed, and why stamp duty rates vary.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 12 November 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

A sale of immovable property in West Bengal, like anywhere else in India, is not legally complete merely because both sides have signed a deed and money has changed hands. Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908 makes registration of the sale deed compulsory for most property transactions above a nominal value, and an unregistered deed, with narrow exceptions, cannot be used to prove the transfer of title in a court, cannot be received as evidence of the transaction it records, and leaves the buyer without the legal protection registration is meant to give against a later competing claim.

This guide sets out how that registration actually works in West Bengal today, from valuation through to the documents the registering officer will want to see, what stamp duty and registration fee a buyer can expect to pay, and the mistakes that most often hold up or undo a registration. Our general guide on Property Registration and Stamp Duty in India and the Uttar Pradesh-specific guide on Buying Property in Noida and Uttar Pradesh cover the same ground for other states, and the process described there differs from West Bengal's in several respects, including the valuation mechanism and the online filing portal used.

Why registration is compulsory, and what happens without it

Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908 requires registration of any non-testamentary instrument that purports to create, assign, limit or extinguish a right in immovable property worth more than one hundred rupees, which in practice covers almost every sale deed, gift deed and most leases of a meaningful term. Section 49 of the same Act then provides the consequence of skipping it: an instrument required to be registered but not registered does not affect the immovable property it deals with, and generally cannot be used as evidence of the transaction, subject to a limited exception allowing it to be used for narrower collateral purposes in a suit.

The practical effect for a buyer is straightforward. An unregistered sale deed does not pass title, however complete the sale may feel to the parties, and a buyer relying on one is exposed to the seller later disputing the transaction, to a competing buyer who registers first, and to banks and later purchasers who will not accept the property as cleanly owned. Registration is what makes the transaction count against the world, not merely between the two signing parties.

Who presents the deed, and the time limit

Under Section 32 of the Registration Act, the document can be presented for registration by a party to it, their authorised agent, or a representative or assign where the party has since died, and Section 34 requires the registering officer to satisfy itself as to the identity of the person presenting the document and, where relevant, the execution of the document by the persons who appear to have executed it, before proceeding to register it. Section 23 fixes the ordinary time limit at four months from the date of execution, after which the document can no longer be registered on the usual basis, though a further limited extension is available on payment of a fine under Section 25 where the delay is explained, so a deed executed and then left unregistered for an extended period can become difficult to register at all.

In practice, both parties, or their authorised representatives acting under a registered power of attorney, are usually expected to appear before the registering officer, since the officer has to be satisfied about identity and execution on both sides, and an unexplained absence of the seller at registration is one of the more common reasons a scheduled registration gets deferred.

Valuation: the State's say in what the property is worth

Stamp duty and registration fee in West Bengal, as in other states, are charged on the higher of the actual consideration stated in the deed or the value assessed under the State's own valuation mechanism for the area, rather than on whatever figure the parties choose to write into the deed. Declaring a consideration below the officially assessed value does not reduce the duty payable, and where the registering authority considers the stated value too low, it can refer the matter for a determination of market value before registration proceeds, which adds delay and, if the under-valuation was deliberate, exposes the parties to a penalty in addition to the shortfall itself.

Because valuation notifications are revised from area to area and from time to time, and the sources available while preparing this guide did not agree closely enough on the current numbers to state a reliable figure here, a buyer or seller should treat any stamp duty percentage quoted informally, including anything found on a generic property website, as indicative only, and should confirm the current duty, fee and any applicable rebate directly through the West Bengal registration department's own portal or at the registration office itself before relying on it for budgeting or for structuring the transaction.

Stamp duty and registration fee: why this guide does not quote a rate

West Bengal's stamp duty on a conveyance is charged under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 as applied to the state, with the applicable schedule and rates set and periodically revised by the State Government, and a separate registration fee charged under the Registration Act rules is added on top of the stamp duty itself. Commercial sources commonly describe West Bengal's conveyance duty as varying with the property's location, whether it is within a municipal corporation, a municipality or a rural panchayat area, and with the value bracket the property falls into, and describe the registration fee as a percentage of the market value, but the specific percentages given across different sources consulted for this guide did not match closely enough, including on whether any rebate currently applies for a particular category of buyer, for a single figure to be stated here with confidence.

What can be said reliably is the structure rather than the number: duty and fee both scale with the assessed value of the property, the location of the property affects the applicable rate, and any rebate scheme in force at a given time is time-bound and has historically been extended, reduced or withdrawn by the State at intervals, so a rate quoted from even a recent source can be out of date by the time a transaction is actually registered. The only sound practice is to obtain the current calculation directly from the registration department or a deed writer working from its current circular, rather than from a general guide of this kind.

e-Registration and the documents the office will expect

West Bengal has moved much of the pre-registration process online, with the duty and fee calculation, appointment booking and document upload for presentation handled through the State's e-registration portal before the parties physically attend the office of the Additional District Sub-Registrar or District Sub-Registrar having jurisdiction over the property, since registration has to take place at the office covering the area where the property is situated and cannot be done at a registry of convenience elsewhere.

The documents a registering officer will typically want include the executed deed itself, proof of identity and address of both parties, the property's own revenue and municipal records showing the seller's title and that current property tax is paid, encumbrance details, and, for a mutated property, the latest record showing the seller as the recorded owner, with photographs and biometric details of the parties and witnesses increasingly captured as part of the e-registration process itself rather than on paper alone.

Common errors that delay or undo a registration

Under-valuing the consideration to save on duty is the most frequent and the most costly error, since it exposes the parties to a valuation reference, a penalty, and, if discovered later in a dispute, an inference against the party who benefited from the understatement. A second common problem is presenting the deed after the four-month window in Section 23 has closed without having applied for the limited extension available under Section 25, which forces the parties to execute the deed afresh rather than simply registering the old one late.

A third recurring issue is a mismatch between the schedule of the property described in the deed and the municipal or revenue record, which the registering officer is likely to flag, and which should be reconciled before the registration appointment rather than discovered on the day, since correcting it afterward, once the deed is registered with an error, is considerably harder than fixing it before execution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there currently a rebate on stamp duty for women buyers in West Bengal?

The sources reviewed for this guide disagree on whether a women-buyer rebate currently applies in West Bengal, with some describing one and others stating there is none. Confirm the current position directly with the registration department or a local deed writer before relying on it.

What documents should I carry to the registration office for a property sale in Kolkata?

Expect to need the executed sale deed, identity and address proof for both parties, the seller's title and mutation records, proof that property tax is current, and encumbrance details for the property, in addition to whatever the e-registration portal's own checklist requires for the appointment.

Can I register a Kolkata property sale at a sub-registrar's office outside the property's own district?

No. Registration has to take place at the office of the Additional District Sub-Registrar or District Sub-Registrar having jurisdiction over the area where the property is situated, not at a registry chosen for convenience elsewhere in the state, and a deed presented at the wrong office will simply be refused.

What happens if I state a lower sale price than the property's assessed value to save on stamp duty?

Stamp duty is charged on the higher of the stated consideration or the officially assessed value, so under-valuation does not reduce the duty payable. The registering authority can refer the matter for a market value determination, which delays registration and can attract a penalty.

Is e-registration mandatory in West Bengal now, or can I still register entirely on paper?

West Bengal has moved valuation, duty calculation, appointment booking and document upload online through its e-registration portal, and the practical expectation is that parties use that process before attending the registration office in person, though the exact current scope of what must be done online should be confirmed with the registration department.
