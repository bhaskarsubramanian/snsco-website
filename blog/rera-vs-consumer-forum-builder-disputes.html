RERA or Consumer Forum? Choosing the Right Forum for a Builder Dispute

Homebuyers often assume they can pursue both remedies. The Supreme Court has made clear that choosing wrong — or choosing both — can cost you the claim entirely.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 10 May 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice — see our disclaimer

Two Remedies, One Choice

When a builder delays possession or fails to deliver as promised, homebuyers in Noida, Greater Noida, Kolkata and elsewhere generally have two potential avenues: a complaint before the state Real Estate Regulatory Authority (RERA), or a complaint before a consumer forum alleging deficiency in service. Both can, in principle, award compensation. But the Supreme Court has held that once a homebuyer elects to proceed under RERA for a possession-delay grievance, the doctrine of election generally bars a subsequent parallel claim before a consumer forum on the same cause of action. In plain terms: pick one, and pick carefully.

The doctrine of election is not unique to real estate disputes, but it has particular bite here because homebuyers often file wherever they hear a faster remedy is available, without realising the choice may be treated as final. Filing in the wrong forum first, only to abandon it for the other once delay drags on, is not a safe fallback plan under the current legal position.

Why RERA Is Often the Better First Move

RERA was purpose-built for exactly this kind of dispute. Developers must register projects, disclose accurate timelines, and deposit a defined percentage of project funds in escrow — giving RERA authorities direct visibility into a developer's project-level finances that a general consumer forum doesn't have. For straightforward possession-delay or refund claims, RERA's specialised process is usually faster and more targeted than a consumer complaint.

That said, RERA's effectiveness has itself come under judicial scrutiny — the Supreme Court has publicly questioned whether state RERA authorities are delivering meaningful relief to homebuyers as intended, rather than merely providing procedural convenience to developers. This scrutiny is likely to translate into tighter enforcement expectations for RERA authorities going forward, which should, over time, make the RERA route more reliably effective.

When a Consumer Forum Might Still Make Sense

Consumer forums remain relevant for complaints that don't fit neatly into RERA's project-registration framework — for instance, disputes over resale transactions, or claims focused heavily on compensation for mental agony and inconvenience rather than possession or refund. The key is to assess, before filing anything, which forum's remedy actually matches what you're trying to achieve.

Consumer forums are also generally the appropriate venue for disputes involving unregistered projects, since RERA's framework presupposes a registered project with defined regulatory obligations. If a project was never registered at all — itself a compliance failure — a consumer complaint may be the more natural first step, alongside flagging the non-registration to the RERA authority separately.

Practical Guidance Before You File

Before filing a complaint in either forum, gather your builder-buyer agreement, payment receipts, the project's RERA registration details, and any correspondence about the delay. If you're unsure which forum fits your situation, get advice before filing — because once you've elected a forum and it's on record, walking that election back later is difficult.

It is also worth checking the project's RERA registration status directly on the relevant state RERA portal before filing anything — UP RERA for Noida and Greater Noida projects, or West Bengal's HIRA portal for Kolkata projects — since the registration number and disclosed timeline will form the backbone of most RERA complaints.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I file in both RERA and a consumer forum to be safe?

Generally no — courts have applied the doctrine of election to bar a parallel consumer forum claim once RERA has been invoked for the same grievance, so filing in both is more likely to backfire than to help.

What can RERA actually award?

RERA can direct possession, refund with interest, or compensation for delay, depending on the specific relief sought and the facts of the case.

What if my builder isn't RERA-registered?

Non-registration is itself a compliance failure by the developer and can be raised before the relevant RERA authority; it does not automatically push the dispute into the consumer forum track.
