Inter-Country Adoption and Relocation: The CARA NOC and Hague Convention Process for NRI Families

An NRI or foreign-resident family adopting a child from India, or relocating an already-adopted child abroad, faces specific CARA and Hague Convention requirements beyond the standard adoption process: this guide explains them.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 30 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

Beyond the Basic CARA Process

Our general guide to adoption in India covers the core CARA/CARINGS process under the Juvenile Justice Act and Adoption Regulations, 2022, including a brief section on NRI, OCI and foreign adoptions. This guide goes deeper into a scenario that raises its own distinct set of legal questions: an NRI or foreign-resident family that is either adopting a child from India while resident abroad, or that has already adopted a child under Indian personal law (commonly the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, for a family that adopted a relative's child, for instance) and now needs to relocate that child abroad to live with the family permanently.

Why Relocating an Already-Adopted Child Is Not Automatic

A family that has completed an adoption under Indian personal law, without going through the CARA/JJ Act route, sometimes assumes that once the adoption itself is legally complete in India, taking the child abroad to live with the family is simply a matter of ordinary travel and immigration formalities. It is not, in every case, where the receiving country has its own immigration and child-protection requirements for a child entering on adoption-related grounds, and particularly where the receiving country is a party to the Hague Convention on Protection of Children and Co-operation in Respect of Inter-country Adoption, additional clearances are often required before the child can be relocated, even though the underlying adoption itself was validly completed under Indian law.

The CARA No-Objection Certificate

A recurring requirement in this scenario is a No-Objection Certificate (NOC) from CARA, confirming that the Authority has no objection to the child's relocation abroad, even where the adoption itself proceeded under personal law outside CARA's direct process. This NOC serves as India's confirmation, for the benefit of the receiving country's immigration and child-welfare authorities, that the adoption is recognised and that the child's relocation is not connected to any irregularity: a 2026 Delhi High Court ruling in Gur Kaur v. Union of India engaged directly with this NOC requirement in the context of an inter-country adoption and relocation dispute, underscoring that families should not treat the NOC as a mere formality that can be sought at the last minute.

Where India's Non-Accession to the Hague Convention Creates Complexity

India is a party to the Hague Adoption Convention (distinct from the separate Hague Convention on child abduction, to which India is not a party), which means inter-country adoptions properly conducted through the CARA/Hague-compliant process generally benefit from streamlined recognition in other Hague Convention member countries. Complexity increases specifically where a family has instead used a personal-law adoption route not directly processed through CARA's Hague-compliant channel, and the receiving country expects Hague-compliant documentation as a precondition for the child's immigration on adoption grounds: this is precisely the gap the CARA NOC, and related documentation, is generally used to bridge, though families should confirm the specific receiving country's own requirements directly, since these vary and are periodically updated on that country's side as well as India's.

Home Study and Post-Adoption Reporting for NRI/Foreign Adoptive Parents

For NRI or foreign-resident families adopting through CARA directly (rather than relocating an already personal-law-adopted child), the process generally requires a Home Study Report prepared to standards recognised in the parents' country of residence, coordinated through an Authorised Foreign Adoption Agency (AFAA) or an equivalent body in that country, along with post-adoption follow-up reporting obligations that can extend for a defined period after the child relocates: periodic reports confirming the child's wellbeing and adjustment, submitted to CARA through the coordinating agency. Families should plan for this ongoing reporting obligation as part of the adoption process, not treat it as complete once the child physically relocates.

Practical Guidance

NRI or foreign-resident families should identify, at the earliest planning stage, whether their intended adoption route (CARA/JJ Act versus personal law) will smoothly support the child's eventual relocation abroad given their specific receiving country's requirements, and should apply for a CARA NOC well in advance of any planned relocation rather than close to a travel date. Where the adoption has already occurred under personal law and relocation is now being planned, obtaining specific advice on the receiving country's current requirements, and on whether a CARA NOC or other documentation will be needed, before finalising travel plans is the safer course.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a family relocate a child abroad immediately after completing an adoption under Indian personal law?

Not always automatically, where the receiving country has its own immigration and child-protection requirements for a child entering on adoption grounds, additional clearances, commonly including a CARA No-Objection Certificate, are often required before relocation, even though the underlying adoption was validly completed in India.

What is the CARA No-Objection Certificate for relocation?

An NOC from the Central Adoption Resource Authority confirming it has no objection to an already-adopted child's relocation abroad, serving as India's confirmation to the receiving country's authorities that the adoption is recognised and the relocation is not connected to any irregularity.

Is India a party to the Hague Convention on inter-country adoption?

Yes, India is a party to the Hague Adoption Convention (a different convention from the Hague Convention on child abduction, to which India is not a party), which generally streamlines recognition of properly conducted CARA/Hague-compliant adoptions in other member countries.

What did the 2026 Gur Kaur v. Union of India ruling address?

It engaged with the CARA No-Objection Certificate requirement in the context of an inter-country adoption and relocation dispute, underscoring that the NOC should not be treated as a last-minute formality.

Does the adoption process end once an NRI family relocates the child abroad?

Not necessarily: for adoptions processed through CARA, post-adoption follow-up reporting obligations, coordinated through the family's Authorised Foreign Adoption Agency, can continue for a defined period after relocation, and families should plan for this ongoing obligation.

References & Further Reading

Specific requirements depend on both Indian regulations and the receiving country's immigration and child-welfare rules, which are periodically updated, and should be confirmed directly before finalising any relocation plan.
