RERA for NRI Homebuyers: Filing a Complaint and Attending a Hearing From Abroad

An NRI homebuyer facing a delayed or defective project doesn't have to be physically present in India to pursue a RERA complaint: this guide explains how filing, representation and hearings work from abroad.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 30 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

An NRI Homebuyer's RERA Rights Are the Same as Any Other Buyer's

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 does not draw any distinction between resident and non-resident homebuyers when it comes to substantive rights: an NRI who has booked a flat and paid instalments toward it has exactly the same right to complain about a delayed possession date, a defective construction, or a promoter's failure to comply with the sanctioned plan as a resident buyer does. What differs, in practice, is how an NRI actually exercises those rights while living abroad, and that procedural dimension is where NRI homebuyers most often stumble or delay filing when they shouldn't.

Filing a Complaint Does Not Require Physical Presence

Most State RERA authorities now accept complaints filed online through the authority's web portal, and even where a physical filing is still required or preferred, an NRI does not need to travel to India to file a complaint personally, filing through an authorised representative, whether a lawyer or an agent holding a Power of Attorney specifically covering RERA proceedings, is well-established practice. Getting the authorisation document right matters: a Power of Attorney or vakalatnama used for this purpose should be drafted and executed (with apostille or consular attestation, if executed abroad) to clearly cover representation before the specific State RERA Authority and Appellate Tribunal, rather than a vague, general authorisation that a Registrar's office or Tribunal might question at the filing stage.

Attending Hearings From Abroad

RERA proceedings are generally more streamlined and less formal than ordinary civil litigation, and many State RERA Authorities and Appellate Tribunals have accommodated video-conferencing appearances, particularly since the pandemic-era shift toward virtual hearings across Indian tribunals generally. An NRI complainant represented by a lawyer or authorised agent often does not need to personally appear at every hearing at all, since Indian procedure generally permits a party to be represented by counsel; where personal appearance or personal testimony is specifically required, video-conferencing is increasingly available as an alternative to physical presence, though this should be confirmed with the specific Authority or Tribunal handling the matter, since practice and available infrastructure can vary between States.

Serving Documents and Evidence From Abroad

NRIs pursuing a RERA complaint should keep organised, complete documentation of the transaction from the outset: the booking form, the agreement for sale, payment receipts (particularly important for an NRI, since these will also need to show payment was made through compliant banking channels as discussed in our FEMA guide), and all correspondence with the promoter regarding delays or defects, since assembling this evidence from abroad after a dispute has already escalated is considerably harder than maintaining organised records as the transaction proceeds. Original documents can generally be submitted through the authorised representative, with copies retained by the NRI complainant.

Execution of a RERA Order or Award From Abroad

Where a RERA Authority or Tribunal rules in the NRI complainant's favour, ordering a refund, interest, or compensation, enforcement follows the ordinary route for execution of such orders in India, generally through the civil courts or the RERA Authority's own recovery mechanism (recovery as arrears of land revenue, in several States), and an NRI complainant does not need to be present in India for this execution process either, provided their authorised representative continues to have the necessary authority to pursue recovery and, eventually, to receive and repatriate the recovered funds through the NRI's own NRE or NRO account.

Practical Guidance for NRI Homebuyers

NRIs with a grievance against a project should not delay filing merely because they cannot be physically present in India: timely filing matters, since RERA has its own limitation and procedural timelines, and unnecessary delay can weaken a complaint. Engaging a lawyer in the project's jurisdiction early, executing a properly scoped and authenticated Power of Attorney for the specific purpose of RERA representation, and maintaining organised transaction records from the outset are the three steps that most directly determine how smoothly an NRI can pursue a RERA complaint from abroad.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an NRI need to travel to India to file a RERA complaint?

No: most State RERA Authorities accept online filing, and where physical filing is required, an NRI can file through an authorised representative, typically a lawyer or an agent holding a properly scoped Power of Attorney.

Can an NRI attend a RERA hearing without being physically present in India?

Often yes: many RERA Authorities and Appellate Tribunals now accommodate video-conferencing appearances, and where a party is represented by counsel, personal appearance at every hearing is frequently unnecessary, though practice varies by State and should be confirmed for the specific matter.

What documentation should an NRI keep for a potential RERA complaint?

The booking form, the agreement for sale, payment receipts (showing payment through compliant banking channels), and all correspondence with the promoter about delays or defects: organised from the outset, since assembling this evidence after a dispute escalates is considerably harder.

How does an NRI enforce a RERA order made in their favour while living abroad?

Through the ordinary execution route available in India, civil courts or the RERA Authority's own recovery mechanism, with the NRI's authorised representative continuing to hold the necessary authority to pursue recovery and receive funds through the NRI's NRE or NRO account.

What should an NRI's Power of Attorney for a RERA matter specifically cover?

It should clearly authorise representation before the specific State RERA Authority and Appellate Tribunal handling the matter, rather than a vague, general authorisation, and should be properly apostilled or consular-attested if executed abroad.
