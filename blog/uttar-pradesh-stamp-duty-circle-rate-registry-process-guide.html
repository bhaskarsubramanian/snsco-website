Buying Property in Noida and Uttar Pradesh: Stamp Duty, Circle Rates and the Registry Process

How stamp duty is calculated against the circle rate in Uttar Pradesh, the women's concession, the registry process, and mutation after registry.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 3 November 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

Stamp duty in Uttar Pradesh is charged on whichever is higher: the price the buyer and seller have actually agreed, or the circle rate the state has notified for that locality. A buyer who thinks a lower sale price means lower duty is usually wrong, because the sub-registrar's office will compute duty on the circle rate the moment the declared price falls below it. For a male buyer the duty currently works out to about 7% of that higher value, with a concession bringing it down for a woman buying alone, plus a flat 1% registration fee layered on top. None of this changes the need to actually check the sector-wise rate before signing anything, since circle rates in Noida and across the state get revised and a figure quoted by a broker six months ago may no longer hold.

This guide walks through how the circle rate and the agreement value interact, what the current duty and registration figures look like, how the registry appointment and document execution actually proceed at the sub-registrar's office, and why registering the sale deed is not the end of the process: the property still needs to be mutated in the revenue or municipal records before it is safe to treat the registered owner as the recorded owner for tax and utility purposes. It also flags the disputes that most often follow a rushed registration, mainly undervaluation references and unregistered documents that buyers later rely on as if they were as good as a registered deed.

Why the circle rate decides your stamp duty, not the price on the deed

Every locality in Uttar Pradesh, down to the sector or even the specific stretch of a colony, has a minimum valuation notified by the state's Stamps and Registration Department, commonly called the circle rate. When a sale deed is presented for registration, the sub-registrar checks the declared consideration against this rate for that category of property, whether it is a plot, a built-up flat, or commercial space. If the agreed price is at or above the circle rate, duty is charged on the agreed price. If it is below, duty is charged on the circle rate regardless of what the deed says the parties paid, and the registering officer has the power to refer the matter for valuation if the declared figure looks understated.

This is why circle rates matter even to a buyer who has no intention of under-declaring anything: they set the floor for duty and therefore for the cash the buyer must keep ready at registration, on top of the purchase price itself. Noida's rates have been revised upward in 2026, reportedly the first general revision in some years, which pushed up both the duty payable on new purchases and the benchmark used in disputes over past undervaluation. The only reliable way to know the applicable rate for a specific plot or flat is to check it on the state's IGRS portal or confirm it with the sub-registrar's office before finalising the sale price, since broker-quoted figures and even recent articles can lag behind a fresh notification.

Current stamp duty, registration charges and the concession for women buyers

The standard stamp duty in Uttar Pradesh works out to roughly 7% of the higher of the agreement value or circle rate where the buyer is a man, with registration charged separately at a flat 1% for every category of buyer. Where a woman is buying alone, the rate drops to around 6%, and where a man and a woman buy jointly the blended rate commonly applied is about 6.5%. This concession for sole women buyers was widened in 2025, with the state raising the ceiling on which the lower rate applies to properties valued up to roughly Rs 1 crore, a change reported from more than one source, though the exact cap and any further revision should be confirmed on the IGRS portal before a deal is structured around it.

Buyers sometimes assume the women's concession is automatic on every transaction with a woman's name on the deed, but it generally applies only where the woman is the sole purchaser or, in the joint case, where the blended rate applies to the specific combination of buyers, and it is usually conditioned on the property value staying within the notified ceiling. A deal structured to use a relative's name purely to access the lower rate, without that person being a genuine party to the transaction and the funding, invites its own set of complications later, particularly if the real buyer wants to resell or mortgage the property without the named owner's full cooperation.

The registry process at the sub-registrar's office

The practical sequence starts with confirming the applicable circle rate for the specific sector or plot, drafting the sale deed (or in Noida, often an authority-issued lease deed or transfer memorandum where the land itself is leasehold from the development authority), and computing stamp duty and registration fee on the higher of the two values. Payment is typically made as e-stamp through the state's authorised collection channels before the deed is presented for registration, since an unstamped or under-stamped document will not be accepted at the counter.

An appointment is then booked through the IGRS Uttar Pradesh portal for a specific date and sub-registrar office, and both parties, along with witnesses, attend in person for biometric verification, photograph capture and signature on the deed in the presence of the registering officer. The deed is scrutinised for the correct description of the property, the parties' identity documents, and proof of duty payment before it is formally registered and a copy is handed back with the registration endorsement. Buyers of leasehold plots from Noida, Greater Noida or YEIDA should separately confirm that the authority has no-objection or has recorded the transfer in its own records, since a registered deed alone does not substitute for the authority updating its allotment file.

Mutation: the step buyers forget after registration

Registration transfers title as between the buyer and the seller and creates the public record of the transaction, but it does not by itself update the revenue or municipal records that determine who is billed for property tax, who appears as the owner for utility connections, or who is treated as the recorded khatedar or occupant for other official purposes. That update, mutation, has to be applied for separately with the concerned revenue authority or, for urban property falling under a development authority or municipal corporation, with that body's records section, after registration is complete.

The documents typically required include the registered sale deed, the previous ownership record, identity proof and, for authority land, the allotment or transfer letter from the development authority. Skipping mutation does not undo the sale, since the registered deed remains valid proof of the transaction, but an unmutated property can cause real friction on resale, on mortgage, or when the earlier owner's name keeps showing up on tax demands and notices meant for the new owner. Buyers should treat the mutation application as part of closing the purchase, not as an optional formality to attend to later.

What goes wrong: undervaluation, unregistered agreements and authority dues

Among registration-stage disputes, the most common is a declared price that the sub-registrar considers understated against the circle rate, which can lead to a reference for valuation and, in cases the authorities view as a deliberate attempt to evade duty, a demand for the deficit along with a penalty. Buyers who agree to a lower figure on the deed to save on duty, with the balance paid outside the documented price, carry that risk and also weaken their own position if a dispute later arises over how much was actually paid for the property.

A second recurring problem is treating an unregistered agreement to sell, a power of attorney, or a builder's allotment letter as if it were equivalent to a registered conveyance. Under the Registration Act, 1908, documents that affect immovable property above a certain value generally require registration to be used as primary evidence of title, and an unregistered instrument, while it may still be relevant for limited purposes such as showing possession or the nature of the transaction, does not pass or prove title in the way a registered sale deed does. A third problem specific to Noida and Greater Noida plots is pending dues owed to the development authority; a transfer recorded by private sale deed without the authority's own no-objection can later run into the authority refusing to recognise the transfer or insisting on dues from the new owner, so clearing or at least disclosing outstanding authority charges before registration avoids an unpleasant surprise after possession.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I pay stamp duty on the price we agreed, or on the circle rate?

On whichever figure is higher. If the agreed price is below the notified circle rate for that locality and category of property, the sub-registrar computes stamp duty on the circle rate, not on the lower price written into the deed.

Is the lower stamp duty rate for women available on any property, regardless of value?

No. The concession for a woman buying alone is generally capped at a notified property value, reported at around Rs 1 crore following the 2025 revision, and the exact ceiling should be confirmed on the IGRS Uttar Pradesh portal before relying on it for a specific purchase.

What happens if I already registered a deed at a value lower than the circle rate?

The registering authority can refer the matter for valuation and demand the deficit stamp duty along with a penalty where it considers the declared value understated. This can surface years later, including at the time of a subsequent sale, so it is worth checking rather than assuming the matter has closed.

Is mutation the same thing as registration?

No. Registration records the sale deed and transfers title between the parties. Mutation is a separate, later step that updates the revenue or municipal ownership record for tax and utility purposes, and it has to be applied for on its own after registration is complete.

Can I challenge the circle rate if I think it is too high for my specific plot?

There is generally a process to raise a valuation objection or seek a reference where a party disputes the applicable rate for a particular property, but this needs to be taken up with the Stamps and Registration Department or through the statutory valuation mechanism rather than simply under-declaring the price and hoping it goes unnoticed.
