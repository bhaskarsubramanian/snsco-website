OCI Cardholder Property Rights in India: What an OCI Can and Cannot Do

An Overseas Citizen of India card gives significant rights in India, but not full parity with a citizen: this guide explains what OCI cardholders can and cannot do when it comes to property, voting and other rights.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 30 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

OCI Is Not Dual Citizenship

Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) status is frequently, and inaccurately, described as India's version of dual citizenship. It is not. An OCI card, granted under the Citizenship Act, 1955 as amended, confers a defined, long-term visa-free travel and residency status in India, together with a specific, statutorily enumerated set of rights and privileges broadly comparable to those of a Non-Resident Indian citizen, but it stops well short of full citizenship, and India does not recognise dual citizenship in the constitutional sense at all. Understanding exactly which rights an OCI cardholder does and does not have is important, because the gaps are easy to assume away incorrectly.

Property Rights: Broadly Aligned With NRIs

For property purposes, an OCI cardholder is generally treated on the same footing as an NRI under the FEMA framework governing acquisition of immovable property discussed in our companion guide: an OCI can purchase residential and commercial property in India without needing specific RBI permission, subject to the same restriction against purchasing agricultural land, plantation property and farmhouses that applies to NRIs (with the same exception preserving property already owned or inherited before the restriction became relevant to that individual). Payment rules, and the conditions around repatriating sale proceeds, likewise generally track the NRI framework rather than differing meaningfully for OCI cardholders.

Rights an OCI Cardholder Does Not Have

The gaps matter as much as the similarities. An OCI cardholder cannot vote in Indian elections, cannot hold Indian constitutional or certain other public offices (including, for instance, the offices of President, Vice-President, or a Judge of the Supreme Court or High Court, among various other specified positions), and cannot generally acquire agricultural land, plantation property or farmhouses, matching the NRI restriction. OCI cardholders also require specific permission to undertake mountaineering, missionary, journalistic or research work in India, and, notably, an OCI card is not equivalent to an Indian passport for any purpose: an OCI cardholder travels on their foreign passport, with the OCI card functioning as a long-term, multiple-entry visa-equivalent document attached to it.

Employment and Professional Practice

OCI cardholders are generally permitted to work in India and to pursue professions, broadly on par with resident Indians in most sectors, subject to sector-specific regulatory requirements that can apply regardless of OCI status (professional licensing requirements for law, medicine, or chartered accountancy, for instance, which involve their own separate qualification and enrolment rules that are not automatically satisfied by OCI status alone). An OCI cardholder considering a specific regulated profession in India should check that profession's own licensing rules separately from their OCI status, rather than assuming OCI status alone resolves professional eligibility.

Inheritance and Succession

OCI cardholders can inherit property in India, including agricultural land, plantation property or a farmhouse, even though they could not have purchased such property directly: inheritance is treated differently from purchase under the relevant FEMA framework, mirroring the position for NRIs. An OCI cardholder inheriting Indian property should nonetheless confirm the specific succession law that applies (generally following the personal law of the deceased, as discussed in our guide on intestate succession and domicile) and should separately confirm any conditions attached to subsequently transferring or repatriating proceeds from such inherited property.

Practical Guidance

OCI cardholders dealing with property, employment, or civic participation in India should treat their status as closely aligned with, but not identical to, that of an NRI citizen, and should specifically check the rules for the particular transaction or activity in question rather than assuming OCI status resolves every question the way full citizenship would. Where a transaction or right depends on citizenship specifically, voting, holding certain public offices, or agricultural land purchase, OCI status will not substitute for it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is OCI the same as dual citizenship?

No. India does not recognise dual citizenship in the constitutional sense. OCI status confers a long-term visa-free travel and residency status with a defined set of rights broadly comparable to an NRI's, but it is not equivalent to citizenship.

Can an OCI cardholder buy property in India?

Yes, generally on the same footing as an NRI: OCI cardholders can purchase residential and commercial property without specific RBI permission, subject to the same restriction against purchasing agricultural land, plantation property and farmhouses.

Can an OCI cardholder vote in Indian elections or hold public office?

No: OCI cardholders cannot vote in Indian elections and cannot hold certain Indian constitutional and public offices, including the offices of President, Vice-President, or a Judge of the Supreme Court or High Court, among other specified positions.

Can an OCI cardholder inherit agricultural land in India even though they can't buy it?

Yes: inheritance is treated differently from purchase under the applicable FEMA framework, so an OCI cardholder can inherit agricultural land, plantation property or a farmhouse even though they could not have purchased such property directly.

Does an OCI card work as an Indian passport?

No: an OCI cardholder travels on their own foreign passport; the OCI card functions as a long-term, multiple-entry visa-equivalent document attached to that foreign passport, not as an Indian travel document.

References & Further Reading

This guide provides a general overview of OCI cardholder rights. Specific rights and restrictions are set out in the Citizenship Act, 1955 as amended, and associated notifications, which should be checked directly for the current position.

  1. Citizenship Act, 1955, Section 7A–7D (as amended): Overseas Citizen of India registration and rights.
  2. FEMA (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules, 2019: property acquisition rules applicable to OCI cardholders on the same footing as NRIs.
  3. Ministry of Home Affairs notifications specifying restricted activities and offices not available to OCI cardholders.
