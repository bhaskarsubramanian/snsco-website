NRI Property Purchase in India: The FEMA Rules You Need to Know

NRIs buying property in India are subject to specific FEMA restrictions and permitted routes — this guide explains what an NRI can and can't buy, and how payment must be made.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 30 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice — see our disclaimer

The Starting Point: FEMA, Not the Transfer of Property Act Alone

An NRI buying property in India is subject not only to the ordinary state-level property law and stamp duty rules that apply to any buyer, but also to the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA) and the specific regulations framed under it governing acquisition and transfer of immovable property in India by persons resident outside India. These rules exist to regulate foreign-exchange-linked property transactions distinctly from ordinary domestic transactions, and getting them wrong can create real complications, from delayed registration to difficulty repatriating sale proceeds later.

What an NRI Can and Cannot Buy

Under the FEMA regulations, an NRI (a person resident outside India who is a citizen of India) or an OCI cardholder can generally purchase residential and commercial immovable property in India without needing specific RBI permission for the purchase itself. The significant restriction is on agricultural land, plantation property and farmhouses — an NRI cannot generally purchase these categories of property, though an NRI who already owned such property before becoming a non-resident, or who inherits it, can generally continue to hold it. NRIs considering a purchase should confirm upfront that the specific property they intend to buy does not fall into a restricted category, since local land records do not always make this immediately obvious.

How Payment Must Be Made

Payment for property purchased by an NRI must be made through banking channels — funds remitted from abroad through normal banking channels, or held in the NRI's NRE (Non-Resident External), NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) or FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) account in India. FEMA regulations specifically prohibit payment in any other manner, including payment outside India or through the transfer of foreign currency in cash — a rule that exists to keep property transactions within the formal, traceable banking and foreign-exchange reporting system. Which specific account (NRE, NRO or FCNR) is used for the payment can also affect how easily the corresponding sale proceeds can later be repatriated, which is a factor NRIs should plan for at the time of purchase, not only at the time of eventual sale.

Repatriation of Sale Proceeds Later

When an NRI later sells property they purchased in India, repatriating the sale proceeds abroad is subject to its own set of conditions — commonly, a cap on the amount that can be repatriated in a financial year without specific RBI permission (linked to remittance limits under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme framework and related FEMA provisions), and a requirement that the original purchase itself complied with FEMA rules and, where relevant, that applicable taxes on the sale have been paid and appropriate certification obtained. An NRI who did not use compliant payment channels at the time of purchase can find repatriation of the eventual sale proceeds considerably more difficult, which is one of the most common and most avoidable mistakes NRIs make in India property transactions.

Power of Attorney for Purchases Made Remotely

Because NRIs are often not physically present in India for every step of a property transaction, many rely on a Power of Attorney to authorise someone in India to execute documents, register the property, and handle related formalities on their behalf. This is legally permissible and commonly used, but it also creates a recognised fraud risk that NRIs should take seriously when drafting and granting a POA for this purpose — a topic our companion guide on NRI Powers of Attorney addresses in detail.

Practical Steps Before Purchasing

Before committing to a purchase, NRIs should confirm the property is not agricultural land, plantation property or a farmhouse; arrange payment exclusively through an NRE, NRO or FCNR account or direct inward remittance, never in cash or through informal channels; keep clear documentary records of the source of funds and the payment trail, since this documentation will matter significantly if and when the property is eventually sold and proceeds need to be repatriated; and, where a Power of Attorney is being used, ensure it is carefully drafted with appropriate safeguards.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an NRI buy agricultural land in India?

Generally no — FEMA regulations restrict NRIs from purchasing agricultural land, plantation property and farmhouses, though an NRI who owned such property before becoming a non-resident, or who inherits it, can generally continue to hold it.

How must an NRI pay for property purchased in India?

Through banking channels only — funds remitted from abroad through normal banking channels, or from the NRI's NRE, NRO or FCNR account in India. Payment in cash or through channels outside the formal banking system is not permitted under FEMA.

Can an NRI freely repatriate the sale proceeds of property they sell in India?

Subject to conditions — generally a cap on the amount repatriable in a financial year without specific RBI permission, and a requirement that the original purchase complied with FEMA and that applicable taxes on the sale have been paid, with appropriate certification obtained.

Does an NRI need RBI permission just to buy residential property in India?

Generally no — an NRI or OCI cardholder can purchase residential and commercial property without needing specific RBI permission for the purchase itself, unlike the restricted categories of agricultural land, plantation property and farmhouses.

Why does the payment channel used at purchase matter for a later sale?

Because the ease of repatriating sale proceeds later depends partly on which account (NRE, NRO or FCNR) and channel was used for the original purchase — an NRI who did not use compliant payment channels at purchase can find repatriation of the eventual sale proceeds considerably more difficult.

References & Further Reading

This guide provides a general overview of FEMA rules on NRI property purchase. Specific transactions should be checked against the current FEMA regulations and RBI master directions in force at the time, which are periodically updated.

  3. Liberalised Remittance Scheme framework, as relevant to repatriation limits on sale proceeds.
