Legal Structuring of Infrastructure Asset Monetisation Under the National Monetisation Pipeline: A Primer

How asset monetisation transactions under India's National Monetisation Pipeline are typically structured: TOT, OMT, InvIT and long-term lease models: and the legal diligence points sponsors and investors should focus on.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 4 March 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

What Asset Monetisation Under the NMP Involves

The National Monetisation Pipeline is a government framework for unlocking value from already-constructed public infrastructure assets: national highway stretches, railway stations, power transmission lines, and similar brownfield assets. By transferring the right to operate and collect revenue from the asset to a private or institutional investor for a defined period, without transferring ownership of the underlying asset itself. It is distinct from privatisation: title remains with the public authority throughout.

Common Structuring Models

Depending on the sector and asset, monetisation transactions are typically structured as a Toll-Operate-Transfer (TOT) concession for highway stretches, an Operate-Maintain-Transfer (OMT) contract for assets where toll or fee collection risk is not transferred, a long-term lease for assets like railway stations or warehousing, or a transfer of the underlying asset (or a bundle of assets) into an Infrastructure Investment Trust (InvIT), which then raises capital from institutional and retail investors against the pooled asset cash flows.

Key Diligence Points for Investors

Investors bidding for a monetisation transaction should focus diligence on: the condition and remaining useful life of the brownfield asset, since a TOT or OMT bid is priced on projected cash flows from an asset the bidder did not construct; any pending litigation, land title or encroachment issues affecting the asset that predate the transaction; the precise scope of operation and maintenance obligations being transferred versus retained by the authority; and the concession or lease deed's provisions on handover condition and asset-return obligations at the end of the term.

Regulatory Approvals and Sector-Specific Consents

Depending on the sector, monetisation transactions may require sector-regulator consent (for instance, from the relevant regulatory authority for transmission assets), environmental or forest clearance transfer where the underlying asset's original clearances need to be carried over to the new operator, and, for an InvIT route, compliance with SEBI's InvIT Regulations governing the trust structure, sponsor lock-in and unit-holder disclosures.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does asset monetisation under the NMP transfer ownership of the asset?

No: monetisation transactions under the NMP framework transfer the right to operate the asset and collect associated revenue for a defined period; legal title to the underlying public asset remains with the government or public authority.

What is the practical difference between a TOT and an OMT structure?

Under a TOT structure the private operator typically takes toll or fee collection (and traffic/demand) risk for the concession period; under an OMT structure, operation and maintenance responsibilities are transferred but fee-collection risk generally remains with, or is structured differently by, the authority. The exact allocation depends on the specific contract.

Can a monetised asset later be bundled into an InvIT?

Yes, this is one of the recognised routes under the NMP framework: operating assets already monetised through a TOT or similar structure can subsequently be transferred into an InvIT to raise further capital, subject to SEBI's InvIT Regulations and the underlying concession or transfer deed's own transferability provisions.
