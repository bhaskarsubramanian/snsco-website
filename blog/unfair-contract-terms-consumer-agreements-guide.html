Unfair Contract Terms in Consumer Agreements: What the 2019 Act Changed

A practical guide explaining how the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 addresses unfair terms in standard-form consumer contracts, and what a consumer can do about a one-sided clause.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 29 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

What Is an 'Unfair Contract'?

Under Section 2(46) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, an 'unfair contract' means a contract between a manufacturer, trader or service provider and a consumer that causes significant change in the rights of the consumer, including terms that impose an excessive security deposit for a breach of contract, impose a disproportionate penalty for a breach by the consumer while limiting the liability of the other party for its own breach, or unilaterally permit termination of the contract without reasonable cause.

Why Standard-Form Contracts Raise Particular Concern

Most consumer agreements, service subscriptions, membership terms, app terms of use, are standard-form contracts drafted entirely by the business, offered on a take-it-or-leave-it basis. A consumer typically has no realistic opportunity to negotiate individual terms, which is precisely why the 2019 Act's unfair-contract provision focuses on terms causing a significant, one-sided change in the consumer's rights rather than requiring proof of individual negotiation unfairness.

Examples of Terms That May Be Scrutinised

Clauses commonly scrutinised include those imposing a disproportionately high cancellation or exit penalty on the consumer while allowing the business to exit with minimal or no consequence, clauses requiring the consumer to pay an excessive security deposit relative to the value of the contract, and clauses allowing the business to unilaterally vary material terms after the contract is entered into, without adequate consumer consent or notice.

Where This Fits Within a Consumer Complaint

An unfair-contract-term allegation is typically raised as part of a broader consumer complaint: for instance, where a business seeks to enforce a disproportionate penalty clause against a consumer, the consumer can challenge that specific clause as an unfair contract term rather than accepting its enforceability at face value.

Practical Guidance for Consumers and Businesses

Consumers entering a standard-form agreement should specifically note any cancellation, penalty, or unilateral-variation clause before agreeing, since these are the categories most likely to be scrutinised. Businesses drafting standard-form consumer contracts should ensure penalty and exit provisions are proportionate and mutual where possible, since a markedly one-sided clause invites both regulatory and forum-level scrutiny.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a contract term 'unfair' under the 2019 Act?

A term causing a significant, one-sided change in a consumer's rights, such as a disproportionate penalty on the consumer while limiting the business's own liability, or an unreasonable unilateral termination right for the business.

Can I challenge just one clause in an otherwise valid contract?

Yes: an unfair-contract-term challenge is typically directed at the specific offending clause rather than the entire agreement.

Does signing a standard-form contract mean I've accepted every term?

Signing generally indicates acceptance, but the 2019 Act specifically allows scrutiny of terms causing a significant, one-sided change in the consumer's rights, regardless of signature.

Where do I raise an unfair-contract-term objection?

Typically as part of a consumer complaint before the appropriate forum, where the term is directly relevant to the relief being sought or resisted.
