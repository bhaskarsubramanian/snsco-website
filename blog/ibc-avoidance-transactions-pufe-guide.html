Avoidance of Transactions Under the IBC: Preferential, Undervalued, Extortionate and Fraudulent Transactions

A guide for a resolution professional, liquidator, or a party that received payment or a transfer from a company that has since gone into CIRP, explaining the PUFE categories the IBC allows a tribunal to unwind, the look-back periods, and what a recipient of an avoided transaction risks.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 28 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice — see our disclaimer

Overview

A guide for a resolution professional, liquidator, or a party that received payment or a transfer from a company that has since gone into CIRP, explaining the PUFE categories the IBC allows a tribunal to unwind, the look-back periods, and what a recipient of an avoided transaction risks.

What You Need to Know

  • The IBC allows an Adjudicating Authority to avoid four categories of pre-insolvency transactions, collectively known as PUFE transactions: preferential (Sections 43-44), undervalued (Sections 45-48), extortionate credit (Sections 50-51), and fraudulent or wrongful trading (Sections 49 and 66)
  • A preferential transaction under Section 43 involves a transfer of the corporate debtor's property for the benefit of a creditor, surety or guarantor on account of an antecedent debt, which places that recipient in a materially better position than it would have received in a Section 53 liquidation distribution
  • The look-back period for preferential transactions is two years before the insolvency commencement date for transactions with a related party, and one year for transactions with an unrelated party — transactions outside these windows generally cannot be avoided as preferential
  • An undervalued transaction under Section 45 involves a gift, or a transfer for consideration significantly below the asset's true market value, made by the corporate debtor without a legitimate commercial rationale
  • Section 66 empowers the Adjudicating Authority to direct any person knowingly party to fraudulent or wrongful trading — carrying on the corporate debtor's business with intent to defraud creditors, or without a reasonable prospect of avoiding insolvency — to contribute to the corporate debtor's assets
  • A resolution professional or liquidator identifying a potential avoidance transaction must make the required application to the Adjudicating Authority within the statutory timeline, since courts have held that a resolution plan cannot ordinarily extinguish avoidance applications that were not disposed of before the plan's approval

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the four PUFE categories a resolution professional can seek to avoid?

Preferential transactions (Sections 43-44), undervalued transactions (Sections 45-48), extortionate credit transactions (Sections 50-51), and fraudulent or wrongful trading (Sections 49 and 66).

How far back can a preferential transaction be avoided?

Two years before the insolvency commencement date for transactions with a related party, and one year for transactions with an unrelated party — transactions outside these windows generally can't be avoided as preferential.

Can a resolution plan wipe out a pending avoidance application?

Courts have held that a resolution plan cannot ordinarily extinguish an avoidance application that was not disposed of before the plan's approval, though this remains a developing, judicially contested area.
