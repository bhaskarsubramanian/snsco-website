The Section 53 Liquidation Waterfall: Order of Priority for Distribution of a Corporate Debtor's Assets

A guide for creditors, liquidators and other stakeholders in an IBC liquidation explaining the mandatory order of priority under Section 53 in which the liquidation estate of a corporate debtor is distributed among different classes of claimants.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 29 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

Overview

A guide for creditors, liquidators and other stakeholders in an IBC liquidation explaining the mandatory order of priority under Section 53 in which the liquidation estate of a corporate debtor is distributed among different classes of claimants.

What You Need to Know

  • Section 53 prescribes a strict, cascading order of priority for distribution of the liquidation estate. Proceeds are applied fully to each class before anything is available for the next class, and claimants within the same class rank equally (pari passu) and abate proportionately if the estate is insufficient.
  • The first priority is the costs of the CIRP and liquidation process (including the resolution professional's or liquidator's fees) and workmen's dues for the twenty-four months preceding the liquidation commencement date, ranking equally with secured creditors who have relinquished their security interest.
  • The next priority covers wages and unpaid dues owed to employees other than workmen for the twelve months preceding liquidation commencement, followed by unsecured financial creditors, then government dues and any remaining debt owed to a secured creditor who enforced its security outside the waterfall but has a shortfall.
  • Preference shareholders and equity shareholders or partners rank last, receiving distributions only after every other class of claim, including operational creditors. Has been paid in full, reflecting the residual, subordinate nature of ownership interests in a liquidation.
  • A secured creditor may choose to either relinquish its security interest to the liquidation estate (and share in the Section 53 distribution at the first-priority level alongside workmen's dues) or realise its security outside the waterfall, in which case any shortfall is treated as an unsecured claim and any surplus must be returned to the estate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is paid first in a Section 53 liquidation distribution?

The costs of the CIRP and liquidation process, together with workmen's dues for the twenty-four months preceding liquidation commencement, rank first, alongside secured creditors who have relinquished their security interest to the liquidation estate.

Where do equity shareholders rank in the Section 53 waterfall?

Last. Equity and preference shareholders are paid only after every other class of creditor, including unsecured operational and financial creditors and government dues, has been satisfied in full.

What happens if a secured creditor realises its security outside the liquidation process?

Any shortfall after realising the security is treated as an unsecured claim in the waterfall, and any surplus realised beyond the secured debt must be returned to the liquidation estate for distribution to other claimants.
