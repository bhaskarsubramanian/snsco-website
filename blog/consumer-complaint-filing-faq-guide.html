FAQs on Filing a Consumer Complaint in India: Forum, Limitation and Relief

A practical FAQ guide for a consumer considering a complaint over defective goods or deficient services, covering which forum to approach, limitation, required documents, and the relief available.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 28 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice — see our disclaimer

Who Qualifies as a "Consumer"?

Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, a "consumer" is a person who buys goods or hires or avails services for consideration, for personal use — not for a purely commercial or resale purpose (though a person using goods exclusively for self-employment to earn a livelihood is included). This threshold question matters because a complaint filed by someone who does not qualify as a consumer under the Act, for instance a business purchasing goods purely for resale, may be dismissed at the outset regardless of the merits of the underlying grievance.

Which Consumer Forum Should I Approach?

The Act establishes a three-tier structure — the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, the State Commission, and the National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC) — with jurisdiction now determined by the value of the goods or services paid for as consideration, rather than the amount of compensation claimed. As a general guide: District Commissions handle complaints up to Rs. 50 lakh in value, State Commissions handle complaints above Rs. 50 lakh up to Rs. 2 crore, and the NCDRC handles complaints above Rs. 2 crore, in addition to their respective appellate roles over orders from the tier below. A complaint filed in the wrong forum on valuation grounds can face a threshold objection, so correctly computing the relevant value at the outset is an important first step.

What Is the Limitation Period?

A consumer complaint must ordinarily be filed within two years from the date the cause of action arose. Where the deficiency or defect is genuinely latent — not apparent or reasonably discoverable at the time of purchase — the limitation period instead runs from the date of discovery, though this exception is applied carefully and does not extend to defects that were reasonably discoverable earlier. Commissions have discretion to condone a delay in filing where sufficient cause is shown, but a complainant should not rely on this discretion as a substitute for filing within the ordinary period.

What Documents Should I Gather Before Filing?

A complaint is generally strengthened by documentary proof of the transaction (invoice, receipt, payment confirmation), correspondence with the opposite party regarding the defect or deficiency (emails, service requests, complaint tickets), any expert or inspection report supporting the existence of the defect, and evidence of the loss or harm suffered as a result. Since compensation must generally be tied to demonstrated loss rather than awarded as an arbitrary figure, gathering evidence of actual loss — repair costs, medical expenses, lost value — from the outset materially strengthens a complaint.

Do I Need a Lawyer?

The consumer forum framework is designed to be relatively accessible, and a complainant can file and pursue a complaint personally without mandatory legal representation, particularly for straightforward, lower-value matters. That said, complex disputes — involving expert evidence, significant sums, or opposing parties represented by counsel — are often pursued more effectively with legal assistance, particularly at the drafting stage where the relief sought and supporting facts need to be framed precisely.

What Relief Can the Forum Grant?

Depending on the nature of the complaint, a consumer forum can direct removal of the defect, replacement of the goods, refund of the price paid, compensation for loss or injury suffered due to the negligence of the opposite party, discontinuation of an unfair trade practice, and in appropriate cases, punitive or exemplary damages along with the costs of litigation. The specific relief granted depends heavily on what is actually pleaded and proved — a complaint should therefore specify precisely what relief is sought, rather than leaving it to the forum's general discretion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which consumer forum should I approach?

Jurisdiction is based on the value of goods or services paid for as consideration — District Commissions up to Rs. 50 lakh, State Commissions above Rs. 50 lakh up to Rs. 2 crore, and the National Commission above Rs. 2 crore.

What is the limitation period for filing a consumer complaint?

Generally two years from the date the cause of action arose; for a latent defect, this runs from the date of discovery, and delay can be condoned for sufficient cause.

Do I need a lawyer to file a consumer complaint?

No — a complaint can be filed personally without mandatory representation, though many consumers engage counsel for more complex or higher-value disputes.

What relief can a consumer forum grant?

Removal of defect, replacement, refund, compensation for loss or injury, and in appropriate cases punitive damages and costs.
