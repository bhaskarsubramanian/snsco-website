Set-Off and Close-Out Netting During CIRP: Where Does the Moratorium Leave Master Agreements?

An explainer on contractual netting under master trading agreements, ordinary set-off and the Section 14 moratorium in corporate insolvency, and why the law on the point is not settled.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 17 July 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice

About this article: A general explainer prepared by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors on a recurring type of dispute. It does not report a specific judgment or order, names no parties and gives no case citation, so it should not be cited as authority. Law and practice change; please verify against the current statute and primary sources before relying on it.

Master trading and derivatives-style agreements commonly provide that, on default or insolvency, the open positions are closed out and the amounts owed in each direction are netted to a single balance. Netting is different from an ordinary set-off, where a creditor deducts an unrelated debt owed by it to the debtor.

When a corporate insolvency resolution process begins, the moratorium under Section 14 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 restricts recovery actions against the corporate debtor and the disposal of its assets. A resolution professional may object that a counterparty's netting or set-off after admission is an adjustment against the debtor's assets. A counterparty will argue that, where all transactions were made under a single master agreement that treats them as one net obligation, netting only determines the true amount owed.

The Code does not contain a general netting safe harbour of the kind found in some other jurisdictions, and tribunals have approached set-off and netting claims case by case, with closer scrutiny of set-off between unrelated mutual debts. Outcomes depend on the agreement's wording and structure and on how the claim is presented.

In Practice

Counterparties relying on netting should be able to show a single master agreement with a netting clause that predates the insolvency, with a clear calculation of the net amount, and should file their claim in the process in case netting is disallowed.
