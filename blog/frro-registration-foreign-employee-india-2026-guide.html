FRRO Registration for a Foreign Employee in India: The 2026 Deadline Change

A foreign employee staying past 180 days must register with the FRRO. Since June 2026 the deadline falls before, not after, the 180 days end.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 14 November 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

A foreign national who takes up employment in India and expects to stay longer than 180 days has to register with the Foreigners Regional Registration Office, and since June 2026 that registration has to be finished before the 180 days run out, not within a grace period after. The earlier rule gave a foreign employee fourteen extra days once the 180-day mark passed; the Immigration and Foreigners (Amendment) Rules, 2026, notified by the Ministry of Home Affairs and published on 1 June 2026, removed that cushion.

This guide sets out who has to register, what actually changed in June 2026, how the registration itself is done for someone on an employment visa, and what an employer bringing a foreign hire into a Delhi, Noida or Kolkata office needs to build into its own onboarding calendar so the paperwork does not lapse quietly around the five or six month mark.

Who has to register, and under which law

The registration requirement now sits under the Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025, Act No. 13 of 2025, which received presidential assent on 4 April 2025 and commenced from 1 September 2025. Section 36 of that Act repealed and replaced four older statutes at a stroke, among them the Registration of Foreigners Act, 1939 and the Foreigners Act, 1946, both of which practitioners had worked with for decades. The new Act's own statement of purpose describes the repealed laws as pre-Constitution legislation shaped by the two World Wars, which is the reason given for consolidating entry control, registration, stay regulation and exit control into one framework instead of several overlapping ones.

A foreign national on an employment visa is squarely within this regime once the length of stay crosses 180 days, whether the visa itself was issued for a shorter period that gets extended or for a longer validity from the outset. The same rule extends to a visa valid for more than 180 days that is itself conditioned on no single stay exceeding 180 days, which catches some multiple-entry employment visas that a company might otherwise assume are exempt because the visa's headline validity looks longer than six months.

The employment visa itself

An employment visa is granted to a foreign national taking up a specific job with an Indian company, branch office or project office, and the categorisation turns on the nature of the role and the sponsoring entity rather than on nationality alone. Indian missions abroad have historically applied a minimum annual salary threshold before an employment visa is granted, with carve-outs reported for certain sectors and for ethnic cooks, translators and similar specialist roles, but the exact current figure and the list of exempt categories should be confirmed with the Ministry of Home Affairs or the relevant Indian mission at the time of application rather than assumed from an older notification, since these thresholds are revised from time to time without always being reported prominently.

The visa is granted for the specific employer and role named in the application, and a foreign employee who changes employer or role within India without the visa being amended or re-issued is not covered for that new engagement, whatever the original visa's remaining validity shows on its face. This catches secondments and intra-group transfers more often than a straightforward new hire, because the paperwork trail for a transfer between group entities is easy to treat as an internal formality when immigration law treats it as a fresh question.

What changed in June 2026

The Ministry of Home Affairs notified the change as G.S.R. 424(E) in the Gazette of India, Extraordinary, dated 1 June 2026, issued under Section 30 of the Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025. Under the position before this amendment, a foreign national whose stay crossed 180 days had fourteen days after that date to complete registration with the FRRO or the local Foreigners Registration Officer. The amendment removes that window: registration must now be completed before the 180 days are up, and extended registration beyond that point is reported to be granted only in emergent circumstances rather than as a routine courtesy.

Business Standard and a client alert from BDO both describe the change in these terms, and both tie it to the same gazette notification and the same 1 June 2026 commencement date, which is the strongest confirmation available for this guide. Some immigration advisory pages still describe the older fourteen-day grace period as current, which means a company checking its policy against an older bookmark or an un-updated vendor guide could easily miss the change; the safer practice is to treat any stay nearing six months as needing registration before, not after, day 180, and to confirm the current position with the FRRO portal or counsel rather than with a saved PDF from an earlier year.

How registration actually happens

Registration is done through the e-FRRO portal, with the application typically supported by the foreign national's passport, visa, a recent photograph, proof of Indian address, and, for an employment visa holder, an employer's letter confirming the engagement. The employer's human resources or compliance team usually drives this process in practice, since the foreign employee is rarely familiar with the Indian portal or paperwork on their own, and a company that treats FRRO registration as the individual's personal responsibility tends to find out about a lapse only when an exit formality or a visa extension gets stuck.

Separately, hotels, landlords and employers providing accommodation to a foreign national have reporting obligations of their own under the registration regime, commonly referred to by the older Form C terminology even as the underlying law has been recast; a company putting a new foreign hire into company-arranged housing should check that whoever manages that accommodation is actually filing this, since the obligation does not go away merely because the employee separately registers with the FRRO.

Consequences of missing the deadline

A foreign national who overstays the registration deadline risks being treated as an overstayer for enforcement purposes, which can affect a pending visa extension, a future visa application, and in a serious case the person's ability to re-enter India at all. For the employer, a foreign employee who cannot continue working because of an immigration lapse is a business problem quite apart from the individual's own difficulty, and it tends to surface at the worst possible time, such as during a client-facing posting or just before an overseas transfer.

The Act and its rules under the June 2026 amendment also introduced an online appeals mechanism for registration-related decisions, reported to require a decision within sixty days, which gives a foreign national or their employer a formal route to contest a refusal or a penalty rather than relying only on informal representations to the local office. Using that route, rather than simply reapplying and hoping the issue resolves itself, is the more reliable path once a registration has actually been refused or flagged.

What an employer should build into onboarding

A Delhi or Noida employer taking on its first few foreign hires, or a Kolkata office receiving a secondee from an overseas group company, should calendar the 180-day point from the date of arrival in India, not from the date the employment visa was granted or the date work actually started, since those dates can differ by weeks. The employer's immigration or HR tracker should flag the file well before that date, because the FRRO process, like most government portals, can take longer than expected around busy periods, and starting the application in the final days before expiry leaves no room for a document query or a portal glitch.

This sits alongside, rather than instead of, the other compliance questions a foreign employee's engagement raises, such as whether Provident Fund contributions apply to an international worker and whether the Prevention of Sexual Harassment framework and the applicable Shops and Establishments registration cover the employee's workplace in the same way as for an Indian national; those questions are addressed in this firm's separate guidance on international workers and establishment compliance and are not repeated here.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every foreign employee working in India need to register with the FRRO?

Only if the stay in India crosses 180 days, whether under one visa or under a visa whose terms limit any single stay to 180 days. A short business visit or assignment that ends before that point does not trigger FRRO registration, though other reporting obligations, such as hotel or landlord intimation, can still apply from the date of arrival.

What exactly changed for foreign employees in June 2026?

The fourteen-day grace period after the 180-day mark was removed. Under the Immigration and Foreigners (Amendment) Rules, 2026, notified on 1 June 2026, registration must be completed before the 180 days are up, and registering late is treated as a lapse rather than something cured within an automatic extra window.

Can a foreign employee register themselves, or must the employer do it?

The individual is legally responsible for registering, but in practice the employer's human resources or compliance team usually manages the e-FRRO application, since the employee is rarely familiar with the portal, the Indian address proof requirements or the employer confirmation letter the application needs.

Does a secondee moving between group companies in India need a fresh registration?

The visa and registration are tied to the specific employer and role named in the original application. A transfer between group entities, even within the same multinational, should be treated as a fresh immigration question rather than an internal formality, and the visa or its conditions may need to be amended before the new role begins.

What happens if a foreign employee misses the new registration deadline?

The person risks being treated as an overstayer, which can complicate a visa extension, a future application or re-entry into India. The 2026 rules also added an online appeals process for registration-related decisions, reported to require a response within sixty days, which gives a formal route to contest a refusal rather than relying on informal follow-up.
