Calculating Delay Compensation Under RERA Section 18: How the Interest Actually Works

A practical guide for a homebuyer or promoter working out what is actually owed under Section 18 of RERA once possession is delayed, distinct from this site's existing general RERA-complaint-filing and RERA-vs-consumer-forum guides, focused specifically on how the interest rate and computation period are determined.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 28 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice — see our disclaimer

Overview

A practical guide for a homebuyer or promoter working out what is actually owed under Section 18 of RERA once possession is delayed, distinct from this site's existing general RERA-complaint-filing and RERA-vs-consumer-forum guides, focused specifically on how the interest rate and computation period are determined.

What You Need to Know

  • Section 18 of RERA entitles an allottee, where a promoter fails to complete or hand over possession by the date stated in the agreement, to either withdraw from the project and claim a refund of the amount paid with interest, or to remain in the project and claim interest for every month of delay until possession
  • The prescribed interest rate under most states' RERA Rules is pegged to the State Bank of India's Marginal Cost of Funds-based Lending Rate (MCLR) plus an additional two percentage points, applying the MCLR rate as published on the first day of the relevant month rather than a rate fixed once at the start of the delay
  • Interest is calculated as simple interest, not compound interest, using the formula: (amount paid x annual rate x number of days of delay) / 365 — MahaRERA and other authorities have consistently rejected attempts to claim compound interest under Section 18
  • A materially more accurate (and usually higher) computation runs interest separately on each instalment from the date that specific instalment was actually paid, rather than applying a single rate to the cumulative total paid from the agreement date — allottees should ask their RERA authority to compute compensation instalment-wise rather than accepting a lump-sum approximation
  • The delay period runs from the promoter's committed possession date under the agreement (not any later, informally extended date the promoter may claim) until the date actual, habitable possession is offered — supported by a valid occupancy certificate — or, for a withdrawing allottee, until the refund is actually paid
  • Because the applicable MCLR changes over time, an allottee or promoter should not assume a single interest rate applies for the entire delay period — the computation should apply the rate notified for each relevant month across the full delay window, which is why a month-by-month calculation (or a purpose-built RERA delay-interest calculator) is more reliable than a single blended-rate estimate

Frequently Asked Questions

What interest rate applies to a Section 18 delayed-possession claim?

Most states' RERA Rules peg it to the State Bank of India's MCLR plus two percentage points, applying the rate as published on the first day of the relevant month rather than a single fixed rate for the whole delay.

Is Section 18 interest simple or compound?

Simple interest — MahaRERA and other authorities have consistently rejected attempts to claim compound interest under Section 18.

Why does calculating interest instalment-by-instalment matter?

Because interest runs separately on each instalment from the date it was actually paid, an instalment-wise computation is more accurate — and usually yields a higher recovery — than applying one rate to the cumulative amount from the agreement date.
