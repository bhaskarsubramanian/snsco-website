Filing for Divorce, Maintenance or Custody If You Live in Noida or Greater Noida: Which Court Hears It

Which court has jurisdiction over divorce, maintenance and custody for Noida and Greater Noida residents, and when a case can be transferred elsewhere.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 12 November 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

Where a divorce, maintenance or custody case can be filed is not a matter of personal preference, and getting it wrong can mean an otherwise sound petition being thrown out on jurisdiction alone, or being forced into a transfer application that delays the real dispute by months. For residents of Noida and Greater Noida, in Gautam Buddha Nagar district, the questions that decide this are the same across the country, but the answers depend on specific facts: where the marriage was solemnised, where the parties last lived together, where the respondent currently resides, and in a maintenance claim, which of several permitted venues the claimant chooses to use.

This guide works through those tests for divorce under the Hindu Marriage Act, maintenance under the current criminal law provision, and custody or guardianship proceedings, and explains when a case already filed elsewhere can be moved to Gautam Buddha Nagar, or away from it. For the underlying substantive questions once the forum is settled, see our guides on Mutual Consent Divorce, Section 13B and How Indian Courts Decide Child Custody.

The Family Court's exclusive hold once one is set up

The Family Courts Act, 1984 lets a State Government set up a Family Court for an area, and once that is done, Section 8 of the Act takes away the jurisdiction of the ordinary civil and criminal courts over the matters the Act assigns to the Family Court, including matrimonial relief, maintenance and guardianship or custody of children, within that area. Section 7 then gives the Family Court the powers of a district court or a magistrate, as the case may be, for those classes of case, so a litigant in an area with a functioning Family Court does not have a choice between the Family Court and the ordinary civil court for a matter the Act covers; the Family Court is the only forum.

For Gautam Buddha Nagar, this means a divorce, judicial separation, maintenance or custody matter that would otherwise go to the district judge or a magistrate instead goes to the Family Court serving the district, and a petition filed in the wrong forum, for instance before the ordinary civil court instead of the Family Court, can be objected to on that basis alone, independent of the merits.

Divorce and judicial separation: the Section 19 tests

Section 19 of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 lists the places where a petition for divorce, judicial separation or similar relief can be presented, and after the Family Courts Act came into force, a reference there to the district court is read as a reference to the Family Court for an area where one has been established. The listed venues are where the marriage was solemnised, where the respondent resides at the time of filing, where the parties last resided together, and, following a 2003 amendment, where the wife herself resides on the date of filing if she is the petitioner, which gives a wife bringing the petition a venue option the husband as petitioner does not have.

For a couple who married in Delhi but lived together in Noida before separating, with the respondent now residing elsewhere, the petitioner typically has a genuine choice among more than one of these venues, and picking the one that is procedurally most convenient, usually where the petitioner or the respondent currently lives, is ordinarily legitimate rather than forum shopping, provided the petition is candid about the underlying facts, since the statute itself builds in that choice rather than fixing a single mandatory venue.

Maintenance claims: a wider set of venues under the current law

Maintenance claims by a wife, children or parents are governed, for proceedings instituted from 1 July 2024, by Section 144 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, which replaced the earlier Section 125 of the Code of Criminal Procedure in substantially the same terms, with pending proceedings filed before that date continuing under the old provision. The venue provision, Section 145 of the new Sanhita, allows a claim to be filed where the respondent resides, where the wife resides, where the parties last resided together, or, as an addition over the earlier Section 126 CrPC, where the claimant parent resides, which widens the options specifically for a maintenance claim brought by an elderly parent against an adult child.

This set of venues is wider than the divorce venue list under Section 19 of the Hindu Marriage Act, and a claimant is not required to bring maintenance in the same court hearing a parallel divorce petition, though doing so is often more efficient and is commonly how maintenance is in fact claimed, as an application within the matrimonial proceeding itself rather than as a standalone filing.

Custody and guardianship: where the child actually is

Custody and guardianship proceedings for a minor, whether brought under the Guardians and Wards Act, 1890 or as an ancillary relief within a matrimonial case before the Family Court, generally turn on where the child ordinarily resides, not on where the parents are domiciled, married, or litigating other issues, which is why a child who has been living with one parent in Noida for some time is ordinarily the basis for Gautam Buddha Nagar jurisdiction over custody, even if the matrimonial petition itself proceeds elsewhere on one of the Section 19 venues.

Where custody and divorce are both in issue between the same parties, courts generally try to avoid the two proceeding on entirely different tracks in different cities, and a custody or guardianship application is often brought within the same Family Court hearing the matrimonial case where that is practical, with a separate Guardians and Wards petition reserved for situations where no matrimonial proceeding is pending or the child's residence genuinely points to a different place.

Transferring a case to or from Gautam Buddha Nagar

If a case is already pending in another court within Uttar Pradesh and a party wants it moved to the Family Court at Gautam Buddha Nagar, the application is made to the Allahabad High Court under Section 24 of the Code of Civil Procedure, which gives the High Court power to transfer a civil proceeding from one court to another within the state. Where the case to be moved is pending in a different state altogether, the application instead goes to the Supreme Court under Section 25 of the Code, which is the only authority that can transfer a proceeding across state lines.

Courts deciding such applications have repeatedly treated the wife's convenience as the central, though not the only, consideration, on the reasoning that she is typically less able to travel repeatedly for hearings, particularly where young children or limited independent income are involved, while stressing that this is a factor to be weighed rather than an automatic entitlement, and that the overall test remains whether the transfer serves the ends of justice on the facts of the particular case. A transfer is refused where the applicant appears to be seeking a more convenient forum for its own sake, without a genuine difficulty in attending the court where the case is currently pending.

Getting the forum right the first time

The safest approach for a Noida or Greater Noida resident is to work out, before filing, which of the available venues under Section 19 of the Hindu Marriage Act or Section 145 of the Sanhita the facts actually support, rather than assuming the district of current residence is automatically correct, since more than one venue can apply and the choice has real consequences for which Family Court hears the matter and how convenient the proceedings will be going forward.

A petition filed in a venue that later turns out not to be supported by the facts pleaded is vulnerable to a jurisdiction objection from the other side, which can consume months before the real dispute is even reached, so setting out the jurisdictional facts, such as where the parties last lived together or where the respondent currently resides, clearly and accurately in the petition itself is worth the care it takes.

Frequently Asked Questions

My spouse lives in Delhi and I live in Noida. Where can I file for divorce?

Section 19 of the Hindu Marriage Act lets you file where the marriage was solemnised, where the respondent resides, where you last lived together, or, if you are the wife, where you yourself reside when filing. If any of these points to Gautam Buddha Nagar, you can generally file there.

Can I get a maintenance case pending in another state transferred to Gautam Buddha Nagar?

A transfer across states needs an application to the Supreme Court under Section 25 of the Code of Civil Procedure, not to the Allahabad High Court. A transfer within Uttar Pradesh, from one UP court to the Family Court at Gautam Buddha Nagar, is instead made to the Allahabad High Court under Section 24.

Does the Gautam Buddha Nagar Family Court handle custody and maintenance together with divorce?

Yes, a Family Court exercising jurisdiction over a matrimonial matter can also deal with maintenance and custody as part of the same proceeding, and this is the more common and generally more efficient route than filing separate standalone applications in different courts.

We married outside Uttar Pradesh. Can I still file for divorce in Gautam Buddha Nagar?

Yes, provided one of the other venues under Section 19 applies, such as the respondent currently residing in Gautam Buddha Nagar, the parties having last resided together there, or, if you are the wife filing the petition, your own current residence being there.

Is there a separate Family Court in Gautam Buddha Nagar, or does a matrimonial case go to the ordinary civil court?

Once a Family Court is established for an area under the Family Courts Act, 1984, it takes over exclusive jurisdiction for matrimonial, maintenance and custody matters within that area, and the ordinary civil court no longer hears them. Confirm the current designated Family Court for Gautam Buddha Nagar before filing.

  2. Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, Section 19, including the clause (iii-a) added by the Marriage Laws (Amendment) Act, 2003 for a wife petitioner

  4. Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, Sections 24 and 25 (transfer of suits within a state, and across states)
