Live-In Relationships in India: Legal Status, Maintenance and Protection Under the Domestic Violence Act

A practical guide for couples in a live-in relationship, and for a partner facing abuse or seeking maintenance after separation, explaining when Indian law treats a live-in relationship as a 'relationship in the nature of marriage' entitled to protection, distinct from this site's existing DV Act step-by-step filing guide, which focuses on procedure rather than eligibility.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 28 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice — see our disclaimer

Overview

A practical guide for couples in a live-in relationship, and for a partner facing abuse or seeking maintenance after separation, explaining when Indian law treats a live-in relationship as a 'relationship in the nature of marriage' entitled to protection, distinct from this site's existing DV Act step-by-step filing guide, which focuses on procedure rather than eligibility.

What You Need to Know

  • Live-in relationships are not separately codified or licensed in India, but the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 recognises a 'domestic relationship' under Section 2(f) that expressly includes parties who live together in a 'relationship in the nature of marriage', extending DV Act protection beyond legally married couples
  • Courts have laid down guiding factors for when a live-in relationship qualifies as 'in the nature of marriage' for DV Act purposes, including a reasonably long period of cohabitation, shared household and finances, holding out to society as a couple, and the absence of a legal impediment such as a subsisting marriage of either partner to someone else
  • The Supreme Court in Chanmuniya v. Virendra Kumar Singh Kushwaha extended maintenance-type relief to women in live-in relationships resembling marriage, and courts have since read protection, residence and monetary relief under the DV Act as available to such a partner, on a footing broadly comparable to a legally wedded wife's remedies
  • Children born from a live-in relationship that qualifies as being 'in the nature of marriage' are generally treated as legitimate for inheritance and maintenance purposes, following the Supreme Court's broader line of cases protecting children from being penalised for their parents' relationship status
  • A partner seeking DV Act relief from a live-in relationship must still establish, on the facts, that the relationship met the 'in the nature of marriage' threshold — a casual, short-term or clearly non-marital cohabitation arrangement will not automatically qualify, so documentation of the relationship's duration and public/social acknowledgment matters
  • Courts have been careful to clarify that recognising live-in relationships for DV Act or maintenance purposes is not intended to legitimise concurrent adultery or bigamy-like arrangements — where one partner has a subsisting valid marriage to a third person, the live-in partner's claim to DV Act relief becomes considerably more contested

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Indian law recognise live-in relationships as equivalent to marriage?

Not automatically — but a live-in relationship that qualifies as a 'relationship in the nature of marriage' between consenting adults, particularly with a genuine intent to marry, can attract specific statutory protections.

Can a woman in a live-in relationship claim maintenance?

Yes, under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005, a woman in a domestic relationship 'in the nature of marriage' can seek maintenance and protection orders even without a legally solemnised marriage.

Does this protection extend to a criminal cruelty complaint too?

The Supreme Court has held Section 498A IPC's cruelty protection can extend to a live-in relationship functioning as a marriage with a genuine intent to marry, though it clarified this reasoning is confined to Section 498A and not automatically every matrimonial-law provision.
