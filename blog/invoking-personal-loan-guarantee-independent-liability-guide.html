Invoking a Personal Loan Guarantee: Why a Guarantor's Liability Is Independent of the Borrower's Defences

A guide to a guarantor's liability under a personal loan guarantee deed: why it is coextensive with the borrower's under the Indian Contract Act, when a guarantor is discharged, and what happens after a guarantor pays.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 30 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

Coextensive Liability Under Sections 128 to 140

Personal loan guarantees are a routine feature of MSME lending and retail credit: a family member, business partner or co-obligant signs a guarantee deed making them liable if the primary borrower defaults. Sections 128 to 140 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 govern this relationship, and Section 128 in particular establishes the central rule — a surety's liability is coextensive with that of the principal debtor, unless the contract of guarantee provides otherwise. In practice, this means a guarantor cannot generally escape liability merely because the borrower has defences, counterclaims or disputes of their own against the lender, unless the guarantee deed expressly limits the guarantor's exposure.

The Lender Need Not Sue the Borrower First

A guarantor is often surprised to learn that a lender can proceed directly against them without first suing, or even formally demanding payment from, the principal borrower. Courts have repeatedly held that unless the guarantee deed specifically requires the lender to exhaust remedies against the borrower before approaching the guarantor, the lender's right to invoke the guarantee arises on the borrower's default itself, independent of whether or how the lender pursues the borrower.

When a Guarantor Can Be Discharged

A guarantor's coextensive liability is not absolute. Under Sections 133 to 139, a guarantor can be discharged, in whole or in part, where the lender materially varies the terms of the loan without the guarantor's consent, releases or impairs security the guarantor was relying on, or otherwise acts in a manner inconsistent with the guarantor's rights under the guarantee. The extent of discharge depends on the specific variance and its effect on the guarantor's position, so a guarantor seeking to resist a demand should examine closely whether the underlying loan terms changed after the guarantee was executed.

Subrogation: The Guarantor's Right After Paying

A guarantor who pays the lender under the guarantee does not simply absorb the loss. By operation of law, the guarantor steps into the lender's shoes and is subrogated to all the rights the lender had against the borrower, including any security the lender held. This gives the paying guarantor a direct route to seek reimbursement from the borrower, rather than being left with only a general right of indemnity.

Interaction With SARFAESI and IBC Personal Guarantor Proceedings

Where the underlying loan is secured, a lender may also be able to pursue SARFAESI remedies against assets a guarantor has pledged as security, independent of a civil suit on the guarantee itself. Separately, Part III of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code allows a creditor to initiate a dedicated insolvency resolution process against a personal guarantor, a route that has become increasingly significant where a corporate borrower's own insolvency proceedings leave a shortfall. A guarantor should treat these as genuinely separate and cumulative exposure points, not alternative routes the lender must choose between.

Frequently Asked Questions

Must a lender sue the borrower before proceeding against the guarantor?

No. Under the Indian Contract Act, a guarantor's liability is coextensive with the borrower's unless the guarantee deed itself says otherwise, so a lender can proceed directly against the guarantor without first exhausting remedies against the borrower.

Can a guarantor be discharged if the lender changes the loan terms without consulting them?

A material variance in the loan terms made without the guarantor's consent, or the release of security the guarantor was relying on, can discharge the guarantor to the extent of that variance, depending on the specific facts and the guarantee deed's wording.

What can a guarantor do after paying the lender?

A guarantor who pays is subrogated to the lender's rights and remedies against the borrower, including any security the lender held, and can pursue the borrower for reimbursement.

Can a personal guarantor be pulled into IBC proceedings against the borrower?

Yes. Part III of the IBC allows a creditor to initiate a separate insolvency resolution process against a personal guarantor, and SARFAESI remedies may also be available against a guarantor's assets depending on the facts.
