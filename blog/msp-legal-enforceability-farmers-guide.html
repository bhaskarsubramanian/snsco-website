Is Minimum Support Price Legally Enforceable? What Farmers Can Actually Claim

MSP announcements make headlines every season, but is the floor price actually enforceable in court? Here is what the law currently says, and what remains pending.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 7 October 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice, see our disclaimer

MSP Is a Policy Price, Not a Statutory Right

Minimum Support Price is announced by the Government on the recommendation of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices, as a policy and administrative price-support mechanism. There is no dedicated statute making MSP a legally enforceable right, and government procurement agencies are not under a general statutory obligation to buy every farmer's produce at the announced MSP.

As matters currently stand, a farmer cannot sue to compel payment of MSP for produce sold in the open market to a private buyer below the announced price, because there is no law mandating that private buyers pay MSP or making the floor price legally binding on them.

Where Remedies Do Exist: Scheme-Specific Procurement

Where the government, or a designated procurement agency, does operate an active procurement scheme for a particular crop, such as paddy, wheat or cotton, farmers can seek remedies tied to that specific scheme's own procurement guidelines, for instance an unjustified rejection on quality grounds, or denial of access to a procurement centre, rather than a freestanding 'right to MSP'.

Separately, the Essential Commodities Act and state APMC or agricultural produce marketing laws regulate where and how produce can be sold and what licensing traders need. These regimes are distinct from, and do not themselves guarantee, a floor price.

The Pending Supreme Court Litigation

A batch of petitions is pending before the Supreme Court seeking a legally guaranteed MSP for all notified crops, computed on a cost-of-cultivation-plus-fifty-percent-profit formula based on the Swaminathan Commission Report, and the Court has sought the Centre's response. This remains an open, unresolved matter and should be tracked rather than treated as settled law.

For now, farmers and farmer producer organisations relying on a government procurement scheme should carefully document produce quality and procurement-centre interactions, since remedies currently run through that specific scheme's own grievance mechanism rather than a general MSP enforcement right.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a farmer sue a private trader for paying below MSP?

Not currently. There is no law making MSP binding on a private buyer, so a farmer cannot compel payment of the announced MSP for an open-market sale through a civil suit.

Is there a court case that could change this?

Yes. A batch of petitions seeking a legally guaranteed MSP on a cost-of-cultivation-plus-fifty-percent formula is pending before the Supreme Court, with the Centre's response sought. This remains unresolved.

What can a farmer do if a government procurement agency rejects produce on quality grounds?

The farmer can pursue a remedy under that specific procurement scheme's own grievance mechanism, rather than relying on a general right to MSP, which does not currently exist in statute.
