Is there an inheritance tax in India? Myth versus the actual tax position

India has had no inheritance or estate tax since 1985. Here is what heirs actually owe tax on when they inherit property, shares or a bank balance.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 20 November 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

India does not tax inheritance. There is no estate duty, no death tax and no separate charge on an heir simply for receiving property, money or shares from a deceased relative, whether by will or by intestate succession. That has been the position since 1985, and it remains the position as on this date, despite recurring public debate about bringing some form of it back.

What confuses people is that the absence of an inheritance tax does not mean the inherited asset is tax-free forever. Once it is in the heir's hands, ordinary income tax rules apply to whatever that asset earns or whatever it fetches on a later sale. The myth is that inheritance is untaxed; the fact is narrower: the transfer itself is untaxed, and the tax exposure begins only after that.

Why India has no inheritance tax today

India did have an estate duty once. The Estate Duty Act, 1953 imposed a duty on the principal value of a deceased person's estate, with rates that climbed steeply at the top end. The government withdrew it for deaths occurring on or after 16 March 1985, after Parliament passed the Estate Duty (Abolition/Repeal) Act. The reasons given at the time were practical rather than ideological: the duty generated very little revenue relative to the cost and litigation of administering it, and it sat awkwardly alongside wealth tax, which was also charging the same pool of assets from a different angle.

Since that repeal, no government has reintroduced a general inheritance or estate tax, though the idea resurfaces in policy discussion from time to time, usually tied to debates about wealth inequality. None of that discussion has produced a bill, and nothing currently pending changes the position for a family handling succession today. Advisers who tell clients to plan now in case such a tax arrives are speaking about a future possibility, not a present rule, and that distinction should be kept clear in any planning conversation.

What tax actually attaches to inherited property

The moment of inheritance itself triggers nothing under the Income Tax Act. Section 56(2)(x), which taxes certain gifts received without consideration, specifically excludes property received under a will or by way of inheritance from that charge. So a flat, a bank deposit, listed shares or gold received from a deceased parent's estate does not create taxable income for the heir at the point of transfer.

The tax exposure starts afterward. If the inherited asset produces income, such as rent from an inherited flat or interest on an inherited fixed deposit, that income is taxable in the heir's hands from the date they become entitled to it, under the ordinary heads of income. If the heir later sells the inherited asset, capital gains tax applies on that sale, and this is where families are most often caught by surprise, because they assume the original owner's old purchase price has simply vanished from the picture.

How the old purchase price carries forward

Section 49 of the Income Tax Act answers the question of what cost basis an heir uses when computing capital gains on inherited property. Where a capital asset became the assessee's property through succession, inheritance or a similar mode listed in the section, the cost of acquisition is deemed to be whatever the previous owner originally paid for it, not the market value on the date of inheritance. The holding period is also carried forward from the original owner, which matters for deciding whether the gain is short-term or long-term.

For assets the original owner acquired before 1 April 2001, the heir can usually opt to substitute the fair market value as on that date for the original cost, under the related valuation provision in the Act, rather than track down a decades-old purchase deed. Where indexation benefit is claimed, whether the indexation period runs from the original owner's purchase or only from the date of inheritance has been argued differently by the tax department and by courts in specific disputes, and that is a live enough question that it should be checked against the current position for the relevant assessment year rather than assumed either way.

Where this catches NRI and resident heirs differently

A resident heir who sells inherited property pays capital gains tax in the ordinary way, with the usual exemptions available if the proceeds are reinvested as the relevant sections require. An NRI heir selling the same inherited property faces an added layer: the buyer is generally required to withhold tax at source on the sale, calculated on the full sale value unless the NRI seller has obtained a lower or nil deduction certificate from the tax department in advance. Many NRI heirs discover this only when the buyer's lawyer insists on a much larger deduction than the eventual tax liability would justify, simply because no certificate was applied for ahead of the sale.

There is also a separate reporting angle for heirs who inherit foreign assets, or who are themselves resident in India and inherit assets located abroad. Disclosure obligations under India's foreign asset reporting framework can apply even though no tax is due on the inheritance itself, and missing that disclosure is a compliance problem distinct from any tax actually owed.

What about wealth tax, since estate duty and wealth tax are often confused

Families sometimes assume that even without an inheritance tax, holding a large inherited estate will attract some annual tax simply for being valuable, the way wealth tax once worked. That is also no longer the case. The Wealth Tax Act, 1957, which taxed the net value of specified assets held by an individual, Hindu undivided family or company each year, was withdrawn by the Finance Act, 2015, with effect from assessment year 2016-17, so the last year it actually applied was assessment year 2015-16. There is accordingly no annual tax today on simply holding an inherited house, inherited jewellery or an inherited investment portfolio.

What replaced that revenue, according to the government's own stated rationale at the time, was an increased surcharge on high-income taxpayers rather than a separate levy tied to asset value. For an heir, the practical takeaway is the same theme that runs through this whole area: receiving and holding wealth is not itself taxed in India, but income the wealth generates, and any eventual sale, is taxed in the ordinary way. An heir who confuses the old wealth tax exposure with today's rules may end up either filing a return that is no longer required or, worse, assuming some annual compliance step still applies when it does not.

Frequently Asked Questions

If I inherit my parent's flat, do I pay any tax at the time of inheritance?

No. Property received under a will or by intestate succession is specifically excluded from the income tax charge on gifts, so the transfer itself creates no tax liability. Tax only enters the picture later, if the flat earns rent while you hold it or when you eventually sell it.

What purchase price do I use if I sell a flat I inherited from my grandfather decades after he bought it?

Under Section 49, you use your grandfather's original cost of acquisition, not the value on the date you inherited it, and his holding period carries forward to you. If he bought it before 1 April 2001, you can generally use the fair market value as on that date instead of his original cost.

Will inheritance tax be brought back in India?

There is no inheritance or estate tax currently in force, and no bill pending that would reintroduce one. The topic resurfaces periodically in public policy debate, but planning should proceed on today's rules, not on a possible future change.

As an NRI, why did the buyer deduct so much tax when I sold inherited property in India?

Buyers are generally required to withhold tax on the full sale value when the seller is an NRI, unless the seller has already obtained a lower or nil deduction certificate from the tax department before the sale. Applying for that certificate in advance, rather than after the buyer has already deducted at the higher rate, avoids locking up funds until a refund is processed.

Do I have to pay wealth tax every year on a large inherited estate?

No. The Wealth Tax Act, 1957 was withdrawn by the Finance Act, 2015, with effect from assessment year 2016-17, so there is no annual tax today merely for holding inherited property, jewellery or investments. Only income the assets generate, and gains on an eventual sale, remain taxable.
