Can a Bank Use SARFAESI to Recover a Loan It Acquired From an NBFC? A Borrower's Guide

Explains, for borrowers and lenders alike, when a bank or ARC can invoke SARFAESI on a loan account it acquired or took over by assignment from an NBFC or other financier that was itself not a notified 'financial institution' under the Act at the time the loan was made: the issue the Supreme Court settled in Kotak Mahindra Bank Ltd. v. Trupti Sanjay Mehta & Ors. (2026 INSC 943).

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 28 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

Overview

Explains, for borrowers and lenders alike, when a bank or ARC can invoke SARFAESI on a loan account it acquired or took over by assignment from an NBFC or other financier that was itself not a notified 'financial institution' under the Act at the time the loan was made: the issue the Supreme Court settled in Kotak Mahindra Bank Ltd. v. Trupti Sanjay Mehta & Ors. (2026 INSC 943).

What You Need to Know

  • What makes an entity a 'bank' or 'financial institution' under Section 2(1)(c)/2(1)(m) of the SARFAESI Act, and why that status matters for who can invoke the Act
  • How assignment or securitisation of a loan account changes (or doesn't change) the borrower's rights and the enforcing entity's SARFAESI powers
  • The Kotak Mahindra Bank v. Trupti Sanjay Mehta ruling and why 'the loan wasn't originally with a SARFAESI-covered lender' is no longer a valid defence once a covered bank holds it
  • Practical steps for a borrower served with a Section 13(2) notice by an assignee bank: what to verify, what can still be challenged, and what can't
  • How this interacts with the borrower's separate right to challenge the underlying assignment itself (as opposed to the mode of recovery)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a bank use SARFAESI on a loan that originally came from an NBFC?

Yes: the Supreme Court has held that a bank covered by SARFAESI can enforce a secured debt it lawfully acquired by assignment, even if the originating NBFC was never itself a notified financial institution under the Act.

Does this mean any assignee can use SARFAESI on any acquired debt?

No: what matters is that the assignee itself is an entity covered by SARFAESI (such as a bank or a registered ARC) at the time it invokes the Act; an assignee outside the Act's coverage still cannot use SARFAESI merely by acquiring a secured debt.

What should a borrower check if a bank invokes SARFAESI on an assigned loan?

Whether the assignment itself was validly executed and the underlying security interest was properly created and perfected: the ruling doesn't cure a defect in how the original loan or security was made, only the originating lender's regulatory status.
