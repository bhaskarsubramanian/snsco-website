E-Commerce Return and Refund Policies: What Platforms Must Disclose Under the 2020 Rules

A practical guide for e-commerce sellers and platforms on the return and refund policy disclosures required under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, and a consumer's related rights.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 29 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice. See our disclaimer

Why Disclosure Matters

The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 require e-commerce entities to prominently display, at or before the point of purchase, information such as the return, refund, exchange, warranty and guarantee terms applicable to the goods or services offered, along with delivery and shipment timelines and the grievance redressal mechanism. Clear, accessible disclosure of these terms is treated as a baseline compliance requirement, not merely good practice.

What a Compliant Return Policy Should Cover

A return and refund policy should specify the applicable return window, the condition in which goods must be returned, whether the cost of return shipping is borne by the seller or the consumer, and the expected timeline for processing a refund once a return is received. Ambiguity in any of these (for instance, a vague 'refunds processed within a reasonable time' without a defined window) invites both consumer disputes and regulatory scrutiny.

Marketplace vs Inventory Models

The Rules distinguish between marketplace e-commerce entities, which facilitate transactions between third-party sellers and consumers, and inventory-based entities, which sell their own inventory directly. A marketplace entity has specific obligations around ensuring sellers make accurate representations about goods and services and maintaining a mechanism for consumers to notify grievances, even though it does not itself own the inventory being sold.

Consumer Rights When a Return Is Refused

Where a return or refund is refused in a manner inconsistent with the platform's own displayed policy, this discrepancy (between what was promised and what was actually honoured) is often central to a subsequent deficiency-in-service or unfair trade practice complaint, since the displayed policy itself sets the standard the platform is expected to meet.

Practical Compliance Steps for Sellers and Platforms

Sellers and platforms should ensure return and refund terms are displayed clearly at the point of purchase (not buried in a separate, hard-to-find policy page), kept consistent across the checkout flow and any post-purchase confirmation, and genuinely reflect what is operationally honoured: since a documented gap between the displayed policy and actual practice is the recurring fact pattern in consumer complaints of this kind.

Frequently Asked Questions

What must an e-commerce return policy disclose?

The applicable return window, condition requirements for the returned item, who bears return shipping cost, and the expected refund timeline, displayed prominently at or before purchase.

Is a marketplace platform responsible for a third-party seller's return policy?

A marketplace platform has specific obligations to ensure sellers make accurate representations and to maintain grievance redressal mechanisms, even though it does not own the inventory sold by third-party sellers.

What can I do if a platform refuses a return that its own policy allows?

A documented mismatch between the displayed policy and what is actually honoured is often central evidence in a deficiency-in-service or unfair trade practice complaint.

Do these disclosure rules apply to all online sellers?

The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 apply to e-commerce entities as defined under the Rules; the specific applicability to a given seller or platform depends on how it is structured and should be assessed against the Rules' definitions.
