Legal Heir Certificate in Delhi and Uttar Pradesh: How to Apply and What It Can Actually Do

Who can apply for a legal heir certificate in Delhi and UP, the documents and offices involved, and where a succession certificate takes over instead.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 3 November 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

A legal heir certificate is an administrative document issued by a revenue officer, usually the Tehsildar, SDM or Circle Officer, recording who the dead person's legal heirs are under the applicable personal law. It is meant for the smaller, relatively undisputed end of a person's affairs after death: pension and gratuity claims, provident fund withdrawal, employment dues, transferring a utility connection or ration card, and releasing a modest bank balance where there is no nominee and no dispute among the heirs. It does not, by itself, settle who owns what, and it is not the same thing as a succession certificate, which is a court order and carries far more legal weight.

This guide covers the domestic application process in Delhi and Uttar Pradesh, including Noida and Ghaziabad, for someone actually dealing with a death in the family and needing this specific certificate. If the estate involves an NRI heir, foreign assets, or a genuine dispute between heirs, the choice between a legal heir certificate, a succession certificate, probate and letters of administration is covered in more depth in our separate guides on those routes, linked below.

What this certificate actually proves, and what it does not

A legal heir certificate lists the surviving spouse, children, and, in the absence of direct heirs, parents or siblings, as the people entitled to step into the deceased's shoes for the specific purposes the certificate is used for. It is issued relatively quickly by a revenue official on the basis of an affidavit and supporting documents, without the kind of notice to all interested parties, or judicial scrutiny of competing claims, that a court applies before granting a succession certificate.

That speed comes at a cost. The certificate is not conclusive proof of who actually inherited what, it does not by itself transfer title to immovable property, and it does not compel a bank or company to hand over funds or securities above whatever internal threshold that institution applies. It is better thought of as a practical shortcut for the kind of claim that no one is likely to fight over, not as a substitute for a will, a succession certificate, or a proper partition where there is real property or real money at stake.

Applying in Delhi

In Delhi, the application is made to the Sub-Divisional Magistrate or Tehsildar having jurisdiction over the area where the deceased ordinarily resided, either through the Delhi e-District portal or by submitting a physical application with supporting documents at that office. Sources differ on how far the fully online route has matured for this particular certificate in Delhi, so it is worth checking the current service list on the portal, or asking the SDM office directly, before assuming the entire process can be completed without an in-person visit.

The documents commonly asked for are the deceased's death certificate, identity and address proof of the applicant and the other heirs, documents showing the relationship to the deceased such as a birth certificate, marriage certificate or ration card, and a self-declaration affidavit on non-judicial stamp paper naming every surviving legal heir. Processing is commonly quoted at around two to four weeks, though this is not a statutory guarantee and genuinely varies by how busy the particular office is and whether any heir's claim needs further verification.

Applying in Uttar Pradesh, including Noida and Ghaziabad

The equivalent route in Uttar Pradesh is the state's e-District portal, where the legal heir certificate sits under the Revenue Department's list of services; some local guides also refer to a separate e-Sathi portal for citizen services in UP, so it is worth confirming which portal is currently being used for this certificate in the specific district before starting an application. The offline alternative is to apply directly at the Tehsildar's office covering the area where the deceased last resided, which remains the practical route in many smaller towns regardless of what is technically available online.

The document list mirrors Delhi's: death certificate, identity proof, relationship proof, a stamped self-declaration affidavit, and usually passport-size photographs of the applicant. Fees for this certificate are nominal everywhere, but the exact amount is set locally and reported figures vary enough between sources that no single number can be stated reliably here; treat it as a small fee rather than a meaningful cost factor, and confirm the current figure, along with the stamp paper value needed for the affidavit, at the tehsil counter or on the portal.

Why your bank may still ask for more than this certificate

Banks are generally willing to act on a legal heir certificate for smaller balances, pension accounts, or accounts with no surviving nominee, but almost every bank sets its own internal rupee threshold above which it will insist on a succession certificate, letters of administration, or, in some cases, an indemnity bond with sureties instead of, or in addition to, the certificate. This threshold is a matter of each bank's own risk policy, not a rule fixed by any statute, so two banks can treat the exact same certificate and the exact same balance differently.

The practical point is to ask the specific bank, insurer or company what it will actually accept before investing time in an application. A legal heir certificate obtained for a small savings account is unlikely to be rejected, but the same certificate presented against a large fixed deposit, a demat account holding shares, or a disputed joint account is very likely to be met with a request for a succession certificate instead, at which point the entire process effectively has to restart through the civil court.

Legal heir certificate against succession certificate

The two documents come from different authorities and carry different weight. A legal heir certificate is issued by a revenue officer under the state's own administrative procedure. A succession certificate is granted by a civil court under Part X of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, on a petition under Section 372 filed before the district court having jurisdiction over the place where the deceased ordinarily resided, and it specifically authorises the holder to collect the deceased's debts and securities, giving anyone who pays the certificate holder in good faith a measure of legal protection that a legal heir certificate does not provide.

In rough practical terms, a legal heir certificate tends to be enough for pension, gratuity, provident fund, small bank balances and routine transfers of utility connections, while a succession certificate becomes necessary for larger or disputed bank and demat holdings, debts owed to the deceased, and securities, and immovable property disputes typically need to be resolved through mutation records, a partition suit, or probate of a will rather than either of these two documents alone. Our separate guides on succession certificates, probate and letters of administration, and on how nominees and legal heirs are actually treated by banks, go into the mechanics of the court-based route and the bank's side of this question in more detail.

Where this goes wrong in practice

The most common mistake is applying in the wrong jurisdiction, at an office near where the applicant lives rather than where the deceased actually resided, which usually just causes delay once the office realises the file does not belong with it. The second is leaving an heir off the affidavit, deliberately or by oversight, which can be challenged later by the excluded heir, can lead to the certificate being revoked or amended, and in a contested family can escalate into a civil suit that takes far longer than simply including everyone's name in the first application.

A third, quieter mistake is treating the certificate as settling a property dispute once it has been issued. It generally is not read that way by a court, and a family that uses a legal heir certificate as the basis for dividing immovable property, rather than a registered partition deed or a court decree, often finds the question reopened years later when one heir's share turns out to be worth disputing. Checking with the specific bank, employer or authority about what they will accept, before applying, remains the single most useful thing an applicant can do to avoid doing this twice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a legal heir certificate the same as a succession certificate?

No. A legal heir certificate is issued administratively by a revenue officer and mainly supports smaller, undisputed claims. A succession certificate is granted by a civil court under the Indian Succession Act, 1925 and specifically authorises the holder to collect the deceased's debts and securities, with legal protection for anyone who pays the certificate holder in good faith.

How long does it take to get a legal heir certificate in Delhi or Uttar Pradesh?

Commonly quoted at around two to four weeks, but this is not a fixed statutory timeline. It depends on the specific tehsil or SDM office, whether the application is complete, and whether any heir's claim needs further verification, so treat any quoted figure as indicative rather than guaranteed.

Can a legal heir certificate transfer a house or land into my name?

Not by itself. It is not treated as conclusive proof of title, and immovable property is generally dealt with through mutation records, a registered partition, probate of a will, or a civil decree. Relying on the certificate alone for property division often leaves the underlying title question unresolved and open to a later challenge.

What documents are usually needed?

The deceased's death certificate, identity and address proof of the applicant and other heirs, documents showing relationship to the deceased such as a birth or marriage certificate, and a self-declaration affidavit on non-judicial stamp paper naming all surviving heirs, along with passport-size photographs in most offices.

What if my bank refuses to accept a legal heir certificate for a large account?

That is common once the balance crosses the bank's own internal threshold, which is a matter of that bank's policy rather than law. In that situation, a succession certificate, letters of administration, or an indemnity arrangement the bank specifies is usually required instead, so it is worth checking the bank's own requirement before applying for either certificate.
