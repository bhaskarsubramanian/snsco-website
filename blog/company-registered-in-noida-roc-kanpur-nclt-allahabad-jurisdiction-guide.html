A Company Registered in Noida: Which ROC, NCLT Bench and High Court Have Jurisdiction

How the Registrar of Companies, the NCLT bench and the High Court are each worked out for a company with its registered office in Noida or Greater Noida.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 12 November 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

A company's registered office decides far more than where its annual return gets filed. It fixes which Registrar of Companies processes its filings, which bench of the National Company Law Tribunal hears a winding up, oppression or insolvency petition against it, and which High Court sits over that bench on appeal and over any writ or criminal proceeding connected with the company. For a company whose registered office is in Noida or Greater Noida, that chain runs through the Registrar of Companies at Kanpur or the newer Noida office, the NCLT's Allahabad Bench, and the Allahabad High Court, but each link has its own test and its own exceptions, and getting one wrong can mean a petition filed in the wrong forum or a limitation point lost while the error is corrected.

This guide sets out how each of those three jurisdictions is actually determined for a Noida-registered company, what happens when the registered office shifts, and where a shareholder, creditor or director most often gets the forum wrong. Our guide on Jurisdiction, Mediation and the Allahabad High Court Route for Commercial Litigation in Noida and Greater Noida covers civil suit jurisdiction for business disputes, which runs on a different test, and is worth reading alongside this one if the dispute could go either way.

Three different jurisdictions, three different tests

The Registrar of Companies is an administrative office under the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, and its jurisdiction over a company is fixed purely by the state or region where the company's registered office sits, as recorded in the certificate of incorporation and the MCA's own records. The NCLT is a judicial tribunal, and its benches are allotted territorial jurisdiction by gazette notification issued under the National Company Law Tribunal Rules, 2016, with the notified area again tracking the company's registered office for an ordinary company petition, whether that petition is for oppression and mismanagement under Sections 241 and 242 of the Companies Act, 2013, a tribunal-route winding up, or a corporate insolvency resolution process under Section 60(1) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.

The High Court's role is narrower than people often assume. It is not a first-instance forum for company petitions, which go to the NCLT, and appeals from NCLT orders go to the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, not to the High Court, with a further appeal to the Supreme Court on a point of law. The Allahabad High Court becomes relevant for a Noida company mainly for writ proceedings against the Registrar or the Ministry, for the exercise of its supervisory jurisdiction under Article 227 over the Tribunal in limited circumstances, and for criminal proceedings and appeals arising out of offences under the Companies Act, which are tried before courts designated for the State and sit within the High Court's ordinary appellate hierarchy.

Which Registrar: Kanpur, or the newer Noida office

For years, a single Registrar of Companies at Kanpur handled every company and LLP filing for the whole of Uttar Pradesh. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs has since split that charge: a notification issued in October 2025 carved out a separate Registrar of Companies, Noida, to cover the state's westernmost districts, including Gautam Buddha Nagar, out of what had been the Kanpur Registrar's charge. The date on which that carve-out actually took effect was pushed back more than once by later notifications, so a company incorporated before the split, with its certificate still showing Kanpur, should not assume that remains its Registrar today.

The practical point for a Noida company is to check its current jurisdiction on the Ministry's own portal before filing anything that depends on knowing the correct Registrar, rather than relying on the office named on an older incorporation certificate or on a filing made a few years ago. A wrongly addressed filing is not usually fatal, since the MCA's systems route electronic filings centrally, but a physical communication, an inspection notice, or a prosecution sanction tied to a specific Registrar's office is a different matter, and getting the office wrong there can create avoidable delay.

Which NCLT bench: Allahabad, and what it actually decides

The NCLT's Allahabad Bench, sitting at Prayagraj, hears company petitions for companies whose registered office is in Uttar Pradesh, and insolvency filings from the same Bench for corporate debtors based in the state follow the same registered-office test under Section 60(1) of the Code. Reported orders of the Allahabad Bench routinely list corporate debtors with registered offices in Noida and Greater Noida sectors, which confirms that a Gautam Buddha Nagar registered office does not pull a company petition or an insolvency filing into Delhi or any other bench merely because the company's real operations, promoters, or the dispute's subject matter happen to be there.

This is the point petitioners most often get wrong. The NCLT Rules do not recognise a cause-of-action test of the kind that governs civil suits or writ petitions. A shareholder aggrieved by conduct that took place at a factory in another state, or a creditor whose loan was disbursed from a branch elsewhere, still files at the Bench with jurisdiction over the company's registered office, not the Bench most convenient to the petitioner or closest to where the underlying events occurred. The one route out of this is Rule 16(d) of the NCLT Rules, 2016, which lets the NCLT President transfer a case between benches in appropriate circumstances, but that is an administrative transfer power invoked after filing, not a basis for choosing the forum in the first place.

Shifting the registered office: what changes, and what does not

A company can move its registered office within the same state by a board and, where required, shareholder resolution followed by filing with the Registrar. Moving it across state lines is a bigger step: Section 13 of the Companies Act, 2013 requires alteration of the memorandum of association, and the approval for an interstate shift, which was originally a Central Government power, now sits with the Regional Director of the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, exercised under Rule 30 of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014, after notice to creditors, debenture holders and other affected parties and an opportunity for them to object.

A shift changes which Registrar and which NCLT bench have jurisdiction going forward, but it does not retrospectively undo jurisdiction that a tribunal already had over a pending matter, and a shift made after a petition is filed, particularly one timed to frustrate an ongoing oppression or insolvency proceeding, invites the argument that the move itself was oppressive or an abuse of process, a point tribunals have been willing to examine closely rather than treat the new registered office as automatically decisive.

Where the Allahabad High Court actually comes in

A director, shareholder or company dealing with the Registrar's refusal to accept a filing, a show-cause notice from the Registrar, or a prosecution sanctioned under the Companies Act for a Noida-registered company has to look to the Allahabad High Court if a writ or an appeal in that chain becomes necessary, since that is the High Court exercising jurisdiction over Uttar Pradesh. The same applies to an application invoking the High Court's supervisory jurisdiction under Article 227 against an NCLT order, though courts have repeatedly cautioned that this is not a substitute for the statutory appeal to the NCLAT and is entertained only in a narrow band of cases involving a jurisdictional error or a gross failure of procedure.

What the Allahabad High Court is not, for a Noida company, is an appellate forum for an NCLT order on the merits. That route runs to the NCLAT under Section 421 of the Companies Act, and from the NCLAT to the Supreme Court under Section 423, bypassing the High Court entirely. Parties who file an ordinary appeal or a writ at Allahabad against a substantive NCLT order, instead of going to the NCLAT, generally lose time they cannot recover, since the limitation period for the NCLAT appeal keeps running while the wrong remedy is pursued.

Common mistakes on jurisdiction, and how to avoid them

The most frequent error is treating the company's corporate or administrative office, where its directors actually sit and its business is run, as the relevant address for a tribunal filing, when the only address that matters is the registered office on record with the Registrar. A company that operates from Delhi or Gurugram but keeps its registered office in Noida for historical reasons remains an Allahabad Bench company for tribunal purposes, whatever its day-to-day presence elsewhere.

The second common error is assuming that because a related recovery or insolvency matter against the same group is pending in Delhi or Mumbai, a fresh petition against the Noida company can simply be filed there too for convenience. Each corporate debtor's registered office is assessed on its own, and consolidation of related proceedings across benches, where it happens at all, is done through the Rule 16(d) transfer mechanism on a reasoned application, not by filing in whichever forum already has the other matter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the new Registrar of Companies, Noida replace Kanpur for a company already incorporated with Kanpur as its Registrar?

The 2025 notification carved Noida and the surrounding western districts out of the Kanpur Registrar's charge, so an existing Gautam Buddha Nagar company can fall under the new office without filing anything itself. Check the current jurisdiction on the Ministry of Corporate Affairs portal rather than assuming the Registrar named on an older certificate still applies.

Can a shareholder choose to file an oppression petition in a bench other than Allahabad if it is more convenient?

No. NCLT territorial jurisdiction for a company petition follows the company's registered office, not the petitioner's convenience or where the disputed conduct occurred. A petitioner based elsewhere still files at the bench covering the registered office, here Allahabad, unless a transfer is separately granted.

If a Noida company is both facing an insolvency petition and an oppression petition, do they go to different benches?

Both an insolvency petition under the Code and an oppression petition under Sections 241 and 242 of the Companies Act are decided by the bench covering the company's registered office, so for a Noida-registered company both ordinarily go to the Allahabad Bench, though they remain separate proceedings heard on their own facts.

Does shifting the registered office during a pending NCLT case change which bench hears it?

A shift changes jurisdiction for matters filed afterward, but it does not automatically remove a tribunal's jurisdiction over a case already pending before it. A shift made after filing, especially one timed to sidestep a pending proceeding, can itself be challenged before the same tribunal as an abuse of process.

The company's registered office is in Noida but its real business is run from Delhi. Which High Court hears a writ against the Registrar?

The Allahabad High Court, since Registrar and Ministry jurisdiction, and the High Court exercising supervisory authority over related proceedings, both track the registered office recorded with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, not the location of the company's actual business activity.
