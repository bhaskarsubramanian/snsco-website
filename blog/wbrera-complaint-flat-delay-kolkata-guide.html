A Kolkata Builder Has Delayed Possession: Filing a WBRERA Complaint and Claiming Interest

How a Kolkata homebuyer complains to WBRERA over delayed possession, claims interest under RERA Section 18, and picks RERA or the consumer forum.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 12 November 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

A homebuyer in Kolkata whose builder has missed the promised possession date has a direct remedy under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016: a complaint to the West Bengal Real Estate Regulatory Authority, usually called WBRERA, asking either for a refund with interest or for interest for the period of delay while keeping the flat. Section 18 of the Act creates this right, and the Supreme Court has confirmed that the Authority itself has jurisdiction to order a refund with interest as part of the regulatory mechanism the Act creates. What complicates a West Bengal complaint slightly, compared with some other states, is that the state's own regulatory history is more recent, since an earlier state law covering the same ground was struck down only in 2021.

This guide works through how WBRERA complaints are filed and decided, how delay interest is calculated, when the consumer forum is the better forum instead, and what changed for West Bengal homebuyers because of the 2021 Supreme Court ruling. Our guide on RERA or Consumer Forum for Builder Disputes covers the general version of that forum choice and is worth reading alongside this one.

Why West Bengal's RERA history is more recent than other states

West Bengal initially regulated real estate under its own West Bengal Housing Industry Regulation Act, 2017, framed around the same subject the central RERA already covered. The Supreme Court struck that state law down on 4 May 2021, holding it unconstitutional and repugnant to the central Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, since the field was already occupied by Parliament's legislation and the state law conflicted with it. The bench made clear the decision would not invalidate registrations, allotments and transactions already completed under the state law, and separately held that striking the 2017 Act down did not revive an older 1993 state law, which had itself been impliedly repealed once RERA came into force.

Following that ruling, the West Bengal government framed the West Bengal Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Rules, 2021 to implement the central Act, and the state set up its Appellate Tribunal shortly after. For a Kolkata buyer today this means WBRERA operates under the national Act and the 2021 state rules rather than any separate state statute, and a complaint should be framed by reference to the central Act's sections, with the state rules filling in procedural detail such as forms and fees.

What the complaint actually has to establish

A complaint under Section 18 requires the complainant to show that the promoter failed to complete the project, or hand over possession, by the date stated in the agreement for sale, and that no valid extension recognised under the Act or the registration applies. Where the project was registered with WBRERA, the registered completion date is usually the reference point; where possession was separately agreed in the sale agreement, this can itself become a contested issue, and a buyer should check both documents rather than rely on memory of what a sales executive said at booking.

Under Section 18, a buyer who no longer wants the flat can seek a full refund of the amount paid together with interest and compensation; a buyer who wants to keep the flat can instead seek interest for every month of delay until possession is actually handed over. The Supreme Court's 2021 decision in Newtech Promoters and Developers v. State of U.P. confirmed that the Authority has jurisdiction to order a refund with interest itself, and a separate, later Supreme Court ruling has clarified that while the Authority has this exclusive power to direct a refund, a claim limited to compensation alone under Sections 12, 14, 18 and 19 is decided by the Adjudicating Officer. A Kolkata complainant should frame the relief sought carefully with this division in mind before choosing where to file.

How delay interest is actually calculated

Interest for delay is pegged, under the Act, to a prescribed rate that most state rules fix by reference to the State Bank of India's highest marginal cost of lending rate plus an additional margin, with promoters obliged to pay a buyer delay interest at broadly the rate the buyer would owe the promoter for a default under the agreement. This reflects the Act's intent to put both sides on comparable footing rather than let a one-sided agreement clause fix a token penalty for the builder's own delay. Where the agreement for sale fixes an unusually low rate payable by the promoter for delay, WBRERA and its Appellate Tribunal can disregard that clause in favour of the statutory formula, since the remedy the Act creates is not limited by a contract term designed to defeat it.

This interest ordinarily runs from the date the builder was supposed to hand over possession until possession is actually offered or handed over, and an authority will generally expect the offer of possession to be genuine, backed by an occupancy certificate and a flat that is actually ready, not a token letter sent to stop the interest clock while construction continues. A buyer who accepted a flawed or premature possession offer and wants to argue the delay continued should be ready to show, with photographs, correspondence or an inspection report, why the offer did not actually amount to real possession.

The procedure: filing, hearing and the Authority's powers

A complaint is filed with either the Authority or the Adjudicating Officer depending on the relief sought, in the prescribed form together with the fee WBRERA's rules specify, along with copies of the agreement for sale, payment receipts and any correspondence about the delay. Section 71(2) sets a target of sixty days from receipt of the application for the Adjudicating Officer to dispose of a compensation application, recording reasons in writing where that target is missed, and the Authority is expected to work to a broadly similar benchmark for complaints filed before it; in practice, both forums can take longer given case volumes, so a complainant should treat the figure as a target rather than a guarantee.

Orders of the Authority or the Adjudicating Officer can be appealed to the state's Real Estate Appellate Tribunal, and from there a further appeal lies to the High Court only on specified grounds. The Supreme Court has upheld the pre-deposit condition a promoter must satisfy to file its own appeal against an order, which exists precisely to stop a builder from using an appeal simply to delay paying what has already been ordered. A buyer who has obtained a favourable order and is not paid can apply to have the amount recovered as arrears of land revenue, a meaningfully different, and often faster, route than filing a fresh civil suit to execute the order.

RERA or the consumer forum: which to choose for a Kolkata flat

A delayed-possession claim can often be brought before WBRERA or before the consumer forum under the Consumer Protection Act, since the Supreme Court has held that RERA does not oust consumer forum jurisdiction for homebuyers, who remain consumers for this purpose. The practical difference is usually speed and remedy: WBRERA and its Tribunal are built specifically around real estate disputes and tend to have more developer-specific experience and enforcement tools such as recovery as arrears of land revenue, while the consumer forum can also award compensation for mental harassment and deficiency in service in a way RERA orders do not typically frame themselves around.

Once a buyer elects one forum and it proceeds to a decision on the merits, switching to the other forum for the same relief afterward is generally not available, since the Supreme Court has treated an election of remedy here as binding once substantively pursued. Our guide on RERA or Consumer Forum for Builder Disputes sets out this election point and the comparative advantages of each forum, and it is worth reading before filing rather than after a hearing has already begun.

What a Kolkata buyer should have ready before filing

The agreement for sale, the registered project details including the registered completion date, every payment receipt, and any written communication about handover should be assembled before drafting the complaint, since WBRERA proceedings move on documents far more than oral argument. Where the flat has already been informally occupied under some arrangement with the builder pending a formal handover, that fact needs to be disclosed honestly rather than glossed over, because an authority that discovers it later tends to treat the omission as relevant to the buyer's credibility on the rest of the claim.

Where multiple buyers in the same project face the same delay, individually filed but similarly drafted complaints are usually more reliable than one combined petition, unless the Authority's rules specifically allow a joint complaint, since each buyer's payment schedule and loss differ even where the underlying project delay is common to all of them. Keeping a simple running note of every email, site visit and phone call with the builder's sales or possession team, dated as they happen rather than reconstructed later, tends to matter more at the hearing than buyers expect, since an authority weighing whether an offer of possession was genuine often looks closely at exactly when the builder first represented the flat as ready and how the buyer actually responded to that representation at the time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I file a WBRERA complaint if my flat was not booked directly from the builder but from an earlier buyer?

Generally yes. A subsequent allottee who has been validly assigned the original allotment steps into the earlier buyer's rights and obligations under the agreement, including the right to claim delay interest from the date the original timeline was missed. The documentation needed to prove a valid assignment should be checked carefully before filing.

Does WBRERA or the consumer forum move faster for a delay claim?

Neither forum guarantees a fixed timeline in practice, though the Act envisages a sixty-day disposal target before the Adjudicating Officer. The practical difference is usually that RERA forums are more familiar with project-specific evidence such as registration timelines, while the consumer forum can also award compensation for harassment.

What happens if the builder offers possession but the flat still has major defects?

An offer of possession that is not backed by a genuinely ready flat and a valid occupancy certificate is unlikely to be treated as stopping the delay interest clock. Structural or major defects discovered after taking possession can separately be pursued under the Act's defect liability provisions within the period the Act allows.

Can I get a refund if I no longer want the flat because of the delay?

Yes, Section 18 allows a buyer to seek a full refund of the amount paid, with interest and compensation, instead of continuing to wait for possession, provided the promoter has in fact defaulted on the agreed or registered timeline without a recognised extension.

Is there a time limit for filing a WBRERA complaint over delayed possession?

The Act does not fix a specific limitation period for a Section 18 complaint the way a civil suit is time-barred, but authorities and tribunals in other states have applied general limitation principles to claims made after a long, unexplained gap. A buyer should not assume an indefinite window and should file once the delay and the builder's default are clear.
