Is an Unstamped Arbitration Agreement Still Valid? What NN Global Actually Decided

A guide to the current legal position on unstamped and insufficiently stamped arbitration agreements in India, following the seven-judge Constitution Bench ruling in NN Global Mercantile v. Indo Unique Flame.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 5 June 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice — see our disclaimer

The Question That Divided the Courts for Years

For several years, Indian courts wrestled with a deceptively simple-sounding question: if a commercial contract containing an arbitration clause is unstamped or insufficiently stamped under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, is the arbitration clause within it enforceable — or does the stamping defect render the entire document, arbitration clause included, void and unenforceable until the deficient duty and penalty are paid? Different benches of the Supreme Court reached different conclusions over time, creating genuine uncertainty for parties trying to invoke arbitration on a contract that had not been properly stamped.

The Seven-Judge Bench Resolution

A seven-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court in NN Global Mercantile Pvt. Ltd. v. Indo Unique Flame Ltd. resolved the conflict, holding that an arbitration agreement, even where it is contained within an unstamped or insufficiently stamped underlying contract, is not rendered void or unenforceable for that reason at the threshold stage. The Bench held that the arbitration agreement is treated as a separate, severable agreement from the main contract under the doctrine of separability, and that the stamping objection concerns the enforceability of the main contract, not the existence of the arbitration clause.

The practical effect is that a court hearing a Section 11 application for appointment of an arbitrator, or a Section 8 application for reference to arbitration, is not required to first examine and rule on the stamping objection — that question is left to the arbitral tribunal to examine once appointed.

What Happens to the Stamp Duty Question Then?

The stamping issue does not disappear — it is simply relocated. Once the tribunal is constituted, it is expected to examine the stamping question as a preliminary matter, and where the underlying contract is found to be insufficiently stamped, the tribunal (or the parties, as directed) typically needs to have the document impounded and the deficient duty, along with any applicable penalty, paid before the tribunal proceeds further with the substantive merits of the dispute. Parties should factor this preliminary step into their expectations for the overall timeline of the arbitration.

Practical Guidance

The safest course remains to stamp commercial contracts adequately at the time of execution, avoiding the stamping question altogether. Where a contract is discovered to be insufficiently stamped only after a dispute has arisen, a party can still proceed to invoke arbitration — the stamping defect is not, under the current position, a bar to getting the arbitration underway — but should be prepared for the tribunal to address the stamp duty issue as an early preliminary matter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an unstamped arbitration agreement mean I cannot arbitrate my dispute?

No — following NN Global, an unstamped or insufficiently stamped underlying contract does not, by itself, prevent a court from appointing an arbitrator or referring the dispute to arbitration.

Who decides the stamp duty question if not the Section 11 court?

The arbitral tribunal, once constituted, typically examines and decides the stamping issue as a preliminary matter before proceeding to the merits.

Can the underlying contract still be impounded for insufficient stamping?

Yes — the stamping defect itself does not disappear; it is addressed by the tribunal, which can direct impounding and payment of the deficient duty and penalty as a preliminary step.

Does this ruling apply to all types of agreements, or just arbitration clauses in commercial contracts?

The ruling specifically addresses the relationship between the Stamp Act and an arbitration clause contained within a commercial contract; parties should still ensure other categories of documents are properly stamped for their own independent legal purposes.
