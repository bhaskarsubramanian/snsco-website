Visitation Rights and Parenting Plans in India: A Practical Guide for Separated Parents

A practical guide to how Indian family courts structure visitation rights and parenting arrangements for non-custodial parents after separation or divorce.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 29 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice — see our disclaimer

Overview

A practical guide to how Indian family courts structure visitation rights and parenting arrangements for non-custodial parents after separation or divorce.

What You Need to Know

  • Visitation rights allow a non-custodial parent to maintain a meaningful relationship with their child, and are treated by courts as flowing from the child's own welfare and right to both parents, not merely as an entitlement of the parent
  • A parenting plan or visitation schedule typically specifies designated days, weekends, holidays and vacation periods for the non-custodial parent, and can include provisions for phone or video calls between in-person visits
  • Courts increasingly incorporate structured virtual visitation (video calls) as a supplement to physical visitation, particularly where parents live in different cities, to preserve regular contact
  • Visitation can be restricted or supervised where a court has concrete concerns about a child's safety, but is rarely denied outright absent serious, specific findings, since courts start from the premise that contact with both parents ordinarily benefits the child
  • Parenting plans agreed between parties, whether as part of a mutual consent divorce settlement or otherwise, are generally recorded by the court and can be enforced or modified through the family court if circumstances change or a parent does not comply

Frequently Asked Questions

Can visitation rights be denied altogether?

Only in limited circumstances where the court finds specific evidence that contact would harm the child; the general approach favours structured, if necessary supervised, contact rather than outright denial.

Can a parenting plan be changed after it is fixed?

Yes — either parent can approach the family court to modify visitation or custody arrangements where there has been a material change in circumstances affecting the child's welfare.

Are virtual visitation arrangements legally recognised?

Yes, courts routinely include structured video-call or phone contact in parenting plans, particularly where parents live apart geographically, as a supplement to in-person visitation.
