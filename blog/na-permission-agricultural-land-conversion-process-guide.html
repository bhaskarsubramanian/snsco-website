Converting Agricultural Land for Non-Agricultural Use: The NA Permission Process Explained

A step-by-step guide to obtaining non-agricultural (NA) land-use conversion permission in India, covering the application process, common grounds for rejection, and how the conversion fee is typically assessed.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 29 September 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice: see our disclaimer

Overview

A step-by-step guide to obtaining non-agricultural (NA) land-use conversion permission in India, covering the application process, common grounds for rejection, and how the conversion fee is typically assessed.

What You Need to Know

  • Land recorded as agricultural in revenue records generally cannot be lawfully used for a non-agricultural purpose, residential, commercial or industrial, without first obtaining non-agricultural (NA) land-use conversion permission from the designated revenue or district authority
  • The NA application typically requires the applicant to submit proof of title, a current revenue extract confirming the land's agricultural classification, a site plan, and, in many states, a no-objection or clearance from the local planning/development authority confirming that the proposed use conforms to the applicable master plan or zoning regulations
  • A conversion fee (sometimes called a premium or NA charge) is assessed by the authority, typically calculated as a percentage of the land's assessed value and varying depending on the intended non-agricultural use, the land's location, and whether it falls within a designated urbanisable zone
  • Common grounds on which an NA application is rejected include the land falling within a zone reserved for agricultural use under the local master plan, an unresolved title or boundary dispute reflected in revenue records, or the proposed use being inconsistent with an environmental or coastal-regulation restriction applicable to the specific parcel
  • Once NA permission is granted and the conversion fee paid, the land's classification in revenue records is updated to reflect the non-agricultural use, but the permission is generally use-specific, meaning a further change to a different non-agricultural use later may itself require fresh permission
  • A landowner who uses agricultural land for a non-agricultural purpose without first obtaining NA permission risks penalty proceedings, retrospective conversion-fee demands, and, in some states, the risk of the unauthorised construction itself being treated as liable to demolition or regularisation only on payment of a penalty premium

Frequently Asked Questions

Can agricultural land be used for a factory or residential colony without NA permission?

No: using agricultural land for a non-agricultural purpose without first obtaining conversion permission from the designated authority exposes the landowner to penalty proceedings and potential action against the unauthorised use.

How is the NA conversion fee calculated?

It is generally assessed as a percentage of the land's assessed value, with the applicable rate depending on the intended use, location and zoning classification: the exact formula varies by state.

Once granted, does NA permission cover any future non-agricultural use of the land?

Not necessarily: permission is typically tied to the specific use applied for, and a later change to a different non-agricultural use may require a fresh application.
