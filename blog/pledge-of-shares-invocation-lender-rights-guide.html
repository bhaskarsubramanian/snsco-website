Invoking a Pledge of Shares on Default: What a Lender Can and Cannot Do

A pledge of shares is a common promoter and acquisition-finance security, but invoking it on default follows its own rules. Here is what a lender can and cannot do.

By S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors · Published 7 October 2026 · Informational content, not legal advice, see our disclaimer

The Legal Framework Behind a Share Pledge

A pledge of shares as loan security is governed by a combination of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, which supplies the underlying pledge and bailment provisions, the Companies Act, 2013, which requires charge registration, the Depositories Act, 1996 and SEBI's depository regulations, and, where applicable, the SARFAESI Act, 2002.

Under the Companies Act, 2013, a company creating a pledge over its shares is required to have the charge registered, and an unregistered charge cannot be enforced against the company by a creditor.

Invocation on Default: Sale or Registration as Beneficial Owner

On default, a pledgee-lender's core contract-law options are to sue for recovery while retaining the pledge as security, or to sell the pledged shares after giving the borrower reasonable notice of the intended sale.

On proper invocation, a pledgee, or its assignee, can get itself registered as beneficial owner of the dematerialised pledged shares, entitling it to exercise voting and other shareholder rights attached to those shares, a point affirmed by Bombay High Court authority.

The Pledgor's Redemption Right Survives Invocation

The pledgor's right of redemption survives invocation and registration as beneficial owner. It is only extinguished once the shares are actually sold to a third party, so a borrower retains a window to redeem by clearing the debt even after the pledge has been invoked.

Practical lender diligence should confirm that the charge is duly registered, that depository and depository-participant level invocation formalities are correctly followed, and that notice requirements are satisfied before relying on invoked pledged shares as the primary recovery route.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a lender immediately sell pledged shares the moment a borrower defaults?

No. A pledgee must give the borrower reasonable notice of the intended sale before selling, or alternatively sue for recovery while continuing to hold the pledge as security.

Does invoking the pledge and becoming beneficial owner end the borrower's right to redeem?

No. The right of redemption survives invocation and registration as beneficial owner, and is extinguished only once the shares are actually sold to a third party.

What happens if the charge over pledged shares was never registered with the Registrar of Companies?

An unregistered charge cannot be enforced against the company by the creditor, so registration is a basic precondition for relying on the pledge.
