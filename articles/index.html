Joint Ventures and Acquisitions in India: Contractual Protections, Regulatory Approvals and the Traps That Break Deals

Most Indian joint ventures and acquisitions are won or lost on paper long before anyone sues anyone: on whether the shareholders' agreement actually binds the company, whether the chosen structure survives a regulator's second look, and whether the exit clause can be enforced rather than merely invoked. This piece sets out where the law stands today on structuring, the CCI, FEMA and SEBI approvals a deal typically needs, and the drafting traps that recur across Indian transactions.

Consumer Protection and E-Commerce in 2026: CCPA Enforcement, Dark Patterns and Platform Liability

Five years after the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 created a dedicated regulator, the Central Consumer Protection Authority has settled into a visible pattern of penalty orders against platforms, advertisers and sellers. A fresh round of amendment rules, due to take effect on 1 January 2027, will tighten that regime further on pricing, search rankings and dark patterns. This piece sets out where the law stands today for businesses and consumers who deal in goods and services online, and where it meets product liability, arbitration clauses, RERA and the ordinary business of banks and insurers before consumer commissions.

Debt Recovery in India Today: SARFAESI, DRT, Commercial Courts and IBC Sections 7 and 9 Compared

A secured lender chasing an unpaid loan in India today can usually choose between four distinct routes to the same money, and the choice is rarely obvious from the loan documents alone. This piece sets out how SARFAESI, the Debts Recovery Tribunals, the ordinary and commercial civil courts, and Sections 7 and 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code actually compare, what a guarantor or promoter can expect under each, and where the Supreme Court has moved the ground in 2025 and 2026.

Oppression and Mismanagement Before the NCLT: Where Minority Shareholder Remedies Stand Today

A minority shareholder who feels frozen out of a company has one principal forum left: the National Company Law Tribunal, under Sections 241 to 246 of the Companies Act, 2013. The route is narrower than it looks, the threshold to even file is often contested before the merits are reached, and recent rulings have reshaped both ends of the process, who may walk through the door and what happens to a shareholder's claim once the company itself goes through insolvency.

Redevelopment and Joint Development Agreements: Society Disputes, Termination and the Right Forum

A redevelopment or joint development project sits across at least four bodies of law at once, the cooperative societies statute governing the owners, the contract and development agreement governing the developer, RERA governing the project once flats are sold, and increasingly the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code when the developer itself runs into trouble. Disputes rarely stay inside one of those boxes, and recent rulings have clarified, and in places complicated, which forum actually has the last word.

Trademark Enforcement in India: Infringement, Passing Off and What Courts Now Require on Evidence and Delay

Indian trademark litigation runs on two tracks at once, the statutory action for infringement and the common law action for passing off, and a brand owner who understands only one of them is working with half a toolkit. Recent rulings have reopened questions that practitioners thought settled: whether a mark must be formally declared well known before it gets broader protection, whether a civil court notified as a commercial court can even hear the suit, and how much a plaintiff's delay in approaching the court should count against it.

Enforcing Foreign Arbitral Awards in India: Part II, Section 48 and Interim Relief in Foreign-Seated Arbitrations

A party that wins an arbitration seated outside India still has to clear a second contest before it sees any money: getting an Indian court to treat the award as a decree against assets here. Part II of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 makes that path deliberately narrow, and a run of 2025-2026 rulings on limitation, issue estoppel and interim protection has sharpened exactly how narrow it now is, in both directions.

The Four Labour Codes a Year On: What Actually Changed for Employers and Workers, and What Is Still Missing

The Code on Wages, the Industrial Relations Code, the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code and the Code on Social Security came into force on 21 November 2025, replacing twenty-nine central labour statutes. Central rules followed by May 2026, but the Codes depend on labour being a concurrent subject, which means each state must write and notify its own rules before the new regime actually governs a given workplace. A year in, the picture is uneven: some provisions are settled law, several carry two conflicting readings in the commentary, and the national floor wage that was meant to anchor the whole wage structure has still not been notified.

Termination Payments and the Arbitration Squeeze: What 2026's Infrastructure Rulings Mean for Concessionaires

Two things happened to Indian infrastructure dispute resolution in 2026 that deserve to be read together. Courts hearing Section 34 and 37 challenges set aside, or substantially cut, several large termination-payment awards that concessionaires had won in arbitration, tightening the margin for error in how such claims are pleaded and calculated. At the same time, the National Highways Authority of India pulled its largest disputes out of binding arbitration entirely through a January 2026 circular. Concessionaires negotiating or litigating a termination today are working with a dispute resolution landscape that looks different from the one their concession agreements were drafted against.

Restitution, Not Relief: How 2025-2026 Rulings Reshaped Change in Law and Jurisdiction in India's Power Sector

Change in law litigation under India's power purchase agreements has moved past the basic question of whether a given government measure qualifies as a change in law at all, and into the harder question of what full restitution actually requires once it does. The Supreme Court and the Appellate Tribunal for Electricity decided several disputes through 2025 and 2026 that push that restitution principle further than before, while a separate line of rulings has drawn tighter boundaries around who can claim deemed distribution licensee status and which forum, the regulatory commission or an arbitrator, actually has the first word on a generator's non-tariff grievance.

FSSAI Enforcement in 2026: Labelling Claims, Licence Suspension, Recall and the Appeal Routes That Actually Apply

Food safety enforcement in India runs on two tracks that are easy to confuse: an administrative track built around the improvement notice, suspension and cancellation of a licence, and a separate penalty track adjudicated under Chapter X of the Act. 2026 has tested both, from a wave of High Court rulings curbing open-ended suspensions to a cluster of interim orders in the Dabur, Britannia, ITC and Red Bull labelling disputes, with the Supreme Court's long-running front-of-pack warning label case still awaiting judgment.

GST Litigation After the GST Appellate Tribunal: Show Cause Notices, Pre-Deposit, ITC Denial and the New Appeal Map

For the first eight years of the GST regime, a disputed demand that survived the first appeal usually went straight to a writ court, because the Tribunal the statute promised did not exist. That changed through late 2025 and into 2026, as the GST Appellate Tribunal was notified, staffed and opened for filing. The result is a more structured and more procedurally exacting path, with pre-deposit obligations at two separate stages, a backlog filing deadline that has already passed, and High Courts now routinely sending taxpayers back to the statutory ladder they had previously bypassed.

Succession and NRIs: Wills, Probate, Inheritance and Repatriation After the 2025 Reform

Every non-resident Indian with a parent, sibling or spouse holding assets in India eventually runs into the same set of questions: which law decides who inherits, whether a will needs a court's blessing before it can be acted upon, and how much of the proceeds can actually leave the country. Those questions got a genuine answer this year, when Parliament quietly removed a century-old precondition to acting on a will in three of India's largest cities. This piece sets out where the law on NRI succession actually stands today, and where the real delays in administering an NRI's Indian estate continue to come from.

Arbitration in India After Balasamy, CORE and the Stamp Act Ruling: Where the Law Stands Today

Between December 2023 and April 2025 the Supreme Court decided, through three separate Constitution Benches, questions that had divided High Courts and earlier Supreme Court benches for a decade: whether an unstamped contract can carry a live arbitration clause, whether one party can control how the arbitrator is chosen, and whether a court can modify an award rather than only set it aside. Each answer narrows what used to be a wide field of threshold objections, though none of the three rulings closes every door it touches.

The IBC Amendment Act, 2026: What Actually Changes for Lenders, Operational Creditors and Promoters

The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026 received Presidential assent on 6 April 2026 and most of its provisions were brought into force from 26 May 2026. It settles, by statute, an admission-stage argument the Supreme Court had been working through for four years, creates a creditor-driven route into insolvency, and gives a failed process one more chance before liquidation. Its most discussed reforms, on group and cross-border insolvency, are not yet operative, and that distinction matters for anyone advising on a live matter.

The DPDP Act's Compliance Clock: What Is in Force Today, What Arrives in November 2026, and What Is Still to Come

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the Rules notified under it in November 2025 did not switch India's data protection regime on all at once. They set a staggered three-tranche timetable running to May 2027, with the Data Protection Board itself already functioning and the next tranche, covering consent managers, due in a matter of weeks from the date of this article. This piece looks past the compliance checklist to the enforcement architecture and the actual clock businesses are working against.

Generative AI has reached Indian courtrooms faster than Parliament has reached generative AI. Four strands of litigation, on authorship, training data, persona and branding, now give a reasonably clear picture of where Indian intellectual property law stands, even without a dedicated AI statute.

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam came into force on 1 July 2024, replacing the Indian Penal Code, the Code of Criminal Procedure and the Evidence Act. Two years on, enough courts have ruled on enough provisions to say with some confidence what changed in day-to-day practice, and what is still being worked out.

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act took partial effect on 1 May 2016 and came fully into force a year later. Ten years on, the Supreme Court itself has asked aloud whether some states should keep the authority at all.
