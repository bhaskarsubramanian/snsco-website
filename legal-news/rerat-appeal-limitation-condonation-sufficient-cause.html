Real Estate & RERA · 22 January 2026

A RERA Appellate Tribunal Can Condone Delay Beyond the Extended 60-Day Window Only on Genuinely Exceptional Facts: Tribunal Reiterates

Source: As reported by LiveLaw on 22 January 2026. This article has been independently researched and rewritten in full by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes; it is not a reproduction of the original report. Readers are encouraged to consult the original source and the underlying order directly.

A state Real Estate Appellate Tribunal dismissed an application for condonation of delay filed by promoter Vishram Green Habitat Pvt. Ltd. seeking to appeal an adverse RERA Authority order nearly 150 days after the prescribed 60-day limitation period under Section 44(2) of RERA had expired, holding that the 'sufficient cause' standard for condonation is not met by generalised explanations of internal administrative reshuffling or a change of counsel mid-way through the limitation period.

The Tribunal reiterated the settled position that Section 44(2) prescribes a 60-day limitation period for filing an appeal, extendable by a further period not exceeding 60 days on sufficient cause being shown: meaning the outer limit for condonation is itself capped, and delay beyond that combined 120-day window cannot ordinarily be condoned at all, regardless of the explanation offered. Within that outer window, the Tribunal held, sufficient cause requires the appellant to show specific, verifiable circumstances that genuinely prevented timely filing, not a bare assertion that internal processes took time.

The Tribunal also addressed the promoter's argument that the delay should be viewed leniently because the appeal, if allowed to proceed, would raise a substantial question on the merits; the Tribunal held that the strength of a proposed appeal on merits is not itself a ground to relax the limitation threshold, since accepting that reasoning would effectively read the limitation provision out of the statute for any appellant confident about their case.

The ruling is a caution to both promoters and allottees that RERA's appellate timelines are applied strictly, and that an appeal strategy should build in the limitation period as a hard constraint from the outset rather than a formality to be managed later.

Why It Matters

A party considering an appeal against a RERA Authority or Adjudicating Officer order should calendar the 60-day limitation period (extendable by at most 60 more days on genuine sufficient cause) from day one: the merits of the underlying appeal will not rescue a late filing.
