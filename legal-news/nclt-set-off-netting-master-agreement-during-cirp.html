NCLT Permits Contractual Netting Under a Master Trading Agreement to Be Given Effect During CIRP, Distinguishing It From a Post-Plan Set-Off Claim

Source: As reported in insolvency-law commentary and tribunal case digests. This article has been independently researched and written by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes, summarising the general legal position rather than reproducing any single report. Party names have been anonymised/fictionalised for illustration where the underlying matter is not a matter of public record verified by us; readers should verify current developments against the underlying order and primary sources.

The National Company Law Tribunal has permitted a commodities trading counterparty to give effect to a contractual netting mechanism under a master trading agreement executed with Vantage Agri Commodities Limited, a corporate debtor undergoing CIRP, holding that a close-out netting provision operating on multiple open positions under a single master agreement stands on a different footing from an ordinary equitable set-off asserted defensively in litigation.

The resolution professional had objected that giving effect to the netting arrangement post-admission would amount to a unilateral adjustment of the corporate debtor's assets in violation of the Section 14 moratorium. The Tribunal disagreed on the facts before it, holding that where a master agreement structures multiple transactions as a single net obligation from inception, such that the parties never intended gross, transaction-by-transaction settlement, recognising the netted balance reflects the true extent of the debt owed rather than a fresh set-off being carved out of the corporate debtor's assets during moratorium.

The Tribunal was careful to confine its finding to the specific single-agreement netting structure before it, and clarified that this is distinct from, and should not be read as authority for, a creditor unilaterally adjusting separate and unrelated mutual debts against a corporate debtor's dues after CIRP admission, which continues to require closer scrutiny under Section 14.

Why It Matters

Counterparties relying on close-out netting under master trading or derivatives-style agreements should be able to point to a genuine single-obligation netting structure predating insolvency: a bare mutual set-off between unrelated debts remains far more vulnerable to a moratorium objection.
