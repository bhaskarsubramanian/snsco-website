An Anti-Dilution 'Full Ratchet' Clause in a Share Subscription Agreement Is Enforceable Even Against a Down-Round Priced in Good Faith: NCLT

Source: As reported by startup-legal trade press in July 2026, on an NCLT order arising from a Series A investor's petition against a technology company. Party names are fictitious.

The National Company Law Tribunal has held that a full-ratchet anti-dilution clause contained in a Series A share subscription agreement, entered into by fictitious technology company Meridian Analytics Pvt. Ltd. and its investor Crestview Growth Fund, remains enforceable against the company even where a subsequent down round is priced by an independent registered valuer acting in good faith.

Meridian had argued that anti-dilution protection was intended only to guard against opportunistic or collusive low-priced issuances, and should not mechanically apply where the down round reflected a genuine, valuer-certified decline in the company's fair value. The Tribunal disagreed, holding that where the subscription agreement's anti-dilution formula is expressed in clear, unconditional terms, a company cannot read in a 'good faith pricing' exception that the contracting parties did not themselves negotiate.

The Tribunal directed the company to issue Crestview additional equity shares for no further consideration, computed strictly under the full-ratchet formula in the agreement, and observed that founders and investors who wish to soften a full-ratchet clause's effect in a genuine down round must negotiate that carveout expressly at the time of contracting, rather than seek it retrospectively.

Why It Matters

Founders negotiating anti-dilution protection with investors should treat a full-ratchet clause as exactly as unforgiving as its wording suggests — a later, good-faith down round will not by itself excuse the company from the formula's mechanical effect unless an exception was expressly built into the agreement.
