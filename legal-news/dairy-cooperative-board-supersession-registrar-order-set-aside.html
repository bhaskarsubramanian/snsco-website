Registrar's Supersession of Elected Dairy Cooperative Board Set Aside for Bypassing Statutory Show-Cause Procedure

Source: As reported in cooperative-sector legal bulletins in July 2026. This article has been independently researched and written by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes and is not a reproduction of any single report.

A cooperative tribunal has set aside an order of the Registrar of Cooperative Societies superseding the elected board of directors of Uttarakhand Milk Producers' Cooperative Society and appointing an administrator in its place, holding that the Registrar bypassed the mandatory show-cause and inquiry procedure prescribed under the applicable state cooperative societies law before ordering supersession.

The Registrar's order had cited alleged financial irregularities in the procurement and payment cycle for milk supplied by member-producers, but the tribunal found that no inquiry report had been placed before the board, nor had the board been served a specific show-cause notice identifying the particular irregularities alleged, before the supersession order was passed.

The tribunal reiterated that supersession of an elected cooperative board is an extraordinary step that displaces the democratic governance structure the cooperative societies law is designed to protect, and that the statute's procedural safeguards, a specific show-cause notice, a reasonable opportunity to respond, and a reasoned order addressing that response, are not optional formalities but conditions precedent to the exercise of the supersession power.

The supersession order was set aside and the elected board restored, without prejudice to the Registrar's liberty to initiate a fresh inquiry strictly in accordance with the statutory procedure if the alleged irregularities are to be pursued.

Why It Matters

Cooperative societies in the dairy and sugar sectors facing threatened supersession should insist on strict compliance with the statutory show-cause and inquiry procedure before any Registrar's order: a supersession passed without a specific notice and a genuine opportunity to respond remains vulnerable to challenge regardless of the seriousness of the underlying allegations.
