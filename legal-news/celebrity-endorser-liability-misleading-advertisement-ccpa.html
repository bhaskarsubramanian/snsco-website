CCPA Reiterates That Endorsers Must Exercise 'Due Diligence' Before Endorsing a Product or Face Penalty

Source: As reported in consumer-law commentary and case digests through 2026. This article has been independently researched and written by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes, summarising the general legal position rather than reproducing any single report. Readers should verify current developments against the underlying order or judgment and primary sources.

The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) has reiterated, in an order concerning a misleading advertisement for a wellness product, that an endorser, whether a celebrity, influencer, or other public figure, must exercise reasonable due diligence to verify the veracity of the claims made in an advertisement before lending their name or image to it, and cannot avoid liability merely by asserting that they relied entirely on assurances given by the advertiser.

The advertisement in question, for a product marketed generically in the order as a 'nutritional wellness supplement', made specific claims about measurable health outcomes within a defined timeframe that were not substantiated by any clinical or scientific backing produced before the Authority. The manufacturer, the advertising agency, and the endorser were all issued notices, with the CCPA examining each party's role separately rather than treating liability as automatically joint.

In its order, the CCPA set out the factors relevant to assessing an endorser's due diligence, including whether the endorser sought and reviewed any supporting substantiation for the specific claims made, whether the endorsement reflected the endorser's own genuine assessment or use of the product, and the endorser's own reach and influence over the target audience. It found that a mere disclaimer of personal opinion, without any inquiry into the underlying factual claims, did not meet the due diligence standard, and imposed a monetary penalty on the endorser in addition to penalties on the manufacturer and advertising agency.

The order continues a consistent CCPA enforcement trend making clear that endorsers cannot treat their role as purely commercial with no independent responsibility for the accuracy of the claims they help disseminate, particularly where the endorsement reaches a large consumer base through social media.

Why It Matters

Anyone endorsing a product, commercially or otherwise, should request and review the substantiation behind any specific, measurable claim before agreeing to the endorsement, and should ensure any personal-opinion disclaimer is genuine rather than a shield for an unverified factual claim.
