Repudiating an Insurance Claim Without Examining the Actual Loss Is 'Deficiency in Service': State Commission

Source: As reported in consumer-law commentary and case digests through 2026. This article has been independently researched and written by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes, summarising the general legal position rather than reproducing any single report. Readers should verify current developments against the underlying order or judgment and primary sources.

A State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission has held that an insurer's repudiation of a claim purely on a technical policy ground, without any genuine inquiry into whether the underlying loss was real and covered, amounts to deficiency in service under the Consumer Protection Act. The complaint arose from a household insurance policy where the insured, referred to in the order as the complainant, suffered fire damage to stored goods and filed a claim supported by a police report and an independent surveyor's assessment.

The insurer, referred to as the opposite party, repudiated the claim citing a minor discrepancy between the declared value of the goods at the time of policy renewal and the value claimed after the loss, without commissioning any fresh investigation into whether the discrepancy reflected genuine appreciation in stock value or was indicative of any misrepresentation. The Commission found that the insurer's own appointed surveyor had, in fact, assessed the loss as genuine and broadly consistent with the insured's claim, making the repudiation on a purely documentary technicality unsustainable.

The Commission distinguished this case from situations involving established fraud or deliberate misrepresentation, where Indian courts have consistently held that no relief — not even partial — is warranted. Here, absent any finding of fraud, the Commission held that an insurer cannot use a minor valuation discrepancy as a pretext to avoid a claim its own surveyor had assessed as genuine, and directed payment of the claim amount along with interest from the date of the original claim, together with compensation for the delay and litigation costs.

The ruling reflects a broader trend in consumer jurisprudence requiring insurers to engage substantively with a claim on its merits before repudiating it, rather than relying on technical policy language to avoid a payout where the surveyor's own findings support the claim.

Why It Matters

Where an insurer repudiates a claim on a technical ground while its own surveyor's report supports the loss, that internal inconsistency is often the strongest evidence in a subsequent consumer complaint — insured parties should always obtain a copy of the surveyor's report before deciding whether to contest a repudiation.
