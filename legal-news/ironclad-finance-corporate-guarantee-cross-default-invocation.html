A Cross-Default Clause Tied to a Corporate Guarantee Cannot Be Triggered by a Default Under an Entirely Unrelated Facility: High Court

Source: As reported by banking and corporate trade press in September 2026, on a High Court order restraining invocation of a corporate guarantee. Party names below are fictitious.

A High Court has restrained a lender from invoking a corporate guarantee issued by fictitious holding company Ironclad Finance Ltd. in support of a term loan facility to its subsidiary, after the lender sought to trigger the guarantee's cross-default clause based on a payment default under an entirely separate working capital facility availed by a different group company.

The lender argued that the cross-default clause, worded broadly to cover 'any default by the Guarantor or any of its Affiliates under any financial indebtedness,' entitled it to treat the unrelated group default as an event of default under the guarantee. The Court disagreed, holding that a cross-default clause, precisely because of its potentially sweeping commercial consequences, must be construed strictly against the party seeking to invoke it, and that the guarantee documentation, read as a whole, contemplated cross-default only in relation to indebtedness connected with the specific guaranteed facility and its designated group of obligors, not any and every default anywhere in a sprawling corporate group.

The Court granted an interim injunction restraining invocation pending final disposal of the suit, while clarifying that its finding on the clause's scope was prima facie and would be tested at trial.

Why It Matters

Both lenders and corporate guarantors should read cross-default language closely at the drafting stage — broad-sounding phrases like 'any Affiliate' or 'any indebtedness' will still be construed by courts in light of the guarantee's overall commercial context, and a lender cannot assume the widest possible reading will automatically be upheld when invocation is actually challenged.
