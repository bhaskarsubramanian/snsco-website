PFRDA Amends NPS Scheme Naming and Display Rules, Compliance Due by December 1

Source: Originally reported by outlookmoney.com, upstox.com, angelone.in and staffnews.in. This article has been independently researched and rewritten in full by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes: it is not a reproduction of the original reports. Readers are encouraged to consult the original sources and the underlying judgement directly.

The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority issued Circular No. PFRDA/2026/52/REG-PF/12 on 2 November 2026, amending its earlier circular of 28 August 2026 that set out a standardised framework for classifying and presenting schemes under the National Pension System. The amendment is addressed to every Pension Fund, Point of Presence and Central Recordkeeping Agency operating in the NPS architecture, and compliance is due by 1 December 2026.

The August framework had already reorganised every NPS investment scheme into five broad categories, sorted by how much of the portfolio each scheme puts into equity. Reports describe the top band, Category A, as covering schemes with roughly 80 to 100 percent equity allocation, with the remaining categories graduated down to more conservative, debt-heavy options. The same framework discontinued the older distinction between what the industry called Common Schemes and Multiple Scheme Framework, or MSF, schemes, requiring Pension Funds to rename or restructure their existing MSF schemes to fit the new five-category system within thirty days.

The November amendment keeps those five categories unchanged, but it also drops the category code (A to E) from an MSF scheme's own name under amended clauses 3.1 to 3.3, so a scheme's title no longer has to carry a code, though the category must still appear next to or below the name in a smaller font. The amendment also fixes how a subscriber actually encounters the categories when choosing a scheme online. Under the revised instructions, the screen flow on the NPS Central platform and on the Central Recordkeeping Agency systems must show schemes from every Pension Fund within whichever category a subscriber selects, rather than steering the subscriber toward a narrower, pre-filtered list. The circular also sets out an interim redirection route for the transition period, so that subscribers already partway through choosing a scheme are not left looking at outdated category names or discontinued MSF labels while the changeover is underway. PFRDA has taken this staged approach, category definitions first in August, then scheme naming and display mechanics in November, because fund houses, banks and online platforms each run their own front-end systems and needed a sequence they could follow without pulling existing subscribers' accounts out of order midway.

For an ordinary NPS subscriber, the practical effect shows up on the application or website used to pick or switch a scheme. Scheme names, brochures and onboarding screens across every Point of Presence, whether a bank, a broker or an online platform, must now use the same five-category vocabulary and must present comparable options from different Pension Funds side by side within a category, instead of defaulting to whichever fund the platform happens to favour. A subscriber comparing an aggressive equity-heavy option against a conservative one should, from 1 December, see that comparison laid out in the same terms wherever they log in.

The change sits alongside a separate, and more consequential, set of changes PFRDA made for Non-Resident Indian and Overseas Citizen of India subscribers at the end of 2025, which eased the onboarding and know-your-customer process for that group by allowing digital onboarding and permitting KYC updates at any overseas branch of the entity holding their NPS account. The November 2026 amendment does not alter those NRI and OCI provisions. It is a general change in how all subscribers, wherever they are based, see and compare schemes, and it applies to every NPS account regardless of whether the subscriber opened it in India or abroad.

Pension Funds, Points of Presence and Central Recordkeeping Agencies that miss the 1 December deadline risk falling out of step with a regulator that has, over the past several months, moved in stages from redefining scheme categories in August, to mandating renaming and restructuring within thirty days, to now dictating the naming format MSF schemes must use and exactly how those categories must be displayed to the person actually choosing between them. Anyone contributing to the National Pension System, including an NRI managing retirement savings from abroad, should expect the scheme list on their provider's platform to look different after 1 December, and would be well advised to re-check which category their existing scheme now falls under rather than assume its old label still applies.

The amendment leaves the underlying investment choices available to a subscriber unchanged; it governs classification, naming and the way choices are displayed, not the schemes' actual asset allocation or returns.

Why It Matters

Every NPS subscriber, including an NRI who keeps contributing to a pension account opened before moving abroad, will see a changed scheme list on their provider's platform from 1 December 2026, with every scheme sorted into one of five equity-based categories and shown alongside comparable options from other Pension Funds. Anyone who has not reviewed their NPS scheme recently should check which category it now falls under, since an old scheme name or the discontinued Common Scheme or MSF label will no longer appear the way it used to on statements, brochures or onboarding screens.
