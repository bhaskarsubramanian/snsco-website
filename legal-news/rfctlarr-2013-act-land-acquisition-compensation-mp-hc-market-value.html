Comparable Sale Deeds From a Different Village Cannot Fix Market Value Under the 2013 Land Acquisition Act: MP High Court

Source: Originally reported by legal trade press on 15 May 2026, with the ruling reported as a Madhya Pradesh High Court decision under the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013. This article has been independently researched and rewritten in full by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes — it is not a reproduction of the original reports. Readers are encouraged to consult the original sources and the underlying judgment directly.

Landowners whose agricultural land was acquired for an infrastructure corridor project under the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013 (RFCTLARR Act) had obtained an enhanced compensation award from the reference court based on sale deeds of land situated in a neighbouring village. The acquiring authority appealed, contending that the reference court failed to establish that the comparator land shared similar characteristics — location, access, land use potential and proximity to developed areas — with the acquired land.

The Madhya Pradesh High Court set aside the enhancement, holding that reliance on comparable sale instances for fixing market value under Section 26 of the RFCTLARR Act requires the court to first establish genuine comparability of the exemplar land — mere geographic proximity between villages is not sufficient where there are material differences in road access, proximity to urban development, and land classification. The matter was remanded for fresh determination of market value with a properly reasoned comparability analysis.

The Court reiterated that the 2013 Act's multiplier-based solatium and rehabilitation entitlements attach only once a legally sound market value base is fixed, and that shortcuts in establishing comparability at the market-value stage will not survive appellate scrutiny even where the underlying statutory formula is otherwise correctly applied.

Why It Matters

Landowners and acquiring authorities in infrastructure land acquisitions under the 2013 Act should ensure comparable sale instances used to fix market value are genuinely similar in location, access and land classification — an enhancement built on loosely comparable land risks being set aside on appeal regardless of the multiplier applied thereafter.
