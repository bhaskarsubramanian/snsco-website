No Penalty Under Section 271C for TDS Default Where Reasonable Cause Is Shown, Rules ITAT

Source: As reported in tax and trade press in August 2026. This article has been independently researched and written by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes and is not a reproduction of any single report.

The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal has deleted a penalty levied under Section 271C of the Income Tax Act against Northgate Consultants, a professional services firm, for allegedly short-deducting tax at source on payments made to a group of retained consultants, holding that the firm had a reasonable cause for the position it took and that penalty cannot follow automatically from a mere finding of short deduction in quantum proceedings.

The underlying dispute concerned whether payments to the consultants were in the nature of professional fees, attracting a higher rate of deduction under Section 194J, or contractual payments attracting a lower rate under Section 194C. The firm had consistently deducted tax at the lower contractual rate, treating the arrangements as service contracts rather than individual professional engagements, and had disclosed the basis for this treatment in its TDS returns and in response to departmental queries.

In the quantum proceedings, the assessing officer had held the payments to be professional fees and raised a demand for the shortfall along with interest, which the firm did not contest further. Separately, however, a penalty under Section 271C — equal to the amount of tax not deducted — was also imposed, treating the short deduction itself as sufficient ground for penalty.

The Tribunal held that Section 273B expressly saves a taxpayer from penalty under Section 271C where reasonable cause for the failure is shown, and that a disclosed, consistently applied, and not implausible classification of a payment — even if ultimately held incorrect on merits — constitutes such reasonable cause. Penalty under Section 271C is not an automatic consequence of every quantum addition for short deduction, and the department must separately establish an absence of reasonable cause before penalty can be sustained.

Why It Matters

A taxpayer who has consistently disclosed and applied a genuine, non-frivolous view on which TDS section governs a category of payments has grounds to resist a Section 271C penalty even after losing the underlying quantum dispute, provided the position was not concealed and was capable of bona fide debate.
