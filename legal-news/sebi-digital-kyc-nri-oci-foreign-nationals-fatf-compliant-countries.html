SEBI Lets NRIs, OCIs and Foreign Nationals Complete Securities KYC Digitally From Abroad

Source: Originally reported by moneylife.in, LiveLaw Biz and calcguru.in. This article has been independently researched and rewritten in full by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes: it is not a reproduction of the original reports. Readers are encouraged to consult the original sources and the underlying judgement directly.

An NRI based in Dubai or Singapore who wants to open a demat account or invest in an Indian mutual fund has, for years, run into the same wall: the digital onboarding route that resident Indians use was not actually available to someone sitting outside India, because the rules required the client to be physically present in India at the moment the video-based verification happened. A circular the Securities and Exchange Board of India issued on 4 November 2026 removes that wall for a defined category of overseas investors.

The circular addresses what SEBI calls an individual Person Resident Outside India, a category that covers non-resident Indians, Overseas Citizen of India cardholders, and foreign nationals who hold or want to hold securities market accounts in India. Under the KYC framework as it stood before this circular, an intermediary such as a stockbroker, depository participant or mutual fund registrar could carry out digital KYC only if the client was physically located in India during the process. An NRI client calling in from London or Toronto could not use that route at all, whatever their documents showed, and was pushed instead toward paper-based verification routed through an Indian consulate, apostilled documents couriered to India, or simply waiting for their next visit.

That relaxation applies to a client resident in a country the Financial Action Task Force treats as compliant with its standards; an investor living in a FATF non-compliant country remains on the older, stricter process. The change covers both fresh onboarding and periodic re-KYC, which matters because a large share of NRI accounts flagged for re-verification each year get stuck for exactly the same physical-presence reason.

SEBI has not done away with in-person verification altogether. What it has done is let intermediaries use Video In-Person Verification, a live video call in which the intermediary's representative checks the client's identity and documents on screen, as a substitute for a face-to-face meeting when that meeting is not practically possible. Passport details stay mandatory, an OCI cardholder still has to produce the OCI card, and PAN continues to be required subject to the exemptions that already existed. None of the underlying document requirements has been diluted; what has changed is the mode by which an intermediary can satisfy itself that the person on screen is who the documents say they are.

Two carve-outs are worth flagging for anyone reading the circular as a green light across the board. First, a foreign national who wants to invest through the Foreign Portfolio Investor route is not covered by this relaxation; that registration continues to run under the separate FPI framework, which has its own know-your-client requirements. Second, the relaxation only applies going forward from the date it takes effect, so a client already mid-process under the old rules does not automatically get moved onto the new one without the intermediary re-initiating the digital route.

The circular takes effect from 3 December 2026, giving intermediaries roughly a month to update their onboarding software, train staff who conduct video verification calls, and revise the compliance manuals that currently assume a client has to be in India. For brokers and depository participants who service a large NRI client base out of Delhi, Mumbai or Kolkata, that lead time is short enough that the sensible move is to start now rather than wait for the effective date to arrive.

For an NRI or OCI family weighing whether to open a trading account, a demat account or a mutual fund folio while still abroad, the practical question becomes whether their country of residence currently sits on the FATF compliant list, since that single fact decides whether they can use the new digital route or have to fall back on the older paper-heavy process. That status can change, so it is worth checking at the time of onboarding rather than relying on what was true a year earlier.

Family offices and wealth managers who handle Indian investments for several NRI clients spread across different countries will want to map each client's country of residence against the FATF list before the 3 December 2026 date, rather than assuming the whole client base qualifies together. A client in a FATF-compliant country gets the benefit of the new digital route; a client just one jurisdiction away on the non-compliant list does not, and still has to go through the older process until that position changes.

Why It Matters

This circular removes the single biggest practical obstacle NRIs, OCI cardholders and foreign nationals have faced when opening Indian securities or mutual fund accounts from abroad: the requirement to be physically present in India during digital verification. Families and individuals managing investments across borders can now complete onboarding by video call from wherever they live, provided that country meets FATF standards. Intermediaries have a short window before the 3 December 2026 effective date to update their systems, and NRI clients already mid-onboarding should confirm which process applies to them before that date arrives.
