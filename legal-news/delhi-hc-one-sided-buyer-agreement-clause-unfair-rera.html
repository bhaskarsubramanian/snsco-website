Real Estate & RERA · 11 April 2026

A Builder-Buyer Agreement Clause Giving the Developer Unlimited Time to Complete but Capping the Buyer's Delay Interest Is Unconscionable: Delhi HC

Source: As reported by Live Law on 11 April 2026. This article has been independently researched and rewritten in full by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes; it is not a reproduction of the original report. Readers are encouraged to consult the original source and the underlying order directly.

The Delhi High Court dismissed a writ petition filed by developer Sunhira Infraprojects Ltd. challenging a Haryana RERA order that had refused to give effect to a clause in the builder-buyer agreement permitting the developer 'reasonable extension' of the possession timeline for reasons left largely to its own discretion, while separately capping the interest payable to the buyer for any delay at a modest fixed rate regardless of how long possession was actually delayed.

Justice Sanjeev Narula held that RERA authorities are entitled to examine whether a specific clause in a builder-buyer agreement is one-sided or unconscionable when adjudicating a delay-compensation complaint, even though the agreement was voluntarily signed by the buyer, because the bargaining position between a promoter and an individual homebuyer at the point of booking is inherently unequal: a point the Supreme Court itself made in an earlier ruling on standard-form builder agreements. The Court held that a clause allowing the developer open-ended discretion over the completion date, paired with a fixed low-rate cap on the buyer's delay compensation irrespective of the length of delay, could not be enforced according to its terms.

The Court clarified that its ruling does not empower RERA authorities to rewrite commercial bargains generally, but that specific terms found to be one-sided in the sense identified, asymmetric obligations going to the very heart of the timeline-and-compensation bargain, can be read down or disregarded when determining the compensation actually payable under Section 18.

The decision reinforces that RERA's protective framework for homebuyers is not confined to statutory provisions alone; authorities retain jurisdiction to look past contractual language that is designed to insulate a developer from the real consequences of delay.

Why It Matters

Homebuyers challenged with a builder-buyer agreement clause that caps delay compensation at a low fixed rate while leaving the developer's timeline open-ended should raise the clause's one-sidedness directly before the RERA authority. Courts have shown willingness to disregard such asymmetric terms when computing compensation.
