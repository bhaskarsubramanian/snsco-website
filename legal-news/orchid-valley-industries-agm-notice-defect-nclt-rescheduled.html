An AGM Held on Defective Notice Is Not Automatically Void, but Its Resolutions Can Be Set Aside on a Member's Challenge: NCLT

Source: As reported by company-law trade press in April 2026, covering an NCLT order in a shareholder petition concerning AGM notice compliance. Party names below are fictitious.

The NCLT has set aside special resolutions passed at an Annual General Meeting of a private limited company, referred to here as Orchid Valley Industries Pvt. Ltd., after a dissenting minority shareholder demonstrated that the statutory notice period of 21 clear days under Section 101 of the Companies Act, 2013 had not been observed, and that the shortened-notice consent required from members holding not less than 95% of paid-up voting share capital had not actually been obtained.

The company argued that all members had, in substance, attended and participated, and that the defect was purely technical. The Tribunal held that while a procedurally defective AGM is not automatically void (resolutions passed at it remain valid unless and until successfully challenged by an aggrieved member within a reasonable time), a genuine shortfall in the statutory consent threshold for shortened notice is a substantive, not merely technical, defect that vitiates the resolutions once properly challenged.

The Tribunal directed the company to reconvene a properly noticed AGM to reconsider the business originally proposed, and clarified that acts taken by the company in the interim in reliance on the invalidated resolutions would need to be independently reviewed for their continuing validity.

Why It Matters

Companies relying on shortened AGM notice under Section 101 must actually obtain, and be able to document, consent from members holding at least 95% of paid-up voting share capital: informal participation by most members is not a substitute for that threshold, and a shortfall leaves resolutions exposed to a later challenge.
