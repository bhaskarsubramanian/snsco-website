Warehouse Operator Held Liable for Double-Pledging Stock Against Same Negotiable Warehouse Receipt: Appellate Forum

Source: As reported in commodity-finance trade press in May 2026. This article has been independently researched and written by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes and is not a reproduction of any single report.

An appellate forum has upheld a finding of liability against Sanchit Agri Warehousing Pvt. Ltd., a warehouse operator registered under the Warehousing (Development and Regulation) Act, 2007, for issuing a second negotiable warehouse receipt against a stock of stored chickpeas that had already been pledged to a financing bank under an earlier NWR, resulting in the bank's collateral being effectively duplicated on the warehouse's own books.

The bank had extended a post-harvest loan against the negotiable warehouse receipt, relying on the statutory presumption that an NWR issued by a WDRA-registered warehouse is a reliable document of title enabling pledge and transfer without physical movement of the goods. When the borrower defaulted, the bank sought to invoke its pledge, only to discover that the same lot had also been the subject of a second NWR issued to another financier, with the warehouse's stock records showing an actual quantity insufficient to honour both receipts.

The appellate forum held that the entire statutory scheme of negotiable warehouse receipts is premised on the warehouse maintaining accurate, singular stock records and issuing only one valid receipt per depositable lot; a warehouse that issues duplicate receipts against insufficient stock defeats the reliability the NWR mechanism is designed to provide and exposes itself to liability for the resulting shortfall, independent of any liability of the borrower.

The forum directed the warehouse operator to make good the shortfall to the first-in-time NWR holder to the extent of the valid pledge, and referred the matter of the warehouse's registration compliance to the Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority for separate examination.

Why It Matters

Banks and other financiers extending credit against negotiable warehouse receipts should independently verify a warehouse's registration status and, where the value at stake justifies it, cross-check stock records directly with the WDRA rather than relying solely on the receipt itself, since liability for a duplicate receipt falls first on the issuing warehouse but recovery can still be practically difficult if the warehouse lacks resources.
