Food Safety & FSSAI · 16 February 2026

Supreme Court Presses FSSAI Over Delay in Rolling Out Front-of-Pack Warning Labels for High Sugar, Fat and Sodium Foods

Source: Originally reported by LiveLaw on 16 February 2026. This article has been independently researched and rewritten in full by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes — it is not a reproduction of the original report. Readers are encouraged to consult the original source and the underlying judgment or order directly.

The Supreme Court expressed clear dissatisfaction with the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India's progress in a long-pending public interest litigation seeking mandatory front-of-package warning labels on packaged food products high in sugar, fat, or sodium — a labelling reform that has been debated in Indian food policy circles for years but has repeatedly stalled at the implementation stage.

Front-of-pack warning labelling has been adopted in various forms across multiple jurisdictions internationally as a public health measure aimed at helping consumers make quicker, more informed choices without having to parse detailed nutritional tables on the back of packaging. India's own move toward such a regime has been the subject of extended regulatory consultation, industry pushback over specific label design and trigger thresholds, and — per the Court's evident frustration — a compliance affidavit from FSSAI that fell short of demonstrating real momentum toward actual notification and rollout.

The delay pattern the Court flagged is not unique to this particular reform — front-of-pack labelling has moved through multiple rounds of draft regulations, public consultation, and industry submissions over an extended period without reaching final notification, a trajectory that public health advocates have repeatedly attributed to sustained industry lobbying over specific design and threshold questions, such as which nutrient trigger levels warrant a warning label and what the label itself should actually look like. The Supreme Court's direct pressure on FSSAI in ongoing PIL proceedings represents one of the few external forcing mechanisms that has proven capable of meaningfully accelerating the regulatory timeline on reforms of this kind, where the underlying policy consensus has existed for years without translating into a finalised rule.

For the packaged food industry, the direction of travel remains clear even if the timeline is not: some form of front-of-pack labelling regime is very likely coming, and businesses would be well served treating package redesign and reformulation planning as a matter of when, not if. Companies that get ahead of the eventual notified standard — rather than waiting for a final rule and then scrambling to comply — will have a meaningful head start on both regulatory readiness and consumer positioning around healthier product formulations. Given how contested the specific trigger thresholds and label design have been in the underlying regulatory consultation, businesses would also be well advised to track the specific technical parameters emerging from that process closely, since the eventual compliance burden will depend heavily on precisely where those thresholds land.

Why It Matters

Front-of-pack warning labelling for high sugar/fat/sodium food is very likely coming to India — packaged food businesses should start package-design and reformulation planning now rather than waiting for the final notified standard.
