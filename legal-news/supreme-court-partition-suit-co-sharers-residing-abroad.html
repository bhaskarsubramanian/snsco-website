A Partition Suit Cannot Be Rejected Merely Because Some Co-Sharers Reside Abroad: Supreme Court

Source: As reported in civil-law commentary and case digests through mid-2026. This article has been independently researched and written by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes, summarising the general legal position rather than reproducing any single report. Readers should verify current developments against the underlying judgment and primary sources.

A partition suit involving inherited or ancestral property frequently includes co-sharers who have relocated abroad, particularly where the family has members settled overseas across more than one generation. A recurring objection raised by defendants at the threshold is that the suit is not maintainable, or cannot proceed effectively, because one or more co-sharers reside outside India and are difficult to serve or actively represent in the proceedings.

The Supreme Court has reaffirmed that residence outside India is not, by itself, a ground to reject or dismiss a partition suit at the threshold. Where a non-resident co-sharer can be validly served — whether through the procedures for service outside India under the Code of Civil Procedure, through a duly constituted power of attorney holder, or through other recognised modes — the suit is maintainable and must proceed on its merits like any other partition suit involving resident co-sharers.

The Court noted that partition is a right flowing from co-ownership itself, and a co-sharer does not forfeit that right, nor can other co-sharers resist a properly framed partition suit, merely on account of one party's geographic location. Courts are expected to make full use of the available procedural mechanisms — substituted service, service through diplomatic channels where required, and representation through power of attorney — to ensure non-resident co-sharers are properly before the court, rather than treating their absence from India as a bar to relief.

The ruling is a useful clarification for the many families with cross-border membership who hold property jointly in India, confirming that partition remains available notwithstanding the practical complications that international service can introduce into the timeline of the suit.

Why It Matters

Families with co-sharers living outside India should not assume that a partition of jointly held Indian property is unavailable or impractical — early steps such as executing a power of attorney in favour of a resident representative can materially simplify service and participation in the proceedings.
