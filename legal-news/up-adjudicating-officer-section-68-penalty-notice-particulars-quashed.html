A Section 68 Penalty Notice That Does Not Specify the Regulation Allegedly Breached Cannot Support an Adjudication Order: Allahabad High Court

Source: As reported by LiveLaw on 22 April 2026. This article has been independently researched and written by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes.

In Ganga Dairy Foods Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Uttar Pradesh, the Allahabad High Court has quashed a penalty order passed by an Adjudicating Officer under Section 68 of the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, on the ground that the show-cause notice preceding it did not specify which particular regulation or standard the food business operator was alleged to have contravened.

The petitioner, a dairy processing unit, had been issued a notice referring only in general terms to 'sub-standard product' detected on inspection, without identifying the specific parameter of the applicable dairy product standard that the sample allegedly failed, or the precise regulation said to have been breached. The Adjudicating Officer proceeded to impose a monetary penalty after a hearing at which the petitioner said it was unable to meaningfully respond, since it did not know which standard was in issue.

The Court held that adjudication proceedings under Section 68, while not criminal in the strict sense, remain penal in substance and must therefore satisfy a basic requirement of particularity: a notice that leaves the noticee to guess at the specific contravention alleged denies a fair opportunity to respond and vitiates the resulting order. The penalty order was set aside, with liberty to the Designated Officer to issue a fresh, properly particularised notice if the department chose to proceed further.

Why It Matters

Food business operators receiving a Section 68 show-cause notice should check, as a first step, whether it identifies the specific regulation or standard allegedly breached: a notice pitched only in general terms is vulnerable to challenge on natural-justice grounds, independent of the underlying merits of the allegation.
