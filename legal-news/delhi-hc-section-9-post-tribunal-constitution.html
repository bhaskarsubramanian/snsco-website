Delhi High Court Clarifies When a Section 9 Application Remains Maintainable After the Arbitral Tribunal Is Constituted

Source: As reported in legal commentary and case-law digests covering Delhi High Court arbitration matters through early 2026. This article has been independently researched and written by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes, summarising the general legal position rather than reproducing any single report. Readers are encouraged to verify the current position against the underlying order and primary sources directly.

Section 9 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 permits a party to approach a court for interim measures of protection before, during, or after arbitral proceedings but before enforcement of the award. A recurring procedural question is what happens to a Section 9 application already pending before a court once the arbitral tribunal itself is constituted and is, in principle, available to grant equivalent relief under Section 17.

The Delhi High Court has reaffirmed the settled position that a pending Section 9 application does not automatically become infructuous the moment a tribunal is constituted. Section 9(3) directs a court not to entertain a fresh application once the tribunal has been constituted, unless the court finds that circumstances exist which may not render the remedy under Section 17 efficacious — but this bar operates prospectively on new applications, and does not compel a court to abandon an application that was validly pending before it, particularly where substantial hearing has already taken place or where the relief sought could not be as effectively granted by the newly constituted tribunal.

The Court reiterated that the efficacy proviso under Section 9(3) requires a case-specific assessment — a blanket rule that all interim relief automatically shifts to the tribunal the moment it is constituted would ignore situations where urgency, the stage of the court proceeding, or the nature of the relief sought make the tribunal a less practical forum for immediate protection.

The ruling is consistent with the broader legislative intent behind the 2015 amendment to Section 9, which was designed to discourage parties from bypassing the tribunal for routine interim relief once one is in place, while preserving the court's residual jurisdiction in genuinely exceptional circumstances.

Why It Matters

Parties should not assume that constitution of the tribunal automatically extinguishes a pending court application for interim relief — but should equally not delay approaching the tribunal under Section 17 once it is available, since courts will scrutinise closely whether the tribunal route remains efficacious for any fresh relief sought.
