Supreme Court Declines to Reopen Forest Clearances for Madhya Pradesh Coal Mining Project Near Elephant Corridor

Source: Originally reported by Bar and Bench on 23 May 2026. This article has been independently researched and rewritten in full by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes — it is not a reproduction of the original report. Readers are encouraged to consult the original source and the underlying judgment or order directly.

The Supreme Court declined to entertain an appeal challenging the National Green Tribunal's rejection of a plea against environmental and forest clearances granted for an Adani-linked coal mining project in Madhya Pradesh's Singrauli region, part of which the petitioners argued falls within a recognised elephant corridor — leaving the NGT's clearance decision undisturbed.

The dispute sits at the increasingly contested intersection of India's continued reliance on coal for energy security and its parallel commitments to wildlife corridor protection and forest conservation — a tension that surfaces repeatedly in mining and infrastructure clearance litigation, particularly in resource-rich but ecologically sensitive states like Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, where major coal reserves and significant forest and wildlife habitat frequently overlap geographically.

The Singrauli region specifically has been a recurring flashpoint in this broader tension for years, given its dense concentration of coal mining and thermal power generation activity sitting alongside significant forest cover and wildlife movement corridors — making it something of a bellwether site for how Indian environmental clearance jurisprudence handles the coal-versus-conservation trade-off more generally. Petitioners in cases of this kind typically face a genuinely difficult evidentiary burden: demonstrating not merely that some environmental or wildlife-corridor impact exists, but that the clearance-granting process itself was legally deficient in a way that justifies judicial intervention, as opposed to representing a policy trade-off the NGT and clearance authorities were entitled to make within their statutory mandate.

By declining to entertain the appeal, the Supreme Court effectively leaves the NGT's assessment of the clearance's environmental adequacy as the final word in this instance, without adding fresh Supreme Court reasoning on how elephant corridor considerations should be weighed against coal mining clearances going forward. For mining project proponents and environmental litigants alike, the case underscores that the NGT — rather than appellate review by the Supreme Court — remains the practical forum where the substantive environmental clearance battles in projects of this kind are most likely to be conclusively fought and decided, making a well-prepared NGT case, rather than reliance on a subsequent Supreme Court appeal, the genuinely decisive stage for both project proponents seeking to defend a clearance and objectors seeking to challenge one.

Why It Matters

For contested mining and infrastructure clearances involving wildlife corridors or forest land, the NGT remains the forum where the substantive battle is most likely to be decided — Supreme Court appellate intervention on the underlying environmental assessment is not something to count on.
