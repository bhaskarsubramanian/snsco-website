Delhi HC Makes Amitabh Bachchan's 2022 Personality Rights Injunction a Final Decree

Source: Originally reported by Bar and Bench, freepressjournal.in and LiveLaw Biz. This article has been independently researched and rewritten in full by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes: it is not a reproduction of the original reports. Readers are encouraged to consult the original sources and the underlying judgement directly.

An interim order the Delhi High Court passed in 2022 to stop people from cashing in on Amitabh Bachchan's name and image has now become permanent. Justice Purushaindra Kumar Kaurav decreed the suit, CS(COMM) 819/2022, on 5 November 2026, converting an ex parte injunction four years in the making into a final decree that restrains misuse of the actor's persona for good.

The suit, filed against Rajat Nagi and several other defendants, arose out of the kind of commercial exploitation that has become routine wherever a celebrity's name carries market value on its own. One defendant, Rajat Nagi, had developed a mobile application called 'Amitabh Bachchan Video Call'. Others named in the original proceedings included Manoj Publications, Tamatina and Mahesh Patel. The original interim order, passed on an ex parte basis on 25 November 2022, had already restrained the use of Bachchan's name, voice and image, including in connection with fake lottery schemes that traded on his public standing.

By the time the matter came back before the Court for final disposal, several of the named defendants had reached a settlement with Bachchan, and the remaining questions of fact had narrowed to the point that the suit could be decided without a full trial. The Court's IPR Division Rules permit exactly this kind of summary disposal where no genuinely disputed question of fact remains, and Justice Kaurav used that route to convert the 2022 interim protection directly into a final decree rather than letting the suit run through years of further litigation.

What makes the decree notable beyond its result is its reach. It does not stop at the named defendants. The Court extended the restraint to unidentified infringers, the 'John Does' whose identities were not known at the time the suit was filed, recognising that the misuse of Bachchan's persona through websites, mobile applications, posters and messaging platforms has continued in a scattered, hard-to-pin-down pattern rather than through any single identifiable actor. A decree that bound only the named defendants would leave the next anonymous infringer free to start the same cycle again.

With the decree in place and all pending applications disposed of, the Delhi High Court's registry has been directed to draw up the formal decree sheet without further delay. For practical purposes, the protection Bachchan has enjoyed on an interim basis since 2022 now has the finality of a decree behind it, closing a suit that has run for four years since it first drew attention as one of the more expansive personality rights orders an Indian court had issued.

The decree adds to a line of Delhi High Court rulings, running through several Bachchan family suits in recent years, that treat personality rights as enforceable against the full range of ways a celebrity's name, voice, image and likeness get commercially exploited online, not merely against a single infringing product or publication. For public figures facing the same scattered pattern of unauthorised use, the ruling is a reminder that an omnibus decree extending to unidentified future infringers is available once the facts are no longer seriously disputed, sparing the plaintiff from having to sue each new infringer from scratch.

That underlying mischief the suit was aimed at is a familiar one in India's digital economy. A recognisable name and face draw clicks, downloads and sign-ups on their own, which is exactly why mobile applications, quiz platforms, fake contests and lottery scams have repeatedly borrowed Bachchan's identity without any authorisation, often with no commercial relationship to him at all beyond the unauthorised use of his name. Civil remedies against each such app or website individually would always lag behind the rate at which new ones appear, since a decree against one operator does nothing to stop the next one from launching a near-identical product under a different name or domain the following week.

A decree that also binds unidentified persons changes that calculation for anyone tempted to exploit a celebrity's persona in the future. Once a court has recorded that any unauthorised commercial use of a particular individual's name, image or voice is restrained, including by persons not yet identified, a new infringer discovered later can be proceeded against for violating an existing decree rather than forcing the plaintiff to start a fresh suit from the beginning each time. That procedural shortcut, more than the restraint on the named defendants who had already settled, is what gives this decree its practical weight going forward.

The decree does not create a free-standing statutory right of publicity; India has no dedicated personality rights statute, and courts have built this body of protection case by case out of existing principles in privacy law, passing off and unfair competition. Each new ruling of this kind, including this one, adds detail to what has so far been judge-made doctrine rather than codified law, which is part of why practitioners continue to study each fresh Bachchan family order closely for how the Delhi High Court is shaping the contours of a right that Parliament has not yet defined in statute.

Why It Matters

The decree shows how the Delhi High Court's summary procedure under its IPR Division Rules can convert a years-old interim injunction into permanent relief once the remaining defendants settle and no disputed facts survive. For public figures and brand owners fighting scattered, anonymous misuse of their identity or marks online, the John Doe-style final decree offers a template for durable protection without having to litigate against each new infringer separately.
