Third-Party Funding of Commercial Litigation Is Not Per Se Unlawful in India: High Court Clarifies Position

Source: Originally reported by LiveLaw and Bar and Bench on and around 8 August 2026, covering a ruling clarifying the legal validity of a third-party funding arrangement disclosed in a pending commercial suit, in proceedings involving a litigation funder referred to as Prayosha Capital Partners. This article has been independently researched and rewritten in full by S&S Co. Advocates & Solicitors for informational purposes; it is not a reproduction of the original reports. Readers are encouraged to consult the original sources and the underlying judgment directly.

A High Court has held that an arrangement under which a third party funds a litigant's commercial suit in exchange for a share of any eventual recovery is not, by itself, unlawful or void under Indian law, clarifying a question that has generated uncertainty as third-party litigation funding has grown more common in high-value commercial disputes.

The issue arose when a defendant in a pending commercial suit objected to the plaintiff's litigation being financed by an outside funder, referred to in the proceedings as Prayosha Capital Partners, arguing that the arrangement amounted to impermissible champerty and maintenance and ought to result in dismissal of the suit or disqualification of the funding arrangement.

The Court held that unlike some common law jurisdictions, India has never treated champertous agreements as automatically void, and that a third-party funding arrangement. Provided it does not involve funding of a false or vexatious claim, does not give the funder improper control over the conduct of litigation such as decisions on settlement, and does not amount to funding by a party barred from doing so, such as counsel of record. Is not inherently objectionable.

The Court did flag the absence of a dedicated regulatory framework for litigation funding in India as an area warranting legislative attention, particularly around disclosure of funding arrangements to the court and safeguards against a funder exercising undue control over litigation strategy.

Why It Matters

Third-party funding is a legitimate, increasingly used tool for financing high-value commercial litigation in India, but funding arrangements should be structured carefully to avoid the funder exercising control over litigation strategy or settlement decisions, since those features are what could expose an arrangement to challenge.
