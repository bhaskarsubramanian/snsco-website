Wills, Estate Planning & Succession

Will drafting, probate, succession certificates, testamentary disputes and estate planning for individuals and family businesses across Noida, Delhi NCR and Kolkata.

Discuss Your Matter →
In Short

Estate planning and succession law governs how property passes on death — by a valid Will, or by the applicable succession law where there is none. S&S Co. advises on drafting robust Wills, obtaining probate and succession certificates, structuring testamentary trusts, and litigating disputes among heirs, drawing on a steady stream of 2026 Supreme Court guidance on testamentary freedom, coparcenary rights and the limits of a bank or company nominee's claim.

Our Services

  • Will drafting — clear, properly executed and attested Wills, including provisions for testamentary trusts for minor or dependent beneficiaries.
  • Probate & Letters of Administration — obtaining probate of a Will or Letters of Administration where there is no Will, and representing executors and administrators.
  • Succession certificates — applications under Section 372 of the Indian Succession Act to establish the right to collect a deceased person's debts and securities.
  • Contesting or defending a Will — representing beneficiaries or excluded heirs in disputes over a Will's validity, execution or suspicious circumstances.
  • Partition and coparcenary disputes — advising sons and daughters on coparcenary and inheritance rights under the Hindu Succession Act, and litigating partition suits.
  • Gift deed revocation — advising senior citizens on revoking a gift deed under the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007.
  • Family settlements — structuring and, where necessary, challenging family settlement deeds dividing an estate among heirs.
  • Succession planning for family businesses — structuring Wills, trusts and shareholding arrangements to pass on a family business with minimal disruption.

How We Work

  1. Initial Assessment

    We review the family's assets, any existing Will, and the applicable personal law to identify the right structure or, in a dispute, the strength of the claim.

  2. Drafting or Filing

    We draft a Will or trust, or file the appropriate probate, succession certificate or partition proceeding before the correct forum.

  3. Evidence & Proof

    Where a Will's validity is in issue, we lead or test the evidence — attesting witnesses, medical records, and the circumstances of execution.

  4. Hearing & Order

    We represent the client through hearings before the District Court, Family Court or High Court as the matter requires.

  5. Administration & Enforcement

    Once probate, a certificate or a decree is granted, we advise on estate administration, distribution, and enforcement against any resisting party.

Notable Judgments

Supreme Court — 2026 INSC 52121 May 2026
Parvathi Nairthi (Dead) Thr. LRs & Ors. v. Laxmi Nairthy (Dead) Thr. LRs & Ors.

Exclusion of Natural Heirs Alone Does Not Make a Will Suspicious

The Supreme Court upheld an unregistered Will bequeathing the testator's property to his sister instead of his wife and children, holding that a testamentary disposition exists precisely to interfere with the ordinary line of succession, so exclusion of natural heirs is not, by itself, a suspicious circumstance.

Key Takeaway — Testators can exclude even a spouse and children with real confidence a validly executed Will will be upheld, provided there is no genuine suspicious circumstance beyond the exclusion itself.
Supreme Court — 2026 INSC 49915 May 2026
B.S. Lalitha & Ors. v. Bhuvanesh & Ors.

Section 6(5) Hindu Succession Act Is a Saving Clause, Not a Bar to Filing a Partition Suit

The Supreme Court held that Section 6(5) of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 does not operate as a jurisdictional bar to a partition suit — whether an alleged prior partition is valid is a question of fact for trial, not a ground to reject the plaint at the threshold.

Key Takeaway — An heir's partition suit cannot be dismissed outright merely because the other side alleges an earlier partition — that claim must be proved at trial like any other disputed fact.
Supreme Court — 2026 INSC 3061 Apr 2026
Deepesh Maheswari v. Renu Maheswari & Ors.

Succession Certificate Obtained Without a Minor's Participation Set Aside

The Supreme Court quashed an ex parte succession certificate obtained without a minor legal heir being given a genuine opportunity to be heard, restoring the matter to the trial court for fresh disposal.

Key Takeaway — A succession certificate is vulnerable to being set aside years later if a minor heir wasn't properly represented when it issued.
Orissa High Court20 Jan 2026
Snigdha Patnaik v. Susama Mohanty

A Bank Nominee Is Not the Owner — the Widow's Succession Rights Prevail

The Orissa High Court held that nomination in a bank account does not amount to ownership; a nominee holds the funds in trust for the legal heirs, and a Hindu male's Class I heirs — including his widow — inherit ahead of any nominee.

Key Takeaway — Families should not assume a recorded nominee settles ownership of a deceased relative's funds — the actual legal heirs retain the superior claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a handwritten or unregistered Will valid in India?

Yes — a Will does not need to be registered to be valid under Indian law, provided it is signed by the testator and attested by at least two witnesses as required by Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925.

Can a Will be challenged just because it excludes a spouse or children?

No — the Supreme Court has confirmed that exclusion of natural heirs alone is not a suspicious circumstance; a challenge needs specific evidence of fraud, undue influence or a genuine irregularity in execution.

Do daughters have the same inheritance rights as sons?

Yes — since the 2005 Amendment to the Hindu Succession Act, daughters are coparceners by birth in the same manner as sons, and also retain independent Class I heir inheritance rights.

What happens if someone dies without a Will?

Their estate is distributed under the succession law applicable to them — the Hindu Succession Act, Muslim personal law, or the Indian Succession Act, depending on their religion or the law under which they married.
