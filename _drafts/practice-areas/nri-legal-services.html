NRI Legal Services

Property, succession, FEMA/repatriation and cross-border family matters for Non-Resident Indians and OCI cardholders, coordinated from Noida, Delhi NCR and Kolkata.

Discuss Your Matter →
In Short

NRIs and OCI cardholders face a distinct layer of Indian law on top of ordinary property, tax and family law — FEMA restrictions on what can be bought or repatriated, Black Money Act disclosure obligations, recognition of foreign divorce decrees, and inter-country adoption clearances. S&S Co. advises NRI clients on property transactions and fraud, succession of Indian assets, FEMA and tax compliance, and cross-border family matters, coordinated remotely wherever the client is based.

How We Work

    We prepare or review the Power of Attorney, property, tax or adoption documentation needed, coordinated remotely with the client abroad.

Illustrative Scenarios

The following are hypothetical, illustrative scenarios prepared for educational purposes. They are not descriptions of actual client matters, and no outcome is guaranteed in any specific case.

Illustrative Scenario

US-Based NRI Discovering an Attempted Impersonation Sale of Her Jalandhar Property

An NRI settled abroad for years learns that imposters attempted to pose as her and sell her property using a forged process. Prompt criminal complaint, an Economic Offences Wing investigation, and civil steps to protect title are all needed in parallel to secure the property and pursue the fraudsters.

Illustrative Scenario

Returning NRI With an Undisclosed Foreign Bank Account

An NRI moving back to India realises a small, rarely-used foreign bank account was never reported once they became a tax resident. Voluntary, complete disclosure going forward — and a review of whether any available disclosure scheme applies to prior years — is generally a far safer course than continued non-disclosure.

Notable Judgments

Punjab and Haryana High Court9 Mar 2026
Sarabjit Kaur v. State of Punjab

NRI Property Fraud Through Impersonation Is Treated Seriously at the Bail Stage

After imposters were produced to pose as the actual owners to sell an NRI's Jalandhar property, the Punjab and Haryana High Court refused anticipatory bail to the accused, reaffirming that a power of attorney alone does not transfer title to immovable property.

Key Takeaway — NRIs unable to personally supervise their India-based property remain a recurring fraud target, and courts are treating impersonation-based property fraud seriously even at the anticipatory bail stage.
ITAT, Delhi Bench5 Aug 2026
Ashok Shankar v. Assistant Commissioner of Income Tax

Black Money Act Penalty Upheld for Undisclosed Foreign Bank Account and Shares

The ITAT upheld a Black Money Act penalty where the taxpayer failed to disclose a foreign bank account and UAE shareholding, holding that low account activity does not excuse leaving a foreign asset undisclosed.

Key Takeaway — NRIs and returning residents should treat full Schedule FA disclosure of every foreign account and holding as non-negotiable, regardless of how dormant the asset appears.
Delhi High Court21 Apr 2026
Gur Kaur (Minor) & Ors. v. Union of India & Anr.

CARA Must Facilitate Hague Convention Compliance for Relocating an Adopted Child

The Delhi High Court held CARA is statutorily obligated to actively process Hague Convention documentation for a child already adopted under Hindu personal law who needs to relocate abroad, and cannot leave adoptive parents with only an informal support letter.

Key Takeaway — Families who adopted under personal law and now need to relocate a child abroad have High Court authority to compel CARA to properly process the Hague Convention NOC the destination country requires.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an NRI buy any property in India?

NRIs and OCIs can freely buy residential and commercial property, but are prohibited from purchasing agricultural land, plantation property or a farmhouse, except by inheritance or gift from a resident relative.

Is a Power of Attorney enough to sell my India property?

A Power of Attorney authorises someone to act on your behalf, but the Supreme Court has held it is not itself an instrument of transfer — an actual sale still requires a registered conveyance.

Will my foreign divorce be recognised in India?

Not automatically — recognition depends on the ground of divorce, whether it's recognised under the personal law applicable to the marriage, and whether the Indian spouse was properly served and participated in the foreign proceedings.

What happens if I forgot to disclose a foreign bank account?

Non-disclosure of a foreign asset can attract steep Black Money Act penalties even if related income was otherwise reported — the safest course is prompt, complete voluntary disclosure going forward.
