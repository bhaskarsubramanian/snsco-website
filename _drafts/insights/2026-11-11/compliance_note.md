# BCI Compliance Check — 2026-11-11

## What was confirmed

Ran fresh searches on the current state of Bar Council of India Rule 36 (BCI Rules, Part VI, Chapter II, Section V) and recent BCI/court activity on advocate advertising and solicitation.

- **Rule 36 text/status:** No source found confirms a formal textual amendment to Rule 36 in 2026. The last confirmed textual change remains the 2008 amendment, which lets an advocate publish only basic details (name, address, contact information, enrolment number) on a website, subject to the Rules' Schedule and BCI notification. The core prohibition — no soliciting work or advertising, directly or indirectly — stands unchanged.
  - [Lawyers, not vendors: Why Rule 36 of the BCI Rules still matters in digital India — Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/columns/lawyers-not-vendors-why-rule-36-still-matters-in-a-digital-india)
  - [PRIME LEGAL: Rule 36 and legal influencers, 2026 analysis](https://blog.primelegal.in/bci-rule-36-legal-influencers-2026-analysis/)
- **BCI circular, 17 July 2026:** The BCI issued a framework on advocates', law students' and interns' social media use, reminding advocates that social media content may amount to indirect advertising or solicitation prohibited under the Rules, citing Sections 7, 35 and 49 of the Advocates Act alongside Rule 36. It restricts promotional reels, client testimonials and legal-influencer content, and treats any self-disclosure beyond name, enrolment and broad practice area as going beyond transparency into advertising.
  - [SCC Online Blog: Promotional Reels, Client Testimonials, Legal Influencers — BCI Issues Digital Conduct Rules](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/07/18/bci-social-media-digital-ethics-guidelines/)
  - [TheQuint/ThePrint coverage: "No court reels, memes": Bar Council draws red line](https://theprint.in/judiciary/no-court-reels-memes-bar-council-draws-red-line-for-social-media-use-by-lawyers-law-students/2990445/)
- **Pending Supreme Court matter:** *Anil Pandey v. Bar Council of India*, W.P.(C) No. 817 of 2026 — a PIL seeking directions to the BCI to regulate advocates' social media use and frame a digital ethics code. The Court has issued notice; no final ruling found.
  - [LiveLaw: Supreme Court Seeks BCI Response On Plea To Regulate Advocates' Use Of Social Media](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/supreme-court-seeks-bci-response-on-plea-to-regulate-advocates-use-of-social-media-for-advertising-and-solicitation-541232)

## What could not be confirmed

No official, primary-source text of the July 2026 BCI circular (gazette or BCI website original) was located in this run — only secondary press/commentary coverage. No confirmation that Rule 36 itself has been amended, only that the BCI has issued conduct guidance around it. Applying the stricter reading: this run treats all of the above restrictions, plus the baseline Rule 36 bar, as binding, and goes further where secondary coverage is ambiguous.

## Applied to all drafts this run

Purely educational and informational content only. No solicitation in any manner — no call to contact, consult, hire or visit the firm; no "free consultation," offers, discounts or enquiry hooks; no testimonials, client stories, results, success rates or case-win claims; no comparisons or superlatives; no rankings, awards or guarantees; no phone/email/WhatsApp/address prompts inside content; no urgency/fear language aimed at attracting clients; no implied invitations such as "we can help." Authors, where named, use roles exactly as given in the site's roster with no self-promotion. A single neutral line noting the content is general information, not legal advice, is used where appropriate. Every draft was re-scanned on completion for solicitation phrasing; none was found.
