# BCI Rule 36 compliance check — 2026-11-10

## What was confirmed by fresh search today

- Rule 36 sits in Chapter II, Part VI of the Bar Council of India Rules, made under Section 49(1)(c) of the Advocates Act, 1961. Its substance, as reported by multiple secondary sources, bars an advocate from soliciting work or advertising, directly or indirectly, through circulars, advertisements, personal communication, interviews, or any other means, including social media and the internet. No official full verbatim text of the current rule was retrieved from a primary BCI source in this search; the operative line is widely quoted as "an advocate shall not solicit work or advertise, either directly or indirectly."
- No amendment to the text of Rule 36 itself was found as of this run. What has changed is enforcement activity and commentary, not the rule's wording.
- March 2025: the BCI publicly condemned advocates advertising through social media, promotional videos and influencer-style endorsements, and warned of suspension, cancellation of enrolment, contempt proceedings and complaints to digital platforms. (Bar and Bench, moneylife.in, SCC Online Blog)
- 17 July 2026: the BCI circulated a framework on use of social media by advocates, law students and interns, reiterating that social media may not be used for direct or indirect advertising, solicitation, client acquisition or commercial promotion, citing Sections 7, 35 and 49 of the Advocates Act along with Rule 36. (SCC Online Blog, India Legal Live)
- 14 July 2026: the Supreme Court issued notice to the BCI in a PIL, reported as *Anil Pandey and Anr. v. Bar Council of India*, W.P.(C) No. 817/2026, seeking enforcement against digital solicitation and "legal influencer" content and a declaration that such content is professional misconduct. This is a notice-stage matter, not a final ruling, and is pending. (LiveLaw, India Legal Live, Bar and Bench)
- Earlier precedent noted in commentary: the Madras High Court's 3 July 2024 ruling on the illegality of lawyer advertising, and the Supreme Court's 2018 *Mathew John / AK Balaji*-line reasoning that online legal-service platforms facilitating lawyer solicitation do not get IT Act Section 79 safe-harbour protection. These were not independently re-verified against primary sources today and are noted for context only.

## What could not be confirmed

- The exact current verbatim text of Rule 36 from an official BCI publication (bci.gov.in rules compilation). Not reachable/confirmed in this run.
- The final outcome of the pending Supreme Court PIL (W.P.(C) No. 817/2026) — it is at the notice stage only.
- Any formal amendment text to Rule 36 itself (none was found; only a circular interpreting/reiterating it).

Because the exact official text could not be pulled from a primary source, the stricter reading is applied to every draft in this run: no solicitation in any form, no advertising-style language, no client acquisition hooks, no testimonials or result claims, no self-praise or superlatives, no comparisons, no rankings/awards, no contact prompts, no urgency language, and no implied invitation to engage the firm. Authors (where named on an Article) use only the name and role exactly as given in the firm roster, with no further self-promotion. A single neutral "general information, not legal advice" line is used where appropriate. Every draft in this run was scanned a second time at the end for solicitation phrasing before being saved.
